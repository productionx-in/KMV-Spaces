# Phase 2 — Residential Market Evidence
## Pricing, product and demand across the Amaravati–Vijayawada–Guntur corridor

**Prepared:** August 2026
**Purpose:** Segment-level pricing evidence for a hypothetical 25-acre, ₹500 crore, four-community development
**Status:** Hypothetical strategic exercise. Nothing here describes an actual KMV Spaces project, land holding or launch.

---

## 0. Method

### 0.1 Why this is built bottom-up from projects, not top-down from averages

The brief says: *do not provide one generic "average price" unless the data genuinely supports it.* The data does not support it, and Section 1 shows exactly why — published locality averages for the same place, in the same period, disagree by **up to 40%**.

So this phase inverts the usual method. Instead of starting from a locality average and reasoning down, it starts from **named, RERA-registered projects with published unit sizes and ticket prices**, derives ₹/sq ft arithmetically from those two numbers, and builds segment bands from the resulting cloud of points.

That has three advantages:
1. **It is checkable.** Every rate below can be recomputed from a stated size and a stated price.
2. **It exposes internal contradictions.** Where a portal's stated ₹/sq ft disagrees with its own price÷size, that is recorded rather than smoothed.
3. **It produces bands, not points** — which is what a pricing decision actually needs.

### 0.2 Source hierarchy applied

| Tier | Source class | Used for | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | Government — AP Registration & Stamps (IGRS), guideline values | Transaction volume and value trends | **High** |
| **2** | RERA registration numbers, project size, unit counts | Project existence, scale, legal status | **High** |
| **3** | Established market reports — Magicbricks PropIndex | Demand/supply/price direction | **Medium-high**, but see §7.3 on currency |
| **4** | Developer's own project pages | Configurations, sizes, positioning | **Medium** — marketing material |
| **5** | Property portals (99acres, SquareYards, CommonFloor, Housing) | Ticket ranges and unit sizes | **Medium** — asking prices, and mutually inconsistent |
| **6** | Broker aggregators and SEO listing sites | Cross-check only | **Low** — used only to corroborate, never alone |

**Where a figure appears at Tier 5 or 6 with no corroboration, it is labelled.** Broker-published market commentary (toplinerealty, ghar.tv, 1acre, amaravathipropertyexperts) is treated as an interested party throughout, per Phase 1 §0.3.

### 0.3 The environment limitation, restated

As in Phase 1, direct retrieval of primary documents was not possible — outbound access was restricted to search. **The AP RERA project register was not directly queried.** RERA numbers below come from portal listings that cite them; they are strong evidence a project is registered, but the registration details were not verified against the authority's own database. §15 lists this and every other gap.

### 0.4 Derived rate convention

Throughout, **derived ₹/sq ft = stated ticket price ÷ stated unit size.** Where a project publishes a range at both ends, both ends are computed; when they agree, confidence is high and the rate is shown as a single figure. When they disagree, the spread is shown and the disagreement is noted.

## 1. The problem with any single "average price" for this corridor

Four independent sources, four different answers for the same market:

| Micro-market | Source A | Source B | Source C | Spread |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Vijayawada city** | ₹4,700/sq ft (99acres, Aug 2026) | ₹5,750/sq ft (ghar.tv, 2025) | ₹5,759/sq ft (Magicbricks PropIndex, Q3 2024) | **~22%** |
| **Mangalagiri** | ₹4,062/sq ft (SquareYards, Sep 2025) | ₹2,900/sq ft (99acres, 2BHK basket) | — | **~40%** |
| **Guntur** | ₹5,150/sq ft (Oct 2024, media) | ₹4,962/sq ft (Magicbricks, Q3 2024) | — | ~4% |
| **Amaravati** | ₹4,470/sq ft (Magicbricks, Q3 2024) | Plots quoted per sq yd, not sq ft | — | not comparable |

**These are not errors. They are different questions being answered.** A locality "average" depends entirely on the basket: all stock vs. new projects only; 2BHK vs. all configurations; asking vs. transacted; Mangalagiri classified as a Guntur-district town vs. as a Vijayawada suburb. The 40% Mangalagiri spread is almost certainly a small-unit resale basket (₹2,900) against a new-gated-project basket (₹4,062).

> **Working rule adopted for the rest of this document: never quote a locality average as a pricing input. Quote the project band for the relevant segment.**

### 2.1 Notes on the three internal contradictions found

These matter because they show how portal data behaves, and they are the reason §0.4's convention exists.

- **Manjeera Monarch.** One portal states ₹4,800/sq ft; the same project's stated ticket range (3BHK ₹91.22 L – ₹1.12 Cr over 1,567–1,954 sq ft) computes to ₹5,732–5,822/sq ft; a third source gives **₹5,100/sq ft asking**. The ₹4,800 figure is most likely a stale base rate excluding floor rise, car parking, corpus and amenity charges. **₹5,100 is used as the working figure**, being the only source that describes itself as a current asking rate.
- **Prime Hill Crest.** Ticket ÷ size gives ₹4,491–4,500 at both ends. The same page separately states "average ₹5 k/sq.ft." **The derived figure is preferred**, because it is reproducible.
- **Raintree Park Dwaraka Krishna II.** Listed 2BHK "from ₹63 L" sits *above* the listed 3BHK band (₹50.58–56.29 L) — arithmetically impossible for the same project. Treated as a portal data error; only the stated ~₹3,900/sq ft average is carried forward, with low confidence.

**The general lesson: a stated ₹/sq ft on a portal is frequently a base rate. The ticket price is what a buyer pays.** All-in realisation typically runs 8–15% above base rate once floor rise, parking, club membership, corpus fund and GST are added. Any pricing model built on portal base rates will understate achievable revenue.

### 4.1 Four things the ladder makes visible

**1. The 2BHK and mid-3BHK bands overlap almost completely** (₹3,800–4,800 vs ₹3,800–4,600). In this corridor, moving from 2BHK to 3BHK buys **more area at roughly the same rate**, not a better rate. That is a market where **configuration is not a pricing lever — size and address are.**

**2. The step from "mid gated" to "premium gated" is roughly ₹700–800/sq ft** (≈18%). The step from premium apartments to luxury villas is roughly **₹2,000/sq ft** (≈35%). **The villa premium is where the pricing power sits**, and it is far larger than any apartment-tier premium available.

**3. The corridor's price ceiling for apartments is around ₹6,300–6,500/sq ft**, set by Vertex Siris Signa in Vijayawada city — a 3-acre, 184-unit, ready scheme. Nothing found in Mangalagiri or Tadepalli reaches it. **The premium apartment ceiling is currently a city-centre phenomenon, not a corridor one.** Whether that ceiling can be imported into the corridor is a genuine open question — and it is the central pricing risk for the hypothetical project's premium apartment communities.

**4. Villas break the apartment ceiling by a wide margin.** KMV Vivaan achieved ₹8,200/sq ft — **30% above the best apartment rate found anywhere in the region.** Villas are the only product type in this evidence base that reliably clears ₹7,000/sq ft.

### 5.1 The finding that reframes the product story

**A 25-acre, ~475-unit scheme would not be the corridor's biggest project by unit count.** Aparna already delivers 612 units and Raintree 632. **It would be the corridor's biggest by land, by roughly 2.5×** — and, far more importantly, **the least dense mixed project in the corridor by a factor of three to five.**

| Comparison | Density |
| --- | --- |
| Corridor apartment schemes | **53 – 113 units/acre** |
| Corridor villa schemes | **6.6 – 10.5 units/acre** |
| **The hypothetical project** | **≈16 – 22 units/acre** |

The brief's implied density sits in an **unoccupied band between the two existing product formats** — and is internally consistent with the 50:50 to 60:40 apartment/villa land split that Phase 1 §6.4 derived independently from the ₹500 crore GDV. **Two separate calculations converge on the same product shape**, which is a meaningful consistency check.

**Commercially, this is the differentiation argument stated in a number.** A buyer comparing a ₹1.5 crore 3BHK here against Manjeera Monarch is choosing between roughly 20 homes per acre and 113. That is a difference visible from the site gate — open space, distance between blocks, parking, tree cover, noise. It does not require a brochure to explain, and it is the most defensible basis found for the price premium Phase 1 identified as the project's central problem.

## 7. Demand, supply and price trends

### 7.1 Government transaction data — the best available demand proxy

| Metric | Figure | Source tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| AP Registration & Stamps revenue, **FY2025–26** | **₹9,331 crore, +27% YoY** | **Tier 1 — government** |
| Against annual target | ₹11,221 crore — **84.45% achieved** | Tier 1 |
| Property registrations handled annually (IGRS) | **500,000+**, growing ~15% p.a. since 2020 | Tier 1 |
| Guideline (circle) values | **Revised upward statewide, effective 1 Feb 2026**, explicitly to close the gap with actual transaction prices | Tier 1 |
| Transaction cost | 5% stamp + 1% registration + 1.5% transfer = **7.5%** | Tier 1 |

**How to read the +27%, honestly.** This is the strongest demand signal available for the state — and it cannot be taken at face value, for a specific reason:

> **The guideline-value revision took effect on 1 February 2026, inside the same financial year.** Registration revenue is charged on the *higher of* consideration or guideline value. So a meaningful part of the +27% is **the same number of transactions being taxed on higher recorded values**, not more transactions.

Volume growth and value growth cannot be separated from the published figure. And the department landed at **84.45% of its own target** — the state itself expected more. The defensible reading is: **transaction activity in AP is genuinely growing, at a rate somewhere below 27%, and the state's own forecast was not met.**

### 7.2 Magicbricks PropIndex — the only established market report covering these cities

The corridor is not in the 13-city panel that Magicbricks reports quarterly. It was, however, covered in a dedicated Q3 2024 (Jul–Sep) analysis:

| City | Demand (searches) YoY | Supply (listings) YoY | Average price | Price YoY |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Amaravati** | **+49.10%** | **−6.70%** | ₹4,470 /sq ft | +3.29% |
| **Guntur** | **+38.27%** | **−22.57%** | ₹4,962 /sq ft | +3.27% |
| **Vijayawada** | **+31.95%** | **−18.46%** | ₹5,759 /sq ft | **+20.06%** |

**Two cautions, both material.**

**First, "demand" here means search activity on a portal, not transactions.** A 49.10% rise in people searching Amaravati listings after the capital was revived is entirely believable and tells you about *attention*. It does not tell you anyone bought anything. Marketing material in this corridor routinely quotes the 49% figure as "residential demand growth" without that qualifier. It should not be used that way.

**Second, this data is from Q3 2024 — nearly two years stale.** It predates the May 2025 relaunch, the April 2026 gazette notification, and the February 2026 guideline revision. **There is no equivalent 2026 read for this corridor**, because these cities are not in the ongoing PropIndex panel. That is the single largest evidence gap in Phase 2 (§15).

What the Q3 2024 data does establish is a **structural scissors** — demand rising 32–49% while listings fell 7–23% — which is the mechanism behind Vijayawada's +20% price move. Whether that scissors is still open in 2026 is unknown.

## 8. Absorption and inventory

**No corridor-level absorption or unsold-inventory dataset is publicly available.** AP RERA publishes project registrations but quarterly sales disclosures were not retrievable in this environment, and the corridor is outside every agency panel (Knight Frank, ANAROCK, PropEquity, Magicbricks PropIndex) that publishes absorption metrics.

What can be inferred, and how strongly:

| Signal | Observation | Inference | Strength |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Long-completed projects still actively marketed** | Aparna Amaravati One was expected ready **Aug 2022**; it is still listed, ready-to-move, with a published price range in 2026 | Roughly **four years** of post-completion selling on a 612-unit scheme | **Moderate** — listings can persist after sell-out |
| **Dedicated resale listing pages** | Jayabheri The Capital and Samruddhi Nexa both carry "Resale Price List" pages | Completed premium stock is turning over — **an exit market exists** | Moderate |
| **Explicit price flexibility** | Sri Hari Kousthubham quotes ₹4,500/sq ft **"negotiable"** | Published rates in the premium-3BHK band are **not firm**; discounting is expected | **Strong** — a developer's own quote |
| **Supply contraction (Q3 2024)** | Listings down 7–23% YoY across the three cities | Either genuine absorption, or sellers withdrawing to wait for higher prices | **Weak** — the two are indistinguishable in listing data |
| **National premium build-up** | ₹2–5 crore unsold inventory **+43% YoY** (Knight Frank H1 2026) | The segment this project depends on is accumulating nationally | Strong nationally, **unverified locally** |

> **Honest conclusion: absorption in this corridor is unmeasured, and the two strongest available signals point in opposite directions.** Supply contraction and rising search demand suggest a tight market. A four-year-old ready project still selling, negotiable pricing in the premium band, and national premium inventory build-up suggest a slow one. Phase 3 pricing should be modelled against the slow case and treated as upside if the tight case proves out.

## 9. Rental potential

| Locality | Gross rental yield |
| --- | --- |
| Kanuru | **3.2%** |
| Gannavaram | 3.1% |
| Prasadampadu | 2.9% |
| Currency Nagar | 2.4% |
| **Tadepalli** | **2.2%** |
| Premium localities, average | ~3.0% |

**Observed rents** (individual listings, wide dispersion): 2BHK Mangalagiri ₹19,500/month; 2BHK Poranki ₹10,000; 3BHK Suryaraopet ₹35,000 + ₹2,500 maintenance; a 3BHK at ₹12,000.

### 9.1 The yield inversion, and what it means

> **Tadepalli — the corridor's highest-priced micro-market — has its lowest rental yield.** Kanuru, at a materially lower capital value, yields 3.2% against Tadepalli's 2.2%.

That inversion is the classic signature of **capital values running ahead of rental fundamentals.** Prices in Tadepalli have risen (+34.2% over three years) faster than rents have followed.

**The commercial consequence is direct and should be stated plainly: rental income is not a sellable argument for premium product in this corridor.**

Sense-check the arithmetic. A ₹1.5 crore 3BHK at a 2.2% gross yield generates roughly **₹27,500/month before maintenance, vacancy and management** — consistent with the observed ₹12,000–35,000 rent band. Net of costs, that is a **~1.5–2.0% net yield.** Against home loan rates of 7.15–7.35% (Phase 1 §6.2), **a leveraged buy-to-let in this corridor is materially cash-flow negative.**

Two implications carry into Phase 3:
1. **Investor messaging built on rental yield will not survive a spreadsheet.** The investor case here is appreciation, and appreciation is the argument Phase 1 §7.1 established this product should not be leading with.
2. **The 2BHK community is the only segment where rental economics are even arguable** — smaller ticket, proportionally higher yield, and a real tenant pool from AIIMS, SRM, NRI Medical College, KIMS and government offices. If any part of this project is sold on rental logic, it is that one.

## 10. Resale considerations

**Evidence that a resale market functions:**
- Jayabheri The Capital (370 units, ready) and Samruddhi Nexa (350 units, completed) both have **dedicated resale listing pages** on major portals.
- KMV Vivaan has an active 2,251 sq ft 3BHK resale listing while the project's own entry price is quoted from ₹1.3 crore.
- Three-year locality appreciation of **+34% to +53%** means early buyers in Tadepalli, Poranki and Penamaluru are substantially in the money.

**Factors that will shape resale for a product like this:**

| Factor | Direction | Reasoning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Scarcity of the format** | **Favourable** | If no comparable low-density 25-acre community exists (§5), resale units have no direct substitute — the strongest structural support for resale value |
| **Villa segment depth** | **Favourable** | Villas outperform apartments ~3:1 on appreciation (+8.39% vs +2.99% in Tadepalli), and villa supply is capped at ~70 units per scheme corridor-wide |
| **Competition from plots** | **Adverse** | Capital-area plots at ₹23,000–70,000/sq yd compete for the same resale rupee, and offer better pure-appreciation math (Phase 1 §7.1) |
| **Premium inventory overhang** | **Adverse** | National ₹2–5 crore unsold inventory **+43% YoY**; a resale seller competes with developer inventory that can offer payment plans and freebies |
| **7.5% transaction cost** | **Adverse** | Stamp duty, registration and transfer duty mean a resale buyer needs ~7.5% appreciation just to break even on entry costs |
| **Guideline-value revision** | **Mixed** | Raises recorded values (supports valuations and loan eligibility) but raises buyer cost |
| **Post-2028 capital completion** | **Unknown** | If the capital's Phase I completes on target (Aug 2028), the corridor's resale story strengthens materially — and that lands roughly when a project launching now would be delivering |

**The most useful resale observation for phasing:** a project launching in 2026 and delivering in phases through 2029–30 would see its **first resale cycle coincide with the capital's own Phase I completion.** That is a favourable alignment, and it is an argument for structuring early phases to attract buyers who will still be holding in 2029 — rather than short-horizon investors who will be trying to exit into the same window.

## 11. What Phase 2 changes about Phase 1

Three material refinements. The first is significant enough to change the project's risk profile.

### 11.1 The premium gap is roughly a third of what Phase 1 estimated

Phase 1 §6.4 established that ₹500 crore on 25 acres requires a blended **~₹5,900–6,000/sq ft**, and benchmarked that against the **Mangalagiri locality average of ₹4,062** — a required premium of about **45%**.

**That benchmark was the wrong comparator, and §1 explains why.** A locality average blends resale stock, standalone buildings and small units with new gated product. The correct comparison is against **what comparable product actually achieves**:

| Benchmark | Rate | Required blended vs benchmark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mangalagiri locality average | ₹4,062 | **+46%** ← *Phase 1's comparator* |
| Prime Hill Crest, premium 3BHK, Mangalagiri | ₹4,500 | +32% |
| Manjeera Monarch asking, Mangalagiri | ₹5,100 | **+16%** |
| Prime Grandeur villas, Mangalagiri | ₹5,400 | **+10%** |
| Tadepalli villa asking average | ₹5,901 | **+1%** |
| Vertex Siris Signa, Vijayawada | ₹6,300 | **−6%** |
| TAG AIRA villas | ₹7,309 | −18% |
| KMV Vivaan villas | ₹8,200 | −27% |

> **Corrected finding: the ₹500 crore target requires a blended rate roughly 10–16% above the best comparable product in the same micro-market — and below what premium apartments and villas already achieve elsewhere in the region.**
>
> That is an ordinary, achievable positioning task. It is not the 45% stretch Phase 1 described.

Phase 1's risk 9.2 — "the premium-pricing gap", rated High impact / High probability — should be **downgraded to High impact / Medium probability**, conditional on the project delivering a product that genuinely justifies sitting alongside Vertex and above Manjeera.

### 11.2 The differentiation argument is now quantified

Phase 1 argued the project's scale (25 acres vs. competitors' 5–7) was a structural differentiator. §5 sharpens this: the real differentiator is not acreage, it is **density — 16–22 units/acre against a corridor apartment norm of 53–113.** That is a three-to-five-fold difference, visible on site, and it is the concrete thing the price premium is *for*.

### 11.3 Unit count and sales rate revise down

Phase 1 estimated ~490–700 units and **10–12 bookings/month**. Applying the observed size distribution in §6 to 8.33 lakh sq ft of saleable area gives **≈400–550 units** — because the corridor's modal 3BHK (1,600–2,050 sq ft) and villa (2,800–3,600 sq ft) are larger than Phase 1 assumed.

**Revised sales task: 400–550 units over 4–5 years ≈ 80–140 units a year ≈ 7–11 confirmed bookings a month.** Marketing envelope is unchanged at ₹20–25 crore (4–5% of GDV), so **allowable cost per unit sold rises to roughly ₹3.6–6.2 lakh.**

## 12. Indicative price architecture

Not a pricing recommendation — Phase 3 does that with cost data this phase does not have. This is a **feasibility test**: does a four-community structure priced against the §4 evidence actually reach ₹500 crore?

Assuming 8.33 lakh sq ft saleable on 25 acres (Phase 1 §6.4):

| Community | Product | Size band | Area share | Saleable | **₹/sq ft** | Revenue | **Ticket band** | Benchmarked against |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | 2BHK + compact 3BHK | 1,150–1,400 sq ft | 15% | 1.25 L sq ft | **₹5,000** | ₹62.5 Cr | **₹58 – 70 L** | Manjeera ₹5,100 · Amaravati Icon ₹5,150 |
| **B** | 3BHK premium | 1,650–1,950 sq ft | 30% | 2.50 L sq ft | **₹5,400** | ₹135 Cr | **₹89 L – 1.05 Cr** | Manjeera ₹5,100 · Prime Grandeur ₹5,400 |
| **C** | 3.5 / 4BHK premium apartments | 2,300–2,800 sq ft | 25% | 2.08 L sq ft | **₹6,250** | ₹130 Cr | **₹1.44 – 1.75 Cr** | Vertex ₹6,300 |
| **D** | Villas | 2,800–3,600 sq ft | 30% | 2.50 L sq ft | **₹7,150** | ₹178.8 Cr | **₹2.00 – 2.57 Cr** | TAG AIRA ₹7,309 · below KMV Vivaan ₹8,200 |
| | | | **100%** | **8.33 L sq ft** | **₹6,078 blended** | **₹506 Cr** | ~400–450 units | |

**Three observations.**

**1. It closes.** ₹506 crore against a ₹500 crore target, with every rate anchored to a named comparable and none of them at the top of its band. Community D at ₹7,150 sits **below** TAG AIRA and **13% below** KMV Vivaan. Community C at ₹6,250 sits **below** Vertex Siris Signa. **No community in this structure needs to set a corridor record.**

**2. Community D carries 35% of revenue on 30% of area.** The villa component is the project's economic engine, consistent with Phase 1 §6.3. It is also the component with the thinnest competitive set (§3.1) and the deepest evidence of pricing power (§4.1).

**3. Community D lands squarely in the ₹2.2–2.8 crore gap identified in §3.1** — between the mid-premium villa cluster that tops out around ₹2.2 crore and the luxury cluster that starts at ₹2.79 crore. **That is either the project's best opportunity or its biggest pricing risk, and the evidence base cannot currently distinguish between the two.** Resolving it is the highest-value item in §15.

## 14. What carries into Phase 3

**Established as usable pricing inputs:**

1. **Corridor segment bands** (§4) — eight segments, each anchored to named RERA-registered projects with derived rates.
2. **The premium required is ~10–16% over the best local comparable, not 45%** over a locality average (§11.1).
3. **Density is the differentiator, and it is quantified** — 16–22 units/acre against a corridor norm of 53–113 (§5).
4. **Villas carry the economics** — they are the only product type clearing ₹7,000+/sq ft, they appreciate ~3× faster than apartments, and no corridor villa community exceeds ~70 units (§3.1).
5. **A four-community structure priced against real comparables reaches ₹506 crore** with no community needing to set a record (§12).
6. **Portal base rates understate realisation by 8–15%** once floor rise, parking, club, corpus and GST are added (§2.1).

**Established as constraints:**

1. **Rental yield does not support the premium** — 2.2% in Tadepalli, ~3% at best, against 7.15–7.35% loan rates. Do not sell on rental income except, arguably, in Community A (§9.1).
2. **Absorption is unmeasured, and the available signals conflict.** Model against the slow case (§8).
3. **The ₹2.2–2.8 crore villa band is empty** — opportunity or buyer boundary, currently indistinguishable (§12).
4. **The apartment ceiling of ₹6,300/sq ft is a Vijayawada city phenomenon.** Importing it into the corridor is the central pricing assumption of Community C, and it is unproven.
5. **The corridor's best appreciation is east of Vijayawada, not toward the capital** (§7.4). The capital-adjacency price argument is not yet supported by comparative appreciation data.

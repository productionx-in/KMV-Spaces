§0

Two problems with the model, before it is built

A journey map that hides these produces a dashboard nobody can act on.

Problem 1 · it is a planning device, not a measurement system

Of the twelve stages, four are hard gates — a system records an unambiguous event. The other eight are inferred from proxies.

That matters commercially: a stage that cannot be counted cannot justify budget on its own, and treating a proxy as a count is how marketing dashboards fill with numbers that move without meaning anything.

Problem 2 · the linear journey describes about half the buyers

S1 trade families — "referral first… digital is a verification channel, not a discovery channel." They enter at Consultation.

S3 diaspora — 6–18 months of consideration, then a five-day decision window. Stages stretch, then compress.

S4 landowners — "responds to a document, not to advertising." They never enter the media funnel at all.

Why that second problem is expensive

Building one journey and running everyone through it would misallocate the budget toward the segments that are cheapest to reach and least valuable per unit.

Which is precisely the failure Phase 7 warned about when it found a Community D booking is worth 3.38 Community A bookings.

One change to the Phase 7 budget

Phase 7 modelled 19% of bookings as referral-sourced — and contained no line for advocacy at all.

Referral is 6.2× cheaper per booking than marketing-sourced demand. Funding the cheapest channel at zero was the single clearest gap in the model.

The layer boundary that is usually drawn wrong

Advocacy is conventionally drawn as the last layer — what happens after the sale. In this project it is also the first.

Phase 5 found S1's opening objection is "who else has bought?" Phase 6 concluded Community D's first tranche should be seeded rather than advertised. Phase 7 found the segment that cannot be bought with advertising delivers the cheapest revenue.

The funnel is a loop. Drawn as a straight line ending in advocacy, the model places the cheapest and most persuasive asset in the project at the point furthest from the launch — where it can do nothing.

The operational consequence: the advocacy layer starts working before the first booking. Its year-one job is not referral volume — it is manufacturing the first fifteen visible, named, local buyers whose existence answers S1's first question.

Brand building

Accountable for the price premium — whether ₹6,500/sq ft is credible before it is quoted

₹6.30 Cr · 24.5%
1

Awareness

Brand buildingEstimated
Objective
Establish that a 25-acre, low-density development exists on this corridor, and that its developer built AIIMS Mangalagiri. Make the price premium thinkable before it is ever quoted.
Audience
The full Phase 5 universe, weighted to S1, S5 and S6 in the Vijayawada–Guntur catchment; S7 in Hyderabad; S3 in US metros with coastal-Andhra origin.
Message
Not “luxury homes in Amaravati” — every competitor says that. The two things none of them can say: the developer that built AIIMS Mangalagiri is building 25 acres beside it, and fifteen homes per acre where the corridor builds fifty-three to a hundred and thirteen. Delivery and density, not aspiration.
Channel
NH-16 and Vijayawada–Guntur corridor hoardings; Mangalagiri bypass; Telugu press (Eenadu, Sakshi, Andhra Jyothi); Telugu YouTube and regional OTT; Meta and Google video for reach; Hyderabad OOH for S7; diaspora digital for S3.
Content / creative
A 60-second film shot on the actual corridor. An aerial holding 25 acres against the 5-to-11-acre neighbours — the density difference is a visual fact, not a claim. The AIIMS credential as a verifiable title card.
CTA
None — deliberately. An awareness asset carrying a lead form is a demand-generation asset with its reach wasted. The next stage exists to catch the response.
KPI
Prompted and unprompted brand recall in the catchment; share of category search against the eleven Phase 3 competitors.
Measurement
Quarterly recall survey, n ≈ 400, split Vijayawada / Guntur / Mangalagiri. Search-volume share on a fixed competitor set. Not clicks, and not reach.
2

Discovery

Brand buildingEstimated
Objective
When someone goes looking — because they saw a hoarding, a peer mentioned it, or they searched “villas in Mangalagiri” — they find something authoritative. Phase 4 found KMV's search visibility thin. This stage is where that costs money.
Audience
Anyone actively looking. Highest value: S2, who Phase 5 found “will check RERA — most of the catchment will not”; S3, researching at night from a different time zone; S7.
Message
Proof, in this order: RERA number · land parcel · density · developer record · possession date. Specificity is the message.
Channel
Organic search; Google Business Profile; the project website; the RERA listing; portal listings (99acres, MagicBricks, Housing) treated as presence, not as a lead source; YouTube; Telugu-language search.
Content / creative
A project site that answers the questions Phase 5's segments actually ask — including the ones competitors avoid: price per square foot, a unit-wise price sheet, the possession date, the RERA number, and the AIIMS credential with evidence attached.
CTA
“See the price sheet.” “Check the RERA registration.” Low friction and not lead-gated — gating price is what makes a buyer leave for a competitor who publishes it.
KPI
Branded search volume; organic visibility on a fixed set of ~20 corridor commercial queries; Google Business Profile views and direction requests.
Measurement
Search Console; GBP Insights; rank tracking on the fixed query set, baselined against the Phase 4 audit so the improvement is provable rather than asserted.

₹9.84 Cr · 38.3%
3

Interest

Demand generationEstimated
Objective
Convert passive attention into a self-identified product interest — which of the four communities, which ticket band. Routing starts here, not at the lead form.
Audience
Segmented four ways from the first impression, because Phase 6 established the four communities share almost no buyer.
Message
Four messages, never one. A — the lowest-density apartment in the corridor, from ₹59 lakh. B — own it from anywhere; leave it without worrying. C — a villa inside a masterplan, not a villa on a plot. D — estate-scale living below the price the luxury tier starts at.
Channel
Meta and Google demand generation structured by community; YouTube; Telugu digital for S8; LinkedIn, diaspora associations and APNRTS in Guntur for S3 and S7.
Content / creative
Community-specific and comparative: a walkthrough film per community; floor plates; the density comparison graphic; a commute-time map to AIIMS, the Secretariat and Benz Circle — Phase 5 found S2 wants the drive measured, not described.
CTA
“Download the [community] brochure.” “See the floor plans.” A content exchange, not a hard enquiry.
KPI
Engaged sessions per community; video 50%-completion rate; brochure downloads; cost per engaged session by community.
Measurement
GA4 with community as a content dimension; platform view-through; UTM discipline enforced by community — without it, nothing downstream can be attributed to the right product.
4

Consideration

Demand generationEstimated
Objective
Get the buyer to compare deliberately — and to compare on the dimensions where this project wins (density, masterplan, delivery record) rather than the one where it does not (₹/sq ft against Prime Grandeur).
Audience
Active shoppers. Phase 5: S6 compares three to five projects across two or three weekends; S2 builds a spreadsheet; S3 researches for months.
Message
Set the comparison frame before the competitor does. “Twenty homes an acre, or a hundred and thirteen. That is the choice.” For Community C, the +20% over Prime Grandeur is explained in land, neighbours and delivery record — not defended.
Channel
Website comparison content; retargeting across Meta, Google and YouTube; email and WhatsApp nurture; third-party proof — RERA, the AIIMS record, construction progress.
Content / creative
A density comparison tool. An EMI and eligibility calculator — Phase 5 found S5 responds directly to these. A “what ₹1.8 crore actually buys, here and elsewhere” piece. Monthly construction-progress updates.
CTA
“Book a call with the project lead.” “Get the full price sheet.”
KPI
Return-visit rate; days from first to second visit; calculator completions; retargeting-assisted conversions.
Measurement
GA4 return-visitor cohorts; CRM lag from first touch to enquiry. This stage's value shows up as a shorter consideration cycle, not as a conversion count.

Lead

Demand generationCounted

78,880 total · 1,753/month · 67 per working day

Objective
Capture a contactable record carrying enough to route it to the right community and the right salesperson.
Audience
All eight segments — but they do not submit in the same way. Phase 5: S5 and S6 “will submit enquiry forms — one of the few segments that reliably will.” S1 “is unlikely to fill a portal form; likely to call a number seen on a hoarding.” S3 has “very low form-fill rates.” S4 does not fill forms at all.
Message
Match the promise the channel made. Never gate at the form what was promised ungated in the ad.
Channel
Website forms; click-to-call; WhatsApp Business as a first-class capture path, not an afterthought; portal enquiries; walk-ins; channel-partner submissions; a missed-call number for the low-digital cohort.
Content / creative
A four-field form — name, phone, community of interest, timeline. Nothing longer. Every additional field costs leads from the segments that were reluctant to begin with.
CTA
“Talk to us.” “Request a callback.” Or simply the WhatsApp icon.
KPI
78,880 leads over 45 months — 1,753 a month, 67 a working day. Cost per lead by community: A ₹350 · B ₹1,600 · C ₹900 · D ₹1,900.
Measurement
The CRM as single source of truth. Every lead carries source, campaign and community — and the self-reported first-touch question is asked at booking, not here, for the reason in §4.

Qualified Lead

Demand generationCounted

12,474 total · 277/month · 15.8% of leads

Objective
Establish contact, budget, timeline and intent. Phase 7's model assumes only 15.8% survive this — 12,474 of 78,880.
Audience
All leads. The qualifying questions differ by community: A on down-payment readiness; B on residency and travel window; C and D on cash position and who else is in the decision.
Message
Helpful, not pushy. Phase 5 found S2 “highly averse to sales pressure” and S3's stated primary concern is fraud — a hard-sell first call confirms exactly the fear that stops the purchase.
Channel
Outbound call within 15 minutes of the enquiry; WhatsApp for S3 and S8; email for S2, who Phase 5 found “often cannot take calls during the day.”
Content / creative
A qualification script per community. For S3: a time-zone-aware scheduling link, not a call attempt at 11 a.m. IST.
CTA
“Let's find twenty minutes.”
KPI
Lead → qualified 15.8% (A 19.8% · B 13.5% · C 18.0% · D 15.3%); speed to first contact; contact rate; 277 qualified leads a month.
Measurement
CRM stage transitions with timestamps; sampled call-recording review. Community B's rate tracked separately and reported weekly, since the model assumes it runs 30% worse and B consumes 65% of the lead-generation budget.

Consultation

Sales conversionEstimated — promote to counted

5,313 total · 118/month · 42.6% of qualified

Objective
A structured conversation before the site visit that fixes need, budget and community — so the visit is a confirmation rather than a discovery.
Audience
Qualified leads. For S3 this is the primary sales interaction, because the physical visit may never happen before the decision.
Message
Diagnostic, not pitching — including recommending Community A to someone who enquired on D. Phase 6's architecture only works if buyers land in the community built for them; a mis-sold B unit to a resident family is a cancellation waiting to happen.
Channel
In person at the experience centre; video call for S3, S7 and S8; the family CA or advocate included for S1 — Phase 5 found they are gatekeepers “not currently addressed by any competitor.”
Content / creative
Price sheet, payment-plan options, EMI working, possession schedule, RERA documents. For S3: a FEMA, Power of Attorney and repatriation explainer — the fraud objection answered with process rather than reassurance.
CTA
“Come and walk it.” Or, for S3: “Let's schedule the live walkthrough.”
KPI
42.6% of qualified leads reach consultation. Consultation → visit 55%. Community-reassignment rate, which measures routing quality.
Measurement
CRM activity logging. Phase 7 does not model this as a separate gate — it sits inside the 23.4% qualified-to-visit conversion. It must be logged as an explicit sub-step or it stays invisible and unmanageable.

Site Visit

Sales conversionCounted

2,922 total · 65/month · 15 per week

Objective
Make the density visible. Phase 6: “a difference visible from the site gate.” Phase 7: visit-to-booking is the single most sensitive variable in the model — a two-point fall costs more than a 25% rise in media prices.
Audience
2,922 visits over 45 months — 65 a month, 15 a week. And they arrive completely differently: S1 unannounced, Sunday, 3–6 family members. S2 by appointment, couple only, weekday evening. S5 and S6 Sunday with children, comparing two or three projects the same day. S3 by proxy relative first, then one compressed trip. S7 Friday-to-Sunday combined with a family visit.
Message
The site does the talking. The sales role is to time the drive from AIIMS or the Secretariat on the way in, and to walk the central open space rather than describe it.
Channel
The experience centre and two show homes — Phase 7's ₹5.00 crore line. Plus a live video walkthrough capability for S3 and S8, staffed to their time zones.
Content / creative
A furnished show home in C or D; the masterplan model; the density comparison demonstrated on site, not on paper; visible construction progress; a Telugu-speaking host.
CTA
“Choose your plot.” “Block a unit.”
KPI
Visit → booking 6.9% blended — A 8.1% · B 6.0% · C 7.0% · D 5.6%. Repeat-visit rate; visits per booking; weekend capacity utilisation, since S1, S5 and S6 all visit on Sundays and the constraint is host availability, not demand.
Measurement
Attendance logged with party size and segment; 24-hour follow-up logged; visit-to-booking tracked by community and by salesperson. This is the number to instrument first.

Negotiation

Sales conversionEstimated — promote to counted

574 total · 13/month · 19.6% of visits

Objective
Close without breaking the two Phase 6 pricing rules: Community B never above ₹1.50 crore, Community C never below ₹1.65 crore. Breaking either collapses the ₹24 lakh gap that keeps them from competing.
Audience
Visited, engaged, not yet committed. The decision unit is rarely one person: S1's CA and eldest brother; S2's spouse, co-equal; S3's inspecting relative, who holds effective veto; S8's spouse in India, who will actually live there.
Message
Flexibility on structure, discipline on price. Phase 5 found S2 clears the ₹94,800 EMI but struggles with ₹52 lakh in cash — a construction-linked payment plan is worth more to that buyer than a discount, and costs the project less.
Channel
In person; a dedicated meeting with the family CA or advocate for S1; a scheduled call including the spouse for S2 and S8.
Content / creative
Payment-plan comparison; home-loan pre-approval tie-ups; the allotment letter; for S4, the exchange-structure term sheet — the document Phase 5 said this segment responds to instead of advertising.
CTA
“Pay the token.”
KPI
Negotiation → booking 35%; average discount as a share of list price — the single number protecting the whole Phase 6 architecture; days in negotiation.
Measurement
CRM stage carrying quoted price and final price on every deal, with a discount register reviewed monthly against the two pricing rules. A rule that is not reported is not a rule.

Referral

Customer advocacyEstimated

71 bookings · 1.6/month · ₹1.69 L each

Objective
Convert buyers into introducers. Phase 7 models 19% of bookings — 71 units, roughly ₹96 crore — as referral-sourced, at ₹1.69 lakh per booking against ₹10.52 lakh for marketing-sourced.
Audience
Every booked buyer, weighted heavily: S1 frequently buys as multiple related households in the same project, and Phase 5 found its peers are the most persuasive channel available anywhere in this catchment. S4's first completed exchange is “the single most persuasive asset available.”
Message
“Bring the people you would want as neighbours.” Phase 5 found S1's first objection is “who else has bought?” — a referral programme answers that objection structurally rather than rhetorically.
Channel
The relationship manager; construction-milestone events on site for booked buyers; a WhatsApp group per community; a formal, documented referral scheme with a stated benefit.
Content / creative
Site-progress events buyers want to bring people to; a referral benefit expressed as something worth having — early access to the next tranche is worth more than a token discount in a scheme where Phase 6 makes villa inventory deliberately scarce.
CTA
“Introduce someone.”
KPI
Referral-sourced bookings against the 71-unit target — 1.6 a month; referrals per booked buyer; cost per referral booking.
Measurement
A referrer field on every booking, reconciled monthly against the 19% assumption. Phase 7 flagged a 10-point error here as roughly ₹3.5 crore of misallocated budget.

Public, findable proof — and the credential for the next project. Phase 4 found KMV's search visibility thin and its review presence sparse; this is the stage that fixes that at source rather than through SEO.

The finding that should govern the whole plan

The linear journey — the one the twelve-stage model describes — carries 47.5% of units but only 33.4% of revenue.

The other two-thirds of revenue travels one of three routes that skip, reorder or compress the stages. A media plan built to the linear model would be pacing 100% of its budget against a third of the money.

Journey 1 · Linear
178 units · ₹169.2 Cr · S5, S6, most of S2 · Communities A and B · 3–10 months
Awareness→Discovery→Interest→Consideration→Lead→Qualified→Consultation→Site visit→Negotiation→Booking

Runs through all twelve stages roughly in order. Awareness through Consideration are genuinely media-driven; the lead form works; they visit on a Sunday comparing two or three projects; the close follows a price-sheet conversation.

This is the journey the standard funnel was designed for, and the only one where conventional pacing applies. It is also the journey competing hardest — Phase 3's 3,269 mid-market units are aimed at exactly these households.

Journey 2 · Referral-entry
91 units · ₹153.5 Cr · S1 and the connected minority of S2 · Communities C and D
AwarenessInterestConsideration Someone else's advocacy→Discovery (verification)→Consultation→Site visit→Negotiation→Booking→Referral

Stages 1–4 happen socially, not through media. Phase 5: "referral and personal introduction first… digital is a verification channel, not a discovery channel." By the time this buyer is reachable they already know the project exists, who is building it, and — critically — who else has bought.

1 · Discovery is a verification stage, not a discovery stage. They will search the developer's name and read page one. Phase 4 found that page is thin. For this journey, fixing search visibility is not lead generation — it is loss prevention on demand that already exists.

2 · The entry point is a person, not a campaign. Which is why Phase 6 specified Community D's first tranche should be seeded rather than advertised.

3 · The CA and the family advocate are in this journey and addressed by nobody. Phase 5 flagged them as gatekeepers no Phase 3 competitor engages. A consultation designed to include them is a cheap, uncontested advantage.

Journey 3 · Window
95 units · ₹155.4 Cr · S3, S7, S8 · Communities B and D
Awareness→Discovery→Interest→Consideration 6–18 MONTHS →Lead→Qualified→Consultation (video) 5 DAYS →Site visit→Booking

The stages do not compress evenly — they stretch, then collapse. Awareness to Consideration takes 6–18 months of slow, self-directed, digital research at odd hours, with very low form-fill rates. Site visit to Booking compresses entirely into a travel window. Phase 5, on S3: "if the site is not ready to close in that window, the sale is lost for a year."

1 · The buying windows are December–January and summer, set by the diaspora travel calendar, not the Indian festive quarter. A launch calendar built on Dasara–Diwali misses ₹155 crore of demand.

2 · The site visit may be performed by a proxy. Phase 5 found the inspecting relative holds effective veto and "is almost never the person the marketing is aimed at." Stage 8 needs a proxy-visit protocol; Stage 7 needs to work over video as the primary interaction.

3 · This is where the worst funnel stage sits. Community B's qualified-to-visit rate of 18.2% is 30% below project average, and Phase 7 found B consumes 65% of the lead-generation budget for 22% of units. The constraint is not lead cost — it is that this buyer physically cannot visit.

Journey 4 · Document
12 units · ₹27.9 Cr · S4 landowners · Community D, and a land supplier · 6–24 months
AwarenessInterestLeadSite visit Village relationship→Term sheet→Family consultation→Precedent→Exchange

This journey does not use the media funnel at all. Phase 5: "does not respond to advertising; it responds to a document" — and "does not need to visit: they live there."

1 · Stage 8 is meaningless and Stage 12 is everything. They can see the site from their land. What they need is proof that someone they know was treated as promised — "the first neighbour to complete a transaction becomes the single most persuasive asset available."

2 · It needs a function, not a campaign — a land-owner relations role working in Telugu, in the villages, with the local advocate and surveyor. Phase 3 found no competitor in the screened set offers a structured land-for-home proposition at all.

3 · Twelve units and ₹27.9 crore understates it. Phase 5: this segment "supplies land, absorbs inventory and creates local advocacy at the same time." Its 6–24 month cycle means it must start in year one to deliver in year three.

Measurement architecture

Four gates are counted. Eight stages are estimated. And with this cycle length, no attribution model can be made honest.

Two recommendations follow directly. Promote Consultation and Negotiation to counted gates. Both are CRM stage changes that cost nothing to record, both sit inside conversions Phase 7 measures only in aggregate, and both are where the sales team's own performance is currently invisible. That gives six counted gates instead of four — and lets the visit-to-booking rate, the model's most sensitive variable, be decomposed into its two halves rather than watched as one number.
The attribution problem, and the one-question fix

The consideration cycle runs 3 to 18 months, and 47% of bookings arrive through channel partners and referral — routes that leave no click trail at all.

Under last-click attribution the brand layer will appear to produce nothing, the referral layer will appear to produce nothing, and the last paid touch before booking will appear to produce everything. Budget follows the report. Within two quarters that logic defunds the two layers delivering the cheapest revenue in the project.

The fix is not a better attribution model. With this cycle length and this channel mix, no click-based model can be made honest. The fix is one question, asked at booking: "How did you first hear about us?" — asked by the relationship manager, recorded as a required CRM field, with a fixed option list.

Attribution modelFirst-touch self-report at booking

376 honest answers beat 78,880 attributed clicks, because the question is asked of the only people whose answer matters. Digital attribution still runs — it optimises campaigns within the demand-generation layer, where clicks and conversions are close together. It does not decide the split between layers.

The capacity constraint hiding in these numbers

65 site visits a month is not the hard number — the distribution is.

Phase 5 established that S1, S5 and S6 all visit on Sundays, and that S1 arrives unannounced with three to six family members. If two-thirds of monthly visits land on four Sundays, that is roughly eleven visiting parties per Sunday, averaging three to four people each — 35 to 45 people on site in a day.

The experience centre and show homes are not sized by monthly visits; they are sized by peak Sunday. Under-sizing them caps the highest-leverage stage in the plan, and no amount of media spend upstream can push more people through a constrained Sunday. This should be settled at the design stage of Phase 7's ₹5.00 crore sales-infrastructure line, not discovered in month four.

The counter-intuitive consequence

The 66,406 leads lost at the top are the least valuable losses in the funnel.

Chasing them with more media, better forms or cheaper CPL is optimising the cheapest stage. The 2,348 people who visited the site and did not proceed are worth far more — they have been paid for all the way through, they have stood on the land, and the difference between 6.9% and 7.9% is ₹39.4 crore.

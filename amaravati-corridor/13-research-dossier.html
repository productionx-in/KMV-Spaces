Six findings that overturn earlier phases

Read these first. Four of the six weaken the strategy, and one of them is the credential everything else rests on.

1
The AIIMS credential is unverified as to scope — and the whole brand position rests on it
Phases 1–12 said“KMV Projects built AIIMS Mangalagiri.” Recorded as FACT in Phase 4 and used as the answer to the one anxiety all eight segments share.
Verification foundKMV lists it on its own site. But the Ministry of Health tender names HSCC (India) Ltd as Executing Agency, and a third source references L&T. No source found establishes KMV's scope — main contractor, package, or sub.
ConsequenceThe two are not actually contradictory — HSCC is the government's agency, contractors sit beneath it. But only the weakest source asserts KMV. A work order resolves it in one document; until then the sentence cannot be published in Phase 4's form.
2
KMV Vivaan is not on the corridor, and its density was wrong
Phases 1–12 saidVivaan is the corridor precedent for both the product ladder and honest density, at 6.6 units/acre.
Verification foundVivaan is at Poranki / Penamaluru, Vijayawada — confirmed by a government environment-clearance filing naming Survey No. 20/P, Poranki. 92 units on 10 acres = 9.2/acre.
ConsequenceThe precedent is real but it is not a corridor precedent, and the density figure was overstated by 39%.
3
KMV already sells villas above the price this plan calls ambitious
Phases 1–12 saidCommunity D at ₹2.30–2.74 Cr is the top of the ladder, in a band seven phases could not prove was occupied.
Verification foundKMV Vivaan asks from ₹3.59 Cr (Phase I) and ₹5.21 Cr (Phase II) — on the developer's own site.
ConsequenceThe developer's existing villa product is priced above the proposed Community D. They hold booking data twelve phases lacked, and it may resolve the largest open question in the exercise in an afternoon.
4
The plan asks a 50% premium to the locality — and nobody had ever sized it
Phases 1–12 saidBlended realisation ₹6,098/sq ft, derived bottom-up from competitor tickets.
Verification foundMangalagiri locality average asking is ₹4,062/sq ft; the wider micromarket ₹5,489. Manjeera Monarch, a delivered comparable, sits at ₹5,100.
ConsequenceA 50% premium to locality, 11% to micromarket. Defensible for low-density product — but Community A at ₹5,150 now prices above a delivered apartment comparable, with no delivery record to justify it. A is 45% of the units.
5
Amaravati's capital status is stronger than any earlier phase could establish
Phases 1–12 saidGraded cautiously throughout, because no primary source could be retrieved.
Verification foundParliament granted statutory status to Amaravati as AP's sole capital (Prasar Bharati, 2 Apr 2026). Works worth ₹57,821 crore in progress; ₹15,000 crore in the 2025–26 state budget.
ConsequenceUpgrade to CONFIRMED. The largest political risk in the demand thesis is resolved by statute — though statutory status is not population, and construction spend is not consumer demand.
6
The net-zero claim could not be corroborated at all
Phases 1–12 said“India's first net-zero-energy residential villa community” — the audited proof asset, and a Phase 10 creative pillar.
Verification foundNothing. Not in the two KMV Spaces project pages surfaced, not in portal listings, not in the 2016 environment-clearance filing, not in news coverage.
ConsequenceAbsence in search is not disproof. But a first-in-India superlative that does not surface in a direct search must not be published without the certificate in hand.

Reconciling them — the sources are not actually in conflict

Under the PMSSY structure, HSCC (India) Ltd is routinely the government's executing agency for AIIMS projects, with civil contractors appointed beneath it. KMV being a contractor is entirely consistent with HSCC being the executing agency. The two describe different layers.

What is not established by any source found is KMV's scope: main civil contractor for the whole campus, contractor for certain packages, or sub-contractor.

Why this is decisive rather than pedantic. The strategy does not need KMV to have been the executing agency. It needs a defensible sentence. “KMV built AIIMS Mangalagiri” may be true, overstated, or misleading depending on scope — and it will be published to a market whose top complaint is misrepresentation. A work order resolves it in one document.

  • Not in the two KMV Spaces project pages surfaced, not in portal listings, not in the 2016 environment-clearance filing, not in news.
  • Absence in search is not disproof.
  • But a first-in-India superlative that does not surface in a direct search must not be published without the certificate in hand.

  • Two domains, split equity — confirmed. Both surfaced as separate entities in the same searches; portals carry the projects independently.
  • The lineage is real and on the wrong site. The institutional portfolio sits on kmvprojects.com; the consumer brand is kmvspaces.com.

II

The sixteen answers

Read as hypothesis, not conclusion. Each names what would falsify it; four marked ⚠ change materially if the AIIMS scope resolves badly.

Strategic hypothesis  Everything in this part carries that tag unless stated otherwise. None of it is a finding.

1
Where should the project be positioned?

Against the corridor's density, not its price. Fifteen homes per acre across twenty-five acres, where the nearest apartment comparable runs at multiples of that. Not “luxury,” not “premium,” not “close to the capital” — Phase 3 established all three are universal and meaningless here.

The corridor's only development you can move within, at a density you can count.

Falsified by The density comparators failing to source (§06), or the locality proving unwilling to pay a 50% premium (§05).
2
Who should it target?

Not one audience — four, mapped to four communities. Priority from Phase 5, adjusted by verification:

1S4 landowners — best-evidenced population, no competitor contestingD and C, via exchange
2S1 trade families — largest revenue, cash-capableC and D
3S3 US diaspora — highest priority scoreB and C
4S2 senior medical — institution confirmed, ladder maps to pay scaleA through C
5S7 Hyderabad, S6 upgradersA and B
↓De-weight S5 government — sizing is nine years staleA
Falsified by Segment sizing, once six of eight are established from registration and institutional data.
3
⚠ Why would those people buy?

Different reasons per segment — conflating them is the error the brief warns against.

  • S4 — converts an illiquid returnable plot into a built, income-capable asset without finding cash.
  • S1 — a verifiable, low-density asset in a corridor where everything else is a tower.
  • S3 — a managed, remotely-purchasable home in the origin district, with someone answering in their time zone.
  • S2 — proximity to AIIMS, and a ladder that matches a published pay scale rung for rung.
  • All eight — delivery certainty, which Phase 5 found is the one shared anxiety. ⚠ This is the answer that depends on §07.
Falsified by The AIIMS scope failing to document, which removes the proof behind the shared objection.
4
⚠ What should KMV own in the customer's mind?

“The builder who shows you the evidence.”

Not luxury, not scale. The construction lineage, published delivery performance, ungated pricing, carpet area declared unprompted, a live construction feed. Phase 3 found not one competitor markets against the category's own top complaint.

⚠ This is the recommendation most exposed by verification. It rests on institutional credentials whose scope is undocumented (§07) and a certification that could not be corroborated. If neither documents, fall back to what is provable without any credential at all: the density arithmetic and the ungated price sheet.

Falsified by Appendix A #1 and #4. Both are single documents held by the developer.
5
How should A/B/C/D be differentiated?

By buyer constraint, not by finish level.

The anti-cannibalisation rules are structural, not promotional: B never above ₹1.50 Cr, C never below ₹1.65 Cr, and B is defined by service while C is defined by land — a buyer choosing between them is choosing between two products, not two price points.

Falsified by Community A's pricing (§09), which may need to fall below Manjeera Monarch rather than above it.
6
⚠ What should the master campaign communicate?

One computable fact, and an invitation to check it. Fifteen to the acre; everything else is open.

What it must not do: claim superlatives, claim future infrastructure as present (§03), or lead with the AIIMS credential until §07 resolves. ⚠

Falsified by The comparators failing to source — in which case the claim must be withdrawn or rebuilt, not softened.
7
Which channels should drive awareness?

Outdoor on the corridor, Telugu OTT and regional cinema, and PR against the capital narrative. These reach the domestic segments in-market and cannot be judged on click metrics — Phase 9 risk 7.

Plus one that verification made urgent: branded and category search. Broker interception is live today (§11).

Falsified by Nothing found contradicts this. It is untested rather than at risk.
8
Which channels should drive demand?

Meta and Google Search carry it; portals are capped; LinkedIn is S3/S7 only.

The correction verification forces: portals are the corridor's dominant organic surface and they are rented. Capping portal spend at ₹1.00 Cr only works if owned search is defended simultaneously — otherwise capping the rented channel while leaving the owned one intercepted loses both.

Falsified by The CPL assumptions, which are borrowed from other markets and untested here.
9
How should leads be qualified?

On cash at purchase, not income — Phase 5's central finding. The gate is ₹17.6 L for A rising to ₹74.1 L for D.

Three required fields at capture: budget band, timeline, and country / origin district. Qualification criteria must be written down and owned by a named person — Phase 12 found neither exists.

Falsified by Nothing external. This is an internal discipline question.
10
How should site visits be generated?

The site visit is the ₹39.4-crore-per-point stage. Treat it as the product.

Domestic: the counted walk, with an accessible route. Overseas: the proxy-visit protocol and video walkthrough — piloted on the existing project before launch, because ₹12.63 Cr of Community B budget depends on a capability never once tried.

Falsified by The proxy-visit pilot converting below half the physical-visit rate.
11
How should marketing work with sales?

On one shared number: site-visit-to-booking rate, weekly, by community and by consultant.

Marketing owns everything up to the booked visit; sales owns the visit onward; both are read on the same metric so neither can blame the other's stage. A structured visit debrief with a closed objection taxonomy is the handover artefact.

Falsified by Nothing external.
12
What should the CRM journey look like?

Twelve stages, of which two are currently fictional. Consultation and negotiation are modelled but have no defined trigger — define both before go-live.

Non-negotiable on day one: WhatsApp Business API integrated (un-integrated it is a liability, not a channel), first-touch attribution as a mandatory picklist, and a named CRM owner.

Falsified by Nothing external.

15
What should determine marketing budget allocation?

Four rules, in order:

  1. Bottom-up, never a percentage. Phase 11 showed top-down under-funds everything that is not media by 28%.
  2. By structural class, not by total. Only ₹21.17 Cr of ₹31.75 Cr is genuinely flexible.
  3. By marginal revenue at risk. ₹1 cut from performance media risks ₹24.60 — while the CPLs hold, which is untested.
  4. Re-based annually, falling toward the 4–5% norm as referral compounds.
Falsified by The catchment CPL test returning materially different numbers, which re-bases rule 3 entirely.
16
How do we optimise toward revenue rather than vanity metrics?
  1. Never optimise the top of the funnel. 66,406 leads are lost at the top and they are the least valuable losses. 2,348 lost site visits are worth more.
  2. Compare within a layer, never across. The naive cost-per-booking-by-channel reading defunds ₹10.25 Cr of the highest-value spend — put the rule in the reporting template before the first review.
  3. Judge brand, outdoor and the experience centre on holdouts and first-touch, never on last click.
  4. Report revenue at risk, not spend efficiency. “CPL improved 12%” is not a result; “site-visit conversion moved 0.4 points, worth ₹15.8 Cr” is.
  5. Kill metrics that cannot change a decision. Impressions, reach, followers and page views appear nowhere in 13 or 14 — deliberately.
The measurement instruction that matters most

Of the six weekly metrics, five require CRM discipline that does not currently exist — a named owner, written qualification criteria, WhatsApp integration, defined stage triggers, and a mandatory first-touch field.

Building the measurement system is a pre-launch dependency, not a reporting task.

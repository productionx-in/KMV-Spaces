# Phase 8 — The Marketing Funnel

## Twelve stages, four layers, and the three journeys the linear model does not describe

**Prepared:** August 2026
**Purpose:** Complete customer journey design for the Phase 6 architecture, tied to the Phase 7 funnel economics
**Method note:** The twelve stages in the brief are built and specified in full (§2). But two things have to be said first, because a journey map that hides them produces a dashboard nobody can act on: **only four of the twelve stages are actually counted** (§4), and **at least three of the eight Phase 5 segments do not travel this journey in order** (§3).

---

## 0. What this phase does, and two problems with the model it is asked to build

Phase 7 produced a funnel with four measured gates — lead, qualified lead, site visit, booking — and the arithmetic connecting them to ₹506.7 crore. This phase turns that skeleton into a **designed journey**: what each stage is for, who is in it, what is said, where, with what asset, to what call to action, measured how.

Two honest problems, stated up front rather than discovered in execution.

### 0.1 The twelve-stage model is a planning device, not a measurement system

Of the twelve stages, **four are hard gates** — a system records an unambiguous event. The other eight are **inferred from proxies**. That distinction matters commercially, because a stage that cannot be counted cannot justify budget on its own, and treating a proxy as a count is how marketing dashboards fill with numbers that move without meaning anything.

§4 sets out which is which and what each estimated stage is actually measured by.

### 0.2 The linear journey describes roughly half the buyers

**Established — from Phase 5.** The eight segments buy in structurally different ways:

- **S1 trade and business families** — *"referral and personal introduction first… digital is a verification channel, not a discovery channel."* Their awareness, discovery, interest and consideration happen socially, through a peer, before any media touches them. **They enter the funnel at Consultation or Site Visit.**
- **S3 US diaspora** — *6–18 months of consideration, then a five-day decision window during the India visit.* Stages stretch, then compress. A monthly pacing plan misses them entirely.
- **S4 land-pooling landowners** — *"does not respond to advertising; it responds to a document."* They never enter the media funnel at all.

> **Reasoned. Building one journey and running everyone through it would misallocate the budget toward the segments that are cheapest to reach and least valuable per unit** — which is precisely the failure Phase 7 §1.1 warned about when it found a Community D booking is worth 3.38 Community A bookings.

§3 maps four journeys. §2 specifies the stages that all four draw on.

## 1. The four layers

The brief asks for brand building, demand generation, sales conversion and customer advocacy to be separated. **They are separated here by what each is accountable for and what budget it holds** — because that is the only separation that survives contact with a P&L.

| Layer | Stages | Accountable for | Budget | Share |
| --- | --- | --- | --: | --: |
| **Brand building** | Awareness · Discovery | **The price premium.** Whether a ₹6,500/sq ft villa is credible at all before it is quoted | **₹6.30 Cr** | 24.5% |
| **Demand generation** | Interest · Consideration · Lead · Qualified Lead | **Lead volume and quality at a cost.** CPL, CPQL, and the 15.8% lead-to-qualified rate | **₹9.84 Cr** | 38.3% |
| **Sales conversion** | Consultation · Site Visit · Negotiation · Booking | **The 6.9% visit-to-booking rate** — Phase 7's most sensitive variable — and price discipline | **₹8.34 Cr** | 32.5% |
| **Customer advocacy** | Referral · Advocacy | **71 bookings — 19% of the scheme, ~₹96 crore** — at the lowest cost per booking available | **₹1.20 Cr** | 4.7% |
| | | | **₹25.68 Cr** | 100% |

*Sales conversion holds ₹5.00 Cr of sales infrastructure plus ₹3.34 Cr of channel-partner commission. Brand building holds Phase 7's brand-and-content line.*

### 1.1 One change to the Phase 7 budget, and why

**Computed.** Phase 7's base case allocated ₹25.67 crore across lead generation, brand, sales infrastructure and channel-partner commission. **It contained no line for advocacy at all** — while simultaneously modelling 19% of bookings as referral-sourced.

> **A layer delivering 71 bookings and roughly ₹96 crore of revenue had a budget of zero.** That is not frugality; it is an omission that would have shown up as an unexplained shortfall in year two.

This phase carves **₹1.20 crore out of the brand line** into an explicit advocacy and referral programme. The arithmetic justifies it immediately:

| Source | Budget | Bookings | **Cost per booking** |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| Marketing-sourced | ₹21.14 Cr | 201 | **₹10.52 L** |
| Channel partner | ₹3.34 Cr | 105 | **₹3.18 L** |
| **Referral** | **₹1.20 Cr** | **71** | **₹1.69 L** |

**Referral is 6.2× cheaper per booking than marketing-sourced demand.** Phase 7 §5.3 found the same thing from the other direction — Community C, the most referral-dependent community, is the most efficient product in the scheme. **Funding the cheapest channel at zero was the single clearest gap in the model.**

### 1.2 The layer boundary that is usually drawn wrong

**Reasoned.** Advocacy is conventionally drawn as the *last* layer — what happens after the sale. In this project it is also the *first*.

Phase 5 found S1's opening objection is **"who else has bought?"** Phase 6 concluded that Community D's first tranche should be **seeded rather than advertised**. Phase 7 found that the segment which cannot be bought with advertising delivers the cheapest revenue.

> **The funnel is a loop.** Advocacy feeds Awareness for the segments that carry 53% of revenue. Drawn as a straight line ending in advocacy, the model would place the cheapest and most persuasive asset in the project at the point furthest from the launch — where it can do nothing.

**The operational consequence:** the advocacy layer starts working **before the first booking**, not after. Its year-one job is not referral volume; it is manufacturing the first fifteen visible, named, local buyers whose existence answers S1's first question.

### BRAND BUILDING

#### Stage 1 · Awareness

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Establish that a 25-acre, low-density development exists on this corridor, and that its developer built AIIMS Mangalagiri. **Make the price premium thinkable before it is ever quoted.** |
| **Audience** | The full Phase 5 universe, weighted to S1, S5 and S6 in the Vijayawada–Guntur catchment; S7 in Hyderabad; S3 in US metros with coastal-Andhra origin. |
| **Message** | Not "luxury homes in Amaravati" — every competitor says that. The two things none of them can say: **the developer that built AIIMS Mangalagiri is building 25 acres beside it**, and **fifteen homes per acre where the corridor builds fifty-three to a hundred and thirteen.** Delivery and density, not aspiration. |
| **Channel** | NH-16 and Vijayawada–Guntur corridor hoardings; Mangalagiri bypass; Telugu press (Eenadu, Sakshi, Andhra Jyothi); Telugu YouTube and regional OTT; Meta and Google video for reach; Hyderabad OOH for S7; diaspora digital for S3. |
| **Content / creative** | A 60-second film shot on the actual corridor. An aerial holding 25 acres against the 5-to-11-acre neighbours — the density difference is a *visual* fact, not a claim. The AIIMS credential as a verifiable title card. |
| **CTA** | **None — deliberately.** An awareness asset carrying a lead form is a demand-generation asset with its reach wasted. The next stage exists to catch the response. |
| **KPI** | Prompted and unprompted brand recall in the catchment; **share of category search** against the eleven Phase 3 competitors. |
| **Measurement** | Quarterly recall survey, n ≈ 400, split Vijayawada / Guntur / Mangalagiri. Search-volume share on a fixed competitor set. **Not clicks, and not reach.** |

#### Stage 2 · Discovery

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | When someone goes looking — because they saw a hoarding, a peer mentioned it, or they searched "villas in Mangalagiri" — they find something authoritative. **Phase 4 found KMV's search visibility thin. This stage is where that costs money.** |
| **Audience** | Anyone actively looking. Highest value: **S2**, who Phase 5 found *"will check RERA — most of the catchment will not"*; **S3**, researching at night from a different time zone; **S7**. |
| **Message** | Proof, in this order: RERA number · land parcel · density · developer record · possession date. **Specificity is the message.** |
| **Channel** | Organic search; Google Business Profile; the project website; the RERA listing; portal listings (99acres, MagicBricks, Housing) treated as *presence*, not as a lead source; YouTube; Telugu-language search. |
| **Content / creative** | A project site that answers the questions Phase 5's segments actually ask — including the ones competitors avoid: price per square foot, a unit-wise price sheet, the possession date, the RERA number, and the AIIMS credential with evidence attached. |
| **CTA** | *"See the price sheet." "Check the RERA registration."* Low friction, **not lead-gated** — gating price is what makes a buyer leave for a competitor who publishes it. |
| **KPI** | Branded search volume; organic visibility on a fixed set of ~20 corridor commercial queries; Google Business Profile views and direction requests. |
| **Measurement** | Search Console; GBP Insights; rank tracking on the fixed query set, **baselined against the Phase 4 audit** so the improvement is provable rather than asserted. |

### DEMAND GENERATION

#### Stage 3 · Interest

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Convert passive attention into a **self-identified product interest** — which of the four communities, which ticket band. Routing starts here, not at the lead form. |
| **Audience** | Segmented four ways from the first impression, because Phase 6 established the four communities share almost no buyer. |
| **Message** | **Four messages, never one.** A — *the lowest-density apartment in the corridor, from ₹59 lakh.* B — *own it from anywhere; leave it without worrying.* C — *a villa inside a masterplan, not a villa on a plot.* D — *estate-scale living below the price the luxury tier starts at.* |
| **Channel** | Meta and Google demand generation, structured by community; YouTube; Telugu digital for S8; LinkedIn, diaspora associations and **APNRTS in Guntur** for S3 and S7. |
| **Content / creative** | Community-specific and comparative: a walkthrough film per community; floor plates; **the density comparison graphic**; a commute-time map to AIIMS, the Secretariat and Benz Circle — Phase 5 found S2 wants the drive *measured*, not described. |
| **CTA** | *"Download the [community] brochure." "See the floor plans."* A content exchange, not a hard enquiry. |
| **KPI** | Engaged sessions per community; video 50%-completion rate; brochure downloads; **cost per engaged session by community**. |
| **Measurement** | GA4 with community as a content dimension; platform view-through; **UTM discipline enforced by community** — without it, nothing downstream can be attributed to the right product. |

#### Stage 4 · Consideration

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Get the buyer to compare deliberately — **and to compare on the dimensions where this project wins** (density, masterplan, delivery record) rather than the one where it does not (₹/sq ft against Prime Grandeur). |
| **Audience** | Active shoppers. Phase 5: S6 compares three to five projects across two or three weekends; S2 builds a spreadsheet; S3 researches for months. |
| **Message** | **Set the comparison frame before the competitor does.** *"Twenty homes an acre, or a hundred and thirteen. That is the choice."* For Community C, the +20% over Prime Grandeur is **explained** in land, neighbours and delivery record — not defended. |
| **Channel** | Website comparison content; retargeting across Meta, Google and YouTube; email and WhatsApp nurture; third-party proof — RERA, the AIIMS record, construction progress. |
| **Content / creative** | A density comparison tool. An **EMI and eligibility calculator** — Phase 5 found S5 responds directly to these. A *"what ₹1.8 crore actually buys, here and elsewhere"* piece. Monthly construction-progress updates. |
| **CTA** | *"Book a call with the project lead." "Get the full price sheet."* |
| **KPI** | Return-visit rate; days from first to second visit; calculator completions; retargeting-assisted conversions. |
| **Measurement** | GA4 return-visitor cohorts; CRM lag from first touch to enquiry. **This stage's value shows up as a shorter consideration cycle, not as a conversion count.** |

#### Stage 5 · Lead

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Capture a contactable record carrying enough to route it to the right community and the right salesperson. |
| **Audience** | All eight segments — **but they do not submit in the same way.** Phase 5: S5 and S6 *"will submit enquiry forms — one of the few segments that reliably will."* S1 *"is unlikely to fill a portal form; likely to call a number seen on a hoarding."* S3 has *"very low form-fill rates."* S4 does not fill forms at all. |
| **Message** | Match the promise the channel made. **Never gate at the form what was promised ungated in the ad.** |
| **Channel** | Website forms; click-to-call; **WhatsApp Business as a first-class capture path, not an afterthought**; portal enquiries; walk-ins; channel-partner submissions; **a missed-call number for the low-digital cohort**. |
| **Content / creative** | A four-field form — name, phone, community of interest, timeline. Nothing longer. Every additional field costs leads from the segments that were reluctant to begin with. |
| **CTA** | *"Talk to us." "Request a callback."* Or simply the WhatsApp icon. |
| **KPI** | **78,880 leads over 45 months — 1,753 a month, 67 a working day.** Cost per lead by community: **A ₹350 · B ₹1,600 · C ₹900 · D ₹1,900.** |
| **Measurement** | The CRM as single source of truth. Every lead carries source, campaign and community — and the **self-reported first-touch question is asked at booking, not here**, for the reason set out in §4.3. |

#### Stage 6 · Qualified Lead

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Establish contact, budget, timeline and intent. **Phase 7's model assumes only 15.8% survive this** — 12,474 of 78,880. |
| **Audience** | All leads. The qualifying questions differ by community: **A** on down-payment readiness; **B** on residency and travel window; **C and D** on cash position and who else is in the decision. |
| **Message** | Helpful, not pushy. Phase 5 found S2 *"highly averse to sales pressure"* and S3's stated primary concern is **fraud** — a hard-sell first call confirms exactly the fear that stops the purchase. |
| **Channel** | Outbound call within 15 minutes of the enquiry; **WhatsApp for S3 and S8**; email for S2, who Phase 5 found *"often cannot take calls during the day."* |
| **Content / creative** | A qualification script per community. For S3: a **time-zone-aware scheduling link**, not a call attempt at 11 a.m. IST. |
| **CTA** | *"Let's find twenty minutes."* |
| **KPI** | **Lead → qualified 15.8%** (A 19.8% · B 13.5% · C 18.0% · D 15.3%); speed to first contact; contact rate; **277 qualified leads a month**. |
| **Measurement** | CRM stage transitions with timestamps; sampled call-recording review. **Community B's rate tracked separately and reported weekly**, since the model assumes it runs 30% worse and Phase 7 §5 found B consumes 65% of the lead-generation budget. |

### SALES CONVERSION

#### Stage 7 · Consultation

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | A structured conversation **before** the site visit that fixes need, budget and community — so the visit is a confirmation rather than a discovery. |
| **Audience** | Qualified leads. **For S3 this is the primary sales interaction**, because the physical visit may never happen before the decision. |
| **Message** | Diagnostic, not pitching — including **recommending Community A to someone who enquired on D**. Phase 6's architecture only works if buyers land in the community built for them; a mis-sold B unit to a resident family is a cancellation waiting to happen. |
| **Channel** | In person at the experience centre; video call for S3, S7 and S8; **the family CA or advocate included for S1** — Phase 5 found they are gatekeepers *"not currently addressed by any competitor."* |
| **Content / creative** | Price sheet, payment-plan options, EMI working, possession schedule, RERA documents. For S3: a **FEMA, Power of Attorney and repatriation explainer** — the fraud objection answered with process rather than reassurance. |
| **CTA** | *"Come and walk it."* Or, for S3: *"Let's schedule the live walkthrough."* |
| **KPI** | **42.6% of qualified leads reach consultation — 5,313 total, 118 a month.** Consultation → visit 55%. **Community-reassignment rate**, which measures routing quality. |
| **Measurement** | CRM activity logging. **Note: Phase 7 does not model this as a separate gate** — it sits inside the 23.4% qualified-to-visit conversion. It must be logged as an explicit sub-step or it becomes invisible and unmanageable. |

#### Stage 8 · Site Visit

*The highest-leverage stage in the entire plan.*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | **Make the density visible.** Phase 6: *"a difference visible from the site gate."* Phase 7: visit-to-booking is the single most sensitive variable in the model — a two-point fall costs more than a 25% rise in media prices. |
| **Audience** | **2,922 visits over 45 months — 65 a month, 15 a week.** And they arrive completely differently: **S1** unannounced, Sunday, 3–6 family members. **S2** by appointment, couple only, weekday evening. **S5 and S6** Sunday with children, comparing two or three projects the same day. **S3** by proxy relative first, then one compressed trip. **S7** Friday-to-Sunday combined with a family visit. |
| **Message** | **The site does the talking.** The sales role is to time the drive from AIIMS or the Secretariat on the way in, and to walk the central open space rather than describe it. |
| **Channel** | The experience centre and two show homes — Phase 7's ₹5.00 crore line. Plus a **live video walkthrough capability** for S3 and S8, staffed to their time zones. |
| **Content / creative** | A furnished show home in C or D; the masterplan model; **the density comparison demonstrated on site, not on paper**; visible construction progress; a Telugu-speaking host. |
| **CTA** | *"Choose your plot." "Block a unit."* |
| **KPI** | **Visit → booking 6.9% blended** — A 8.1% · B 6.0% · C 7.0% · D 5.6%. Repeat-visit rate; visits per booking; **weekend capacity utilisation**, since S1, S5 and S6 all visit on Sundays and the constraint is host availability, not demand. |
| **Measurement** | Attendance logged with **party size and segment**; 24-hour follow-up logged; visit-to-booking tracked by community *and* by salesperson. **This is the number to instrument first** — §7 item 1. |

#### Stage 9 · Negotiation

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Close **without breaking the two Phase 6 pricing rules**: Community B never above ₹1.50 crore, Community C never below ₹1.65 crore. Breaking either collapses the ₹24 lakh gap that keeps them from competing. |
| **Audience** | Visited, engaged, not yet committed — **574 negotiations, 13 a month.** The decision unit is rarely one person: S1's CA and eldest brother; S2's spouse, co-equal; S3's inspecting relative, who holds effective veto; S8's spouse in India, who will actually live there. |
| **Message** | **Flexibility on structure, discipline on price.** Phase 5 found S2 clears the ₹94,800 EMI but struggles with ₹52 lakh in cash — **a construction-linked payment plan is worth more to that buyer than a discount**, and costs the project less. |
| **Channel** | In person; a dedicated meeting with the family CA or advocate for S1; a scheduled call **including the spouse** for S2 and S8. |
| **Content / creative** | Payment-plan comparison; home-loan pre-approval tie-ups; the allotment letter; **for S4, the exchange-structure term sheet** — the document Phase 5 said this segment responds to instead of advertising. |
| **CTA** | *"Pay the token."* |
| **KPI** | Negotiation → booking 35%; **average discount as a share of list price — the single number protecting the whole Phase 6 architecture**; days in negotiation. |
| **Measurement** | CRM stage carrying **quoted price and final price on every deal**, with a **discount register reviewed monthly against the two pricing rules.** A rule that is not reported is not a rule. |

#### Stage 10 · Booking

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Token to signed agreement — **and capture the one data field that fixes attribution for the entire programme.** |
| **Audience** | 376 buyers across 36–54 months. **8.4 a month** in the base case: 4.5 marketing-sourced, 2.3 channel partner, 1.6 referral. |
| **Message** | Confirmation and reassurance. Phase 5 found **delivery anxiety is the one concern shared by all eight segments** — it is what the first post-booking communication should address. |
| **Channel** | In person; **remote under registered Power of Attorney for S3**, which Phase 5 established is legally routine and is the difference between capturing that segment and losing it to a travel schedule. |
| **Content / creative** | Allotment letter; RERA-compliant agreement; payment schedule; a **named relationship manager**; the construction-update cadence, committed in writing. |
| **CTA** | *"Complete the agreement."* |
| **KPI** | Bookings a month against 8.4; token-to-agreement conversion; **cancellation rate**; revenue per booking ₹1.35 crore. |
| **Measurement** | CRM plus RERA filings. **And the field that matters most: self-reported first touch — "how did you first hear about us" — asked here, at booking.** §4.3 explains why this single question is worth more than the attribution model. |

### CUSTOMER ADVOCACY

#### Stage 11 · Referral

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | Convert buyers into introducers. **Phase 7 models 19% of bookings — 71 units, roughly ₹96 crore — as referral-sourced**, at ₹1.69 lakh per booking against ₹10.52 lakh for marketing-sourced. |
| **Audience** | Every booked buyer, weighted heavily: **S1 frequently buys as multiple related households in the same project**, and Phase 5 found its peers are the most persuasive channel available anywhere in this catchment. **S4's first completed exchange is *"the single most persuasive asset available."*** |
| **Message** | *"Bring the people you would want as neighbours."* Phase 5 found S1's first objection is **"who else has bought?"** — a referral programme answers that objection structurally rather than rhetorically. |
| **Channel** | The relationship manager; **construction-milestone events on site for booked buyers**; a WhatsApp group per community; a formal, documented referral scheme with a stated benefit. |
| **Content / creative** | Site-progress events buyers want to bring people to; a referral benefit expressed as something worth having — **early access to the next tranche is worth more than a token discount** in a scheme where Phase 6 makes villa inventory deliberately scarce. |
| **CTA** | *"Introduce someone."* |
| **KPI** | **Referral-sourced bookings against the 71-unit target — 1.6 a month**; referrals per booked buyer; cost per referral booking. |
| **Measurement** | A **referrer field on every booking**, reconciled monthly against the 19% assumption. Phase 7 §8 flagged a 10-point error here as roughly ₹3.5 crore of misallocated budget. |

#### Stage 12 · Advocacy

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Objective** | **Public, findable proof** — and the credential for the next project. Phase 4 found KMV's search visibility thin and its review presence sparse; this is the stage that fixes that at source rather than through SEO. |
| **Audience** | Booked and possessed buyers; the local community around the site; **the AIIMS and institutional networks**, which Phase 5 found are small, highly connected, and where *"a colleague buying"* is itself a purchase trigger. |
| **Message** | **Let them say it.** The most valuable content at this stage is not produced by the marketing function. |
| **Channel** | Google reviews; resident films on YouTube; Telugu press; **the landowner community across the 29 capital-area villages**, where Phase 5 found precedent is the decisive factor; institutional word-of-mouth at AIIMS and the hospital cluster. |
| **Content / creative** | Resident video testimonials; handover films; **a published construction and delivery record** — which is the asset Phase 4 established the developer already has in AIIMS Mangalagiri and does not yet use. |
| **CTA** | *"Share your experience."* |
| **KPI** | Google review volume and rating; NPS at defined milestones; unprompted mentions; **the share of new leads self-reporting "someone I know" as first touch** — the loop closing measurably. |
| **Measurement** | Review monitoring; quarterly NPS at booking, at slab completion and at handover; the booking-stage first-touch field read in aggregate over time. |

## 3. The three journeys the linear model does not describe

§2 specifies twelve stages. **Four different journeys draw on them, and only one runs through all twelve in order.**

### 3.1 The four journeys

**Reasoned** — segments mapped from Phase 5 §3 behaviour, unit and revenue shares rescaled from the Phase 5 §5.5 allocation onto Phase 6's 376 units.

| Journey | Segments | Units | Share | Revenue | Share |
| --- | --- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| **1 · Linear** | S5 government · S6 upgraders · 60% of S2 medical | **178** | 47.5% | **₹169.2 Cr** | 33.4% |
| **2 · Referral-entry** | S1 trade families · 40% of S2 medical | **91** | 24.1% | **₹153.5 Cr** | 30.3% |
| **3 · Window** | S3 US diaspora · S7 Hyderabad · S8 Gulf | **95** | 25.2% | **₹155.4 Cr** | 30.7% |
| **4 · Document** | S4 land-pooling landowners | **12** | 3.3% | **₹27.9 Cr** | 5.5% |

> **Computed — the finding that should govern the whole plan.**
>
> **The linear journey, which is the one the twelve-stage model describes, carries 47.5% of units but only 33.4% of revenue.** The other two-thirds of revenue travels one of three routes that skip, reorder or compress the stages.
>
> A media plan built to the linear model would be pacing 100% of its budget against a third of the money.

### 3.2 Journey 1 — Linear

**Who:** S5 government households, S6 Vijayawada upgraders, the digital-first majority of S2.
**Where they buy:** Communities A and B, with the top of S2 reaching C.

Runs through all twelve stages roughly in order, over **3–10 months**. Awareness through Consideration are genuinely media-driven; the lead form works; they visit on a Sunday comparing two or three projects; the close follows a price-sheet conversation.

**This is the journey the standard funnel was designed for, and the only one where conventional pacing applies.** It is also the journey competing hardest — Phase 3's 3,269 mid-market units are aimed at exactly these households.

### 3.3 Journey 2 — Referral-entry

**Who:** S1 trade and business families, and the connected minority of S2 whose trigger Phase 5 identified as *"a colleague buying — this is a small, highly connected professional community."*
**Where they buy:** Communities C and D — **30.3% of revenue from 24.1% of units.**

**Stages 1–4 happen socially, not through media.** Phase 5: *"referral and personal introduction first… digital is a verification channel, not a discovery channel."* By the time this buyer is reachable, they already know the project exists, who is building it, and — critically — **who else has bought.**

```
Advocacy (someone else's) → Discovery (verification) → Consultation → Site Visit → Negotiation → Booking → Referral
```

**Three operational consequences:**

1. **Discovery is not a discovery stage for them — it is a verification stage.** They will search the developer's name and read page one. Phase 4 found that page is thin. For this journey, fixing search visibility is not lead generation; it is **loss prevention on demand that already exists.**
2. **The entry point is a person, not a campaign.** Which is why Phase 6 specified Community D's first tranche should be **seeded rather than advertised**, and why §1.2 argues the advocacy layer must be working before the first booking.
3. **The CA and the family advocate are in the journey and are addressed by nobody.** Phase 5 flagged them as gatekeepers no Phase 3 competitor engages. A stage-7 consultation designed to include them is a cheap, uncontested advantage.

### 3.4 Journey 3 — Window

**Who:** S3 US diaspora, S7 Hyderabad-resident Telugu households, S8 Gulf remittance households.
**Where they buy:** B and D primarily — **30.7% of revenue.**

**The stages do not compress evenly — they stretch, then collapse.**

| Stages | Duration | Character |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Awareness → Consideration | **6–18 months** | Slow, self-directed, digital, at odd hours. Very low form-fill rates |
| Lead → Consultation | Weeks | Often initiated by the *relative in India*, not the buyer |
| Site Visit → Booking | **5 days** | Compressed entirely into a travel window |

**Phase 5, on S3:** *"if the site is not ready to close in that window, the sale is lost for a year."*

**Three operational consequences:**

1. **The buying windows are December–January and summer**, set by the diaspora travel calendar, not the Indian festive quarter. **A launch calendar built on Dasara–Diwali misses ₹155 crore of demand.**
2. **The site visit may be performed by a proxy.** Phase 5 found the inspecting relative holds effective veto and *"is almost never the person the marketing is aimed at."* Stage 8 needs a **proxy-visit protocol** — a named host, a structured walkthrough, a report the buyer receives — and Stage 7 needs to work over video as the primary interaction.
3. **This is where Phase 7's worst funnel stage sits.** Community B's qualified-lead-to-visit rate of 18.2% is 30% below the project average, and Phase 7 §5 established B consumes **65% of the lead-generation budget for 22% of units.** The constraint is not lead cost — **it is that this buyer physically cannot visit.** Fixing Stage 8 for this journey is worth more than any media optimisation in the plan.

### 3.5 Journey 4 — Document

**Who:** S4 land-pooling landowners across the 29 capital-area villages.
**Where they enter:** Community D, and as a **land supplier as well as a buyer.**

**This journey does not use the media funnel at all.** Phase 5: *"does not respond to advertising; it responds to a document"* — and *"does not need to visit: they live there."*

```
Village relationship → Term sheet → Family consultation → Precedent (a neighbour completing) → Exchange
```

**Three operational consequences:**

1. **Stage 8 is meaningless and Stage 12 is everything.** They can see the site from their land. What they need is **proof that someone they know was treated as promised** — Phase 5: *"the first neighbour to complete a transaction becomes the single most persuasive asset available."*
2. **It needs a function, not a campaign** — a land-owner relations role working in Telugu, in the villages, with the local advocate and surveyor. Phase 4 found KMV already publishes a `/land-owners` page; Phase 3 found **no competitor in the screened set offers a structured land-for-home proposition at all.**
3. **12 units and ₹27.9 crore understates it.** Phase 5 §5.4: this segment *"supplies land, absorbs inventory and creates local advocacy at the same time."* It is a supply-side and demand-side segment simultaneously, and its 6–24 month cycle means **it must start in year one to deliver in year three.**

## 4. Measurement architecture

### 4.1 Four gates are counted. Eight stages are estimated.

| Stage | Type | What actually records it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Awareness | **Estimated** | Quarterly recall survey · search-volume share |
| 2 Discovery | **Estimated** | Branded search volume · organic visibility · GBP views |
| 3 Interest | **Estimated** | Engaged sessions · video 50% completion · brochure downloads |
| 4 Consideration | **Estimated** | Return-visit cohorts · calculator completions · first-touch-to-enquiry lag |
| **5 Lead** | **COUNTED** | **CRM record created** |
| **6 Qualified Lead** | **COUNTED** | **CRM stage change after qualification call** |
| 7 Consultation | **Estimated** *(recommend promoting to counted)* | CRM activity log — **not a gate in the Phase 7 model** |
| **8 Site Visit** | **COUNTED** | **Attendance logged, with party size and segment** |
| 9 Negotiation | **Estimated** *(recommend promoting to counted)* | CRM stage with quoted vs final price |
| **10 Booking** | **COUNTED** | **Token received** |
| 11 Referral | **Estimated** | Referrer field on the booking record |
| 12 Advocacy | **Estimated** | Review volume · NPS · first-touch self-report |

> **Two recommendations follow directly.** Promote **Consultation** and **Negotiation** to counted gates. Both are CRM stage changes that cost nothing to record, both sit inside conversions Phase 7 measures only in aggregate, and **both are where the sales team's own performance is currently invisible.**
>
> That would give **six counted gates instead of four** — and would let the visit-to-booking rate, the model's most sensitive variable, be decomposed into its two halves rather than watched as a single number.

### 4.2 What an estimated stage may and may not be used for

**May:** diagnose direction, compare periods, compare communities, trigger investigation.
**May not:** justify budget on its own, or appear in a performance report as if it were a conversion.

**Reasoned.** The failure mode is specific and common: a brand campaign reports reach and video completions, those numbers rise, and the layer is judged successful without any evidence it moved a booking. **The brand layer's ₹6.30 crore should be justified by recall and search share against the competitor set — measures of the thing it is actually accountable for (§1) — and by a holdout test, not by impressions.**

### 4.3 The attribution problem, and the one-question fix

**This is the most consequential measurement decision in the plan.**

The consideration cycle runs **3 to 18 months** (Phase 5), and **47% of bookings arrive through channel partners and referral** (Phase 7 §2.3) — routes that leave no click trail at all.

> **Under last-click attribution, the brand layer will appear to produce nothing, the referral layer will appear to produce nothing, and the last paid touch before booking will appear to produce everything.** Budget follows the report. Within two quarters that logic defunds the two layers Phase 7 identified as delivering the cheapest revenue in the project.

**The fix is not a better attribution model.** With this cycle length and this channel mix, no click-based model can be made honest.

**The fix is one question, asked at booking:** *"How did you first hear about us?"* — asked by the relationship manager, recorded as a required CRM field, with a fixed option list.

| | Attribution model | First-touch self-report at booking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Captures referral | No | **Yes** |
| Captures a hoarding seen 14 months ago | No | **Yes** |
| Captures the peer who mentioned it | No | **Yes** |
| Sample size | Every click | **376 responses** |
| Cost | A martech stack | **One field** |

**376 honest answers beat 78,880 attributed clicks**, because the question is asked of the only people whose answer matters. Digital attribution still runs — it optimises campaigns *within* the demand-generation layer, where clicks and conversions are close together. **It does not decide the split between layers.** That is what the booking-stage field is for.

### 4.4 Reporting cadence

| Cadence | Who | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Weekly** | Sales lead | Site visits, visit-to-booking by community and by salesperson, weekend capacity utilisation, **Community B's qualified-to-visit rate separately** |
| **Monthly** | Marketing lead | Leads and CPL by community, lead-to-qualified, consultation and negotiation counts, **the discount register against the two Phase 6 pricing rules** |
| **Quarterly** | Both | First-touch mix from booking records reconciled against the 53 / 28 / 19 source assumption; brand recall survey; NPS; **the layer budget re-based on what the reconciliation shows** |

## 5. The stage-volume model

**Computed** — Phase 7 base case, 45-month window. The four Phase 7 gates are shown in bold; the two proposed additions are derived to reconcile exactly to them.

| Stage | Layer | Total | Per month | Per week | Conversion from prior |
| --- | --- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| **Leads** | Demand gen | **78,880** | 1,753 | 405 | — |
| **Qualified leads** | Demand gen | **12,474** | 277 | 64 | **15.8%** |
| Consultations | Sales conversion | 5,313 | 118 | 27 | 42.6% |
| **Site visits** | Sales conversion | **2,922** | 65 | 15 | 55.0% |
| Negotiations | Sales conversion | 574 | 13 | 3 | 19.6% |
| **Bookings — marketing-sourced** | Sales conversion | **201** | 4.5 | 1.0 | 35.0% |
| + channel partner | Sales conversion | 105 | 2.3 | — | — |
| + referral | **Advocacy** | 71 | 1.6 | — | — |
| **Total bookings** | | **376** | **8.4** | 1.9 | — |

*Reconciliation: consultation 42.6% × consultation-to-visit 55.0% = 23.4%, matching Phase 7's qualified-to-visit gate. Visit-to-negotiation 19.6% × negotiation-to-booking 35.0% = 6.9%, matching Phase 7's visit-to-booking gate.*

### 5.1 The capacity constraint hiding in these numbers

**65 site visits a month is not the hard number — the distribution is.**

Phase 5 established that S1, S5 and S6 all visit **on Sundays**, and that S1 arrives **unannounced with three to six family members**. If two-thirds of monthly visits land on four Sundays, that is roughly **11 visiting parties per Sunday, averaging three to four people each — 35 to 45 people on site in a day.**

> **Reasoned.** The experience centre and show homes are not sized by monthly visits; they are sized by **peak Sunday**. Under-sizing them caps the highest-leverage stage in the plan, and no amount of media spend upstream can push more people through a constrained Sunday. **This should be settled at the design stage of Phase 7's ₹5.00 crore sales-infrastructure line, not discovered in month four.**

## 6. Where the funnel breaks

### 6.1 Volume lost at each stage

| Stage transition | In | Out | Rate | **Lost** |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| Lead → Qualified | 78,880 | 12,474 | 15.8% | **66,406** |
| Qualified → Consultation | 12,474 | 5,313 | 42.6% | 7,161 |
| Consultation → Site visit | 5,313 | 2,922 | 55.0% | 2,391 |
| Site visit → Negotiation | 2,922 | 574 | 19.6% | 2,348 |
| Negotiation → Booking | 574 | 201 | 35.0% | 373 |

The largest absolute loss is at the top. **That is not where the money is.**

### 6.2 What a one-point improvement is worth

**Computed** — one percentage point added at each stage, everything else held constant:

| Improve by one point | Extra bookings | **Extra revenue** |
| --- | --: | --: |
| Lead → Qualified, 15.8% → 16.8% | +12.7 | ₹17.2 Cr |
| Qualified → Site visit, 23.4% → 24.4% | +8.6 | ₹11.6 Cr |
| **Site visit → Booking, 6.9% → 7.9%** | **+29.2** | **₹39.4 Cr** |

> **Established, and it confirms Phase 7 §7 from a different direction.** A point at the site-visit stage is worth **2.3× a point at the lead stage and 3.4× a point at the visit-scheduling stage.**
>
> **The 66,406 leads lost at the top are the least valuable losses in the funnel.** Chasing them with more media, better forms or cheaper CPL is optimising the cheapest stage. **The 2,348 people who visited the site and did not proceed are worth far more** — they have been paid for all the way through, they have stood on the land, and the difference between 6.9% and 7.9% is ₹39.4 crore.

### 6.3 The three specific breakages, named

| # | Break | Where | Why | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **Community B, qualified lead → site visit: 18.2%** — 30% below project average | Journey 3, Stage 8 | The buyer **physically cannot visit**. Phase 5: a proxy relative goes first, the personal visit compresses into one India trip | A **proxy-visit protocol** and a staffed live-walkthrough capability. Phase 7 §5.2: moving this to 25% cuts B's lead requirement by 27% and saves ~₹1.7 crore |
| **2** | **Community D, site visit → booking: 5.6%** — the lowest close rate in the scheme | Journey 2, Stages 8–9 | Largest decision, longest family consultation, and Phase 5's first objection — *"who else has bought?"* — is unanswerable at launch | **Seed the first tranche** (Phase 6). Fifteen named local buyers convert this from a marketing problem into a social fact |
| **3** | **Consultation and Negotiation are unmeasured** | All journeys, Stages 7 and 9 | Both sit inside Phase 7 aggregates. **The sales team's own contribution is currently invisible** | Promote both to counted CRM gates (§4.1). Zero cost, and it decomposes the model's most sensitive variable |

## 7. Risks, and what would falsify this design

| # | Risk | Why it matters | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **The four-journey split is Reasoned, not observed** | It drives the pacing, the calendar and the layer budget. A wrong split misallocates ₹25.68 Cr | The booking-stage first-touch field (§4.3). **Two quarters of bookings settle it** |
| **2** | **The 19% referral assumption may not survive a first project** | Referral requires prior buyers. **In year one there are none** — the assumption is structurally back-loaded | Model referral as ramping from ~0% in year one to above 19% in years three to four, not flat. **Year-one budget must carry more demand generation than the average implies** |
| **3** | **Consultation and negotiation rates are newly derived here** | They are reverse-engineered to reconcile with Phase 7's gates, not observed | They become counted gates on day one. Ninety days of CRM data replaces the derivation |
| **4** | **Sunday capacity** | The highest-leverage stage may be constrained by physical throughput, not demand | Size the experience centre for peak Sunday at launch. A booked-appointment system for S2 and S3 that keeps Sunday free for walk-in families |
| **5** | **Brand spend cannot be defended under last-click** | ₹6.30 Cr is the layer most likely to be cut for looking unproductive | The first-touch field, plus a **geo or time-based holdout** on the brand layer. Agree the measurement method *before* the spend, not when it is challenged |
| **6** | **The two Phase 6 pricing rules erode quietly** | B above ₹1.50 Cr or C below ₹1.65 Cr collapses the gap that stops them competing | The **monthly discount register** (Stage 9). A rule that is not reported is not a rule |
| **7** | **This phase assumes Phase 6's prices hold** | Phase 6 §7 and Phase 7 §8 both flag that **31% of revenue sits in a band nobody has proven is occupied** | Unchanged and unresolved. **No journey design compensates for a price the market refuses** |

## 8. Carry-forward

**Established and Computed.**
- **Four counted gates, eight estimated stages.** Promoting Consultation and Negotiation to counted gates costs nothing and makes the sales team's contribution visible.
- **The linear journey carries 47.5% of units but only 33.4% of revenue.** Two-thirds of the money travels a referral-entry, window or document route that skips, reorders or compresses the twelve stages.
- **Stage volumes:** 78,880 leads → 12,474 qualified → 5,313 consultations → 2,922 site visits → 574 negotiations → 201 marketing-sourced bookings, plus 105 channel partner and 71 referral = **376.**
- **A point at the site-visit stage is worth ₹39.4 crore** — 2.3× a point at the lead stage. The 66,406 leads lost at the top are the least valuable losses in the funnel.
- **Referral costs ₹1.69 lakh per booking against ₹10.52 lakh marketing-sourced — 6.2× cheaper.**

**The four layers, with budget.**

| Layer | Budget | Accountable for |
| --- | --: | --- |
| Brand building | ₹6.30 Cr | The credibility of the price premium |
| Demand generation | ₹9.84 Cr | Lead volume and quality at a cost |
| Sales conversion | ₹8.34 Cr | The 6.9% visit-to-booking rate and price discipline |
| **Customer advocacy** | **₹1.20 Cr** | **71 bookings, ~₹96 Cr, at the lowest cost per booking available** |

**One change to Phase 7.** ₹1.20 crore moves from the brand line into an explicit advocacy budget. **Phase 7 modelled 19% of bookings as referral-sourced and funded that layer at zero** — an omission that would have surfaced as an unexplained shortfall in year two.

**Three design positions carried forward.**
1. **Advocacy is an input to awareness, not only an output of booking.** Its year-one job is manufacturing the first fifteen visible, named local buyers who answer S1's opening question. That is why Community D's first tranche is seeded rather than advertised.
2. **One question at booking beats any attribution model here.** With a 3–18 month cycle and 47% of bookings arriving without a click trail, last-click reporting would defund the two cheapest layers within two quarters.
3. **The diaspora calendar is not the Indian festive calendar.** ₹155.4 crore of demand buys in December–January and summer travel windows. A launch plan paced on Dasara–Diwali misses it.

**Open.** The seven risks in §7 — of which risk 2 (referral cannot exist in year one) changes the **year-one** budget shape, and risk 7 is Phase 6's pricing question, still the largest open item in the whole exercise.

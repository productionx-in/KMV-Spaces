§0

What the creative work has to carry

Nine phases produced a set of facts. Three of them are the whole brief.

Fact 2 · the developer built the hospital
  • KMV Projects built AIIMS Mangalagiri, three kilometres from the site.
  • Phase 5 found delivery anxiety is the one concern shared by all eight segments.
  • It is the only proof point in the exercise that answers it without asking for trust.
Fact 3 · the site visit is where the money is
  • Phase 8: a point of visit-to-booking conversion is worth ₹39.4 crore — 2.3× a point at the lead stage.
  • Phase 9 put ₹4.10 crore on the experience centre, the largest single line in the plan.
The brief that follows
  • The work is not there to make people feel something about a home.
  • It is there to get people onto 25 acres and let the land do the arguing.
The cliché test. Published guidance on Indian real-estate advertising names the failures precisely: superlatives without evidence — "India's most luxurious project" — and general lifestyle statements — "live your dream." The working test for a line is that it should be hard to swap onto a competitor without losing meaning. That is the test this phase is built to pass.
§1

The master idea

Every developer in this corridor sells how much they built. This one sells how much it didn't.

Master brand idea

The most valuable thing on these twenty-five acres is what isn't on them.

Three properties make it usable rather than merely clever. It is true and computable — 376 homes where 874 would fit; a buyer can verify it from a public FAR calculation or by standing at the gate and counting. It is unownable by anyone else — every competitor in the Phase 3 set is denser, so attacking the claim means stating their own number, which is the argument this brand wants them to have. And it generates work across four communities and eight segments, because arithmetic scales.

Fifteen to the acre.

Everything else is open.

ఎకరాకు పదిహేను · Ekaraaku padihenu

Why a number, and not a warmer line

It cannot be exaggerated, cannot be matched, and cannot be swapped onto a competitor. It states the product's defining fact in four words. And it is strange enough for real estate to be remembered — the category does not use numbers as headlines, which is precisely why one works.

The second line does the emotional work the first refuses to do. Everything else is open is literally true — 25 acres minus 376 building footprints — and it is what the buyer is actually purchasing.

What the idea is not
  • Not minimalism — a design style, and it would read as cold to S1's trade families.
  • Not exclusivity — Phase 5 established ₹2.74 Cr is an upper-affluent ticket, not a high-net-worth one. The word would promise a tier the price sheet does not deliver.
  • Not sustainability — the open space is a commercial choice, not an environmental one. Dressing it as green is exactly the unevidenced claim the cliché test rules out.
What it is
  • Restraint, presented as arithmetic.
  • A price premium reframed: not a markup, but the visible cost of 498 homes that were not built.
  • An invitation to check rather than a request to believe.

Why verification, and not aspiration

Phase 5 found the buyers who carry 53% of revenue — S1 trade families, S2 doctors, S3 diaspora — are all, in different ways, verifiers. S2 "will check RERA; most of the catchment will not." S3's stated primary concern is fraud. S1's opening question is "who else has bought?"

Aspirational creative asks all three of them to do the one thing they are least willing to do: take a developer's word for it.

The brand narrative

Used at the experience centre, on the About page, as the launch film's script, and as the internal north star.

Twenty-five acres on the Mangalagiri corridor.

The rules would have let us put eight hundred and seventy-four homes here. The developer down the road managed a hundred and thirteen to the acre, and nobody stopped him.

We built three hundred and seventy-six.

That is fifteen homes to an acre. It means two thousand nine hundred square feet of this land belongs to each home — nearly eight times what you get in the tallest tower on this road. It means the distance between your window and the next one is a distance, not a gap. It means the children's play area is a field. It means your car is parked where you left it, not behind three others.

It also means four hundred and ninety-eight homes that could have been sold, were not.

We know what that cost. It is the reason this address is priced where it is. Not a markup — the visible price of what we left out.

The people who built this are the people who built AIIMS Mangalagiri, three kilometres from here. You can drive past it on the way in. We would rather you did.

Come and count.

Why this narrative works on this audience. It is written the way this catchment actually talks about property: in numbers, land measures and named landmarks. It contains no adjective that cannot be checked. And its closing instruction is an invitation to the site visit — the stage worth ₹39.4 crore a point.

A · The Apartments S5 government · ₹59–90 L

Community line

Twenty-six to the acre.

The lowest number on this road.

The narrative. The corridor builds apartments at fifty-three to a hundred and thirteen homes an acre. This is twenty-six. It is the least expensive address on these twenty-five acres, and it is on the same twenty-five acres. The clubhouse you can walk to is the same clubhouse. The open space is the same open space. You are not buying the cheap corner of a good place — you are buying into it, at the point where it starts.

The emotional truth being served. Phase 5, S5: ending a long rental period, and respectability of address. The insight is that this buyer's fear is not price — it is being the poor relation inside a premium scheme. The narrative's whole job is to say the shared spine is genuinely shared.

Trigger creative. The pay-revision arrears window is this segment's purchase trigger. A distinct flight: "Your arrears are the down payment. Here is the arithmetic." — with the actual working shown.

B · The Managed Residences S3 US diaspora · ₹1.23–1.45 Cr

Community line

Built to be empty eleven months a year.

That is not a flaw. It is the specification.

The narrative. Most homes punish you for leaving. Dust, damp, a garden that dies, a lock nobody checks. This one is built the other way round — single level, lift-served, managed maintenance on a schedule, security designed for homes with nobody in them, and a rental desk if you want one. You can buy it from New Jersey under a registered Power of Attorney without boarding a plane. You can hand the keys to your parents in the same week. And when you come in December, it will be exactly as you left it.

The emotional truth. Phase 5, S3: guilt and duty toward parents, and the stated primary concern, fraud. The narrative answers the second with process, not reassurance: PoA, RERA, published documents, a named person.

And the objection it must not pretend away. "Why Amaravati and not Hyderabad?" The competitor is a city, not a project. The honest answer is roots and price, and it should be said plainly rather than dodged.

C · The Compact Villas S1 trade families · S2 medical · ₹1.69–1.95 Cr

Community line

Ten to the acre.

The villa down the road is twelve.

The narrative. There is a good villa community ten minutes from here, on ten acres, at roughly twelve homes an acre, for about four hundred rupees a foot less. It is a real choice and you should look at it. Then look at what twenty-five acres buys that ten cannot: a central open space large enough to be a place rather than a gap, a clubhouse that serves three hundred and seventy-six homes instead of a hundred and nineteen, a retail row, a school site. You are not paying twenty per cent more for a villa. You are paying it for what surrounds the villa.

Why naming the competitor works here

Phase 5 found S1's information source is peers and brokers who will name Prime Grandeur in the conversation anyway. A brand that pretends the alternative does not exist loses credibility with the exact segment whose first instinct is to verify.

Naming it, fairly, and then answering it is the stronger position — and it is only available to the project that wins the comparison on masterplan.

D · The Estate Villas S1 trade families · ₹2.30–2.74 Cr

Community line

Seven to the acre.

Six thousand two hundred square feet of this land is yours.

The narrative. The house is about six hundred square feet bigger than the one in Community C. The ground under it is a hundred and twenty-five square yards bigger. Two-thirds of what you are paying is land, not construction — which is the calculation you were going to do anyway. Sixty-three homes on nine acres. There is no phase two on this land, because there is no more land.

This is the sharpest audience-message fit in the document. Phase 5 found S1's default alternative is "why not just buy land?" — Community D answers that objection inside the project rather than losing to it outside, and it does so by speaking the segment's own unit of account. A segment that thinks in square yards is being sold square yards, in the one form that also comes with a house on it.

The launch rule that overrides all creative

Phase 6: D's first tranche is seeded, not advertised. Phase 5: S1 "does not buy a project; it buys the other buyers."

No paid media runs against Community D until fifteen named local households have bought. The creative for D in year one is not a campaign — it is a list of names.

The rule that keeps it honest

Every claim must survive the question "what is the number?"

If a line cannot answer it, the line does not run. Spacious fails. 2,904 square feet of site per home passes. Trusted builder fails. Built AIIMS Mangalagiri passes. Prime location fails. Twelve minutes to AIIMS, timed at 8am on a Tuesday passes.

This is a production rule, not a philosophy. It sits at the top of every creative brief and every agency scope.

§5

Visual direction

One decision determines everything else.

The decision

The hero image is a plan view, not a facade.

Density is only legible from above. A photograph of a building elevation tells a buyer nothing about how many other buildings there are — which is the entire proposition.

A vertical aerial of 25 acres, with 376 roofs visible against everything that is not a roof, is the single most persuasive image this project can produce — and no competitor on 5–11 acres can shoot its equivalent.

Palette — drawn from the site, not a mood board

Black cotton soilThe Guntur belt's ground, before anything is built
Krishna siltThe river four kilometres north
November greenThis district after the monsoon — not CGI emerald
Granite greyThe Mangalagiri hill temple stone
Burnt ochreMangalagiri handloom. Used only on the numbers

Why a district palette. It reads as belonging here — which matters for segments (S1, S4, S5) whose scepticism is partly about outsiders arriving to sell them their own land back. And it rules out the category default of navy-and-gold, which signals a luxury tier the price sheet does not support.

Typography

The numbers are the typography. Set large, set alone, in a face with genuine authority in figures. Body copy in a Telugu-and-Latin pair that sets both scripts at equal weight — not Telugu as a translated afterthought at 80% size, which is what most of the corridor's advertising does and which reads exactly as it is.

Production rule: the number is never smaller than the words around it.

Photography

Do
  • Photograph the space between buildings — that is the product
  • Shoot in real seasons, including a hot April and a wet August
  • Real families from Vijayawada, Guntur and Mangalagiri, cast locally
  • Wide frames that include ground and sky
  • Show the scheme in use — untidy, mid-life, with people in it
  • Photograph the AIIMS building, factually
Do not
  • Photograph the buildings as objects
  • Shoot one permanent golden hour
  • Aspirational-looking models, or families of exactly four
  • Tight lifestyle vignettes that could be anywhere
  • Empty perfection
  • Imply an association that isn't stated

Phase 5 found S1 visits with three to six family members and S6 with children — the audience is families in motion, and the imagery should look like the site on a Sunday, because that is when they will see it. Empty, perfect, golden-hour photography sets up a disappointment the site visit then has to survive.

CGI — three rules

Unavoidable pre-construction, and the place where the category's credibility collapses fastest.

1 · Every CGI carries a visible label and a date. Artist's impression, March 2027. Trees shown at eight years.

2 · Skies are photographed from the site, not generated.

3 · Every CGI has a real-photograph counterpart published beside it as construction proceeds — same frame, same angle, month by month. The comparison becomes pillar 2's proof asset, and the discipline of knowing the real photograph is coming keeps the CGI honest.

Digital and performance systems

Four communities × two languages × six formats is 48 permutations before a single message changes. A system is the only way to produce that without it degrading.

The constant across every asset: the number, top-left, at a fixed size relative to the frame. It is the brand's recognition device, the way a logo usually is.

The website is the verification surface. Its structure follows pillar 5: the price sheet is a page, not a download form. The RERA file is a page. The construction feed is a page. §10

Site experience and collateral

₹4.10 crore serving the stage worth ₹39.4 crore a point. This is where creative strategy stops being communication and becomes staging.

1 · The drive in
  • The approach road is the first execution. A single marker at the turn-off with the drive time to AIIMS on it.
  • The host times the drive with the visitor on the way in — S2 wants it measured.
2 · The two models
  • Two physical masterplan models, same scale, side by side. This scheme at 15 to the acre; a corridor-typical block at 113.
  • No commentary needed — the models are the argument.
  • The cheapest high-impact element in the whole ₹4.10 crore line, and one a competitor cannot answer without building a model that defeats them.
3 · The counted walk
  • A marked route with numbered posts. A visitor can walk it and count the homes.
  • The brand's central claim is the only one in the corridor that survives being checked on foot — so the site invites exactly that.
4 · The show homes
  • One in C, one in D — the two communities carrying 53% of revenue.
  • Furnished, lived-in, not staged empty. A Telugu-speaking host, always.
5 · The wall
  • A permanent wall listing every household that has bought, by name, with permission.
  • S1's first question is "who else has bought?" — the wall answers it before it is asked, and costs almost nothing.
The capacity rule
  • S1, S5 and S6 all visit on Sundays; S1 arrives unannounced with 3–6 people. Roughly 35–45 people on a peak Sunday.
  • Size for peak Sunday, not the monthly average, and route S2 and S3 to weekday evenings and video to keep Sunday free for families who arrive without warning.

The cliché register

The brief said to avoid generic real-estate clichés unless research proves they work. Both halves, answered.

Cut — eight devices, each with the finding that rules it out

DeviceWhy it is cut

Kept — four devices the research supports

DeviceThe evidence

The distinction running through the whole register

A cliché is not a device that is common. It is a device used without evidence.

Vaastu, scarcity, family imagery and print are all category conventions — and all four have specific support in this catchment's research. Superlatives, lifestyle abstraction and amenity grids do not, in this market, for these buyers.

Carry-forward · the idea

The most valuable thing on these twenty-five acres is what isn't on them.

Why it holds. 376 homes where 874 would fit at the corridor's own lowest density. 2,904 sq ft of site per home against 384 at Manjeera Monarch — 7.6×. ₹344–500 crore of gross development value deliberately forgone. Every figure computable from public data, and no competitor in the corridor can say the line without stating a number that defeats them.

Four decisions a conventional plan would not make
  • The hero image is a plan view, not a facade. Density is only legible from above, and it is the entire proposition.
  • Community D runs no paid media in year one. Its creative is a list of fifteen names.
  • The price sheet is published ungated, with a comparison against three named competitors — including where this project loses on rate.
  • The campaign gets quieter and more evidential over time, because year three has a built place and real neighbours.
The system
  • Four communities, four numbers — 26 · 24 · 10 · 7, averaging the master 15.
  • It was not invented for the campaign; it fell out of Phase 6's land model.
  • Five pillars: The Number · The Builder · The Open · The Neighbours · The Document.
The production rule
  • Every claim must survive the question "what is the number?"
  • If a line cannot answer it, the line does not run.
  • It sits at the top of every creative brief and every agency scope.

§0

How each channel was judged

Three questions, all answerable from Phases 5–8. A channel list where everything is important is not a strategy.

1 · Whose reach is it, uniquely?
  • Phase 5 established eight segments with almost non-overlapping media behaviour.
  • A channel that only duplicates reach is a cost, not a channel.
2 · Which stage does it move?
  • Phase 8: a point at the site-visit stage is worth ₹39.4 crore; a point at the lead stage, ₹17.2 crore.
  • Volume at the cheapest stage is worth least.
3 · Can its contribution be established?
  • With a 3–18 month cycle and 47% of bookings arriving without a click trail, most channel-level attribution here is fiction.
  • Channels are judged on the job they do, not the leads they claim.

The budget

₹25.68 crore allocated across channels, summing exactly to the Phase 8 layer totals.

What this allocation is

Phase 7's sensitivity analysis said conversion beats media price. Phase 8 said a point at the site-visit stage is worth 2.3× a point at the lead stage.

This budget is what those two findings look like when they are actually acted on — rather than agreed with and then ignored at allocation time, which is the usual outcome.

The channels

Role, audience, funnel stage, expected contribution, KPI, measurement, strength and weakness — for each of the seventeen funded channels. The five cut or reclassified follow at the end.

Tier 1

Load-bearing

The plan fails without these · ₹9.34 Cr

Experiential / site marketing₹4.10 Cr

Tier 1
Role
Make the density visible. Phase 6's entire price premium rests on 15 homes per acre against a corridor norm of 53–113, and Phase 2 called that “a difference visible from the site gate.” The experience centre and show homes are the only place that argument can be made.
Audience
All eight segments — but the visit patterns diverge completely. S1 unannounced Sunday, 3–6 family. S2 by appointment, couple only, weekday evening. S5 / S6 Sunday with children, comparing two or three projects. S3 proxy relative first, then one compressed trip.
Funnel stage
Site Visit (8), carrying Consultation (7) and Negotiation (9)
Contribution
Not a lead source — it converts. Every one of the 376 bookings passes through it except the S4 exchange route.
KPI
Visit-to-booking 6.9% blended (A 8.1 · B 6.0 · C 7.0 · D 5.6); repeat-visit rate; peak-Sunday capacity utilisation
Measurement
Attendance logged with party size and segment; 24-hour follow-up logged; conversion by community and by salesperson
Strength
The highest-return channel in the plan — ₹39.4 crore per point of conversion. Also the only channel a 5-to-11-acre competitor cannot replicate, because the asset being shown is the land itself.
Weakness
Fixed capital, spent before the first booking, and its capacity is set by peak Sunday, not monthly average — an estimated 35–45 people on site on a busy Sunday. Under-size it and no upstream spend compensates.

Referral marketing₹0.70 Cr

Tier 1
Role
Convert buyers into introducers, and answer S1's first objection structurally. Phase 5: the opening question from trade families is “who else has bought?”
Audience
Every booked buyer, weighted to S1 — who frequently buys as multiple related households — and S4, where “the first neighbour to complete a transaction becomes the single most persuasive asset available.”
Funnel stage
Referral (11) — and, uniquely, it feeds back into Awareness (1) for Journey 2
Contribution
70 bookings — 18.5% of the scheme, ~₹94.5 crore
KPI
Referral-sourced bookings against 70; referrals per booked buyer; cost per referral booking
Measurement
A referrer field on every booking, reconciled monthly against the 18.5% assumption
Strength
₹1.71 lakh per booking against ₹10.52 lakh marketing-sourced — 6.1× cheaper. It also compounds: each cohort of buyers enlarges the next.
Weakness
It cannot exist in year one. Referral requires prior buyers, and at launch there are none.

Channel partners / brokers₹3.34 Cr

Tier 1
Role
Distributed reach into networks the developer cannot address directly — particularly S1's trade circles and the Vijayawada–Guntur broker network Phase 5 identified as one of S1's core information sources.
Audience
Primarily S1 and S6; secondarily S5. Weakest with S3 and S4.
Funnel stage
Lead (5) through Booking (10) — partners run their own mini-funnel
Contribution
105 bookings — 28% of the scheme, ~₹142 crore
KPI
Bookings per active partner; partner-sourced visit-to-booking rate; commission as a share of agreement value against the 2.5% assumption
Measurement
Partner code on every lead and booking; monthly active-partner count; conversion tracked separately from direct
Strength
Pay-on-performance. No working capital, no cost until a unit sells, and it scales without a media plan.
Weakness
The developer does not control the message. A partner selling on discount rather than on density erodes the Phase 6 pricing rules, and partners routinely present competing projects in the same conversation.

WhatsAppinside CRM & demand-gen

Tier 1
Role
The primary conversational channel in this catchment, not a follow-up tool. Phase 5 found it primary for S1, S4 and S8, and the follow-up channel for all eight segments.
Audience
All eight. Decisive for S8 — “mobile-only, Telugu-language, WhatsApp-centric… will not fill a desktop web form” — and for S3, whose form-fill rates are very low but who will respond on WhatsApp.
Funnel stage
Lead (5), Qualified Lead (6), Consultation (7), and post-booking through Referral (11)
Contribution
Not separately attributed. It is the capture and contact layer under Meta, portals and referral alike.
KPI
Response time to inbound; contact-establishment rate versus phone; lead-to-qualified rate for WhatsApp-originated versus form-originated leads
Measurement
WhatsApp Business API integrated into the CRM so conversations are logged as activity, not held on a salesperson's handset
Strength
Highest contact rate of any channel here, works across literacy and language, and it is the only channel that spans a Gulf earner and his wife in Vijayawada in one thread.
Weakness
Ungoverned by default. Conversations live on personal phones, leave no CRM trail, and walk out of the door when a salesperson does. Without API integration it is not a channel — it is a liability.
Tier 2

Necessary at scale

The working majority of reach and capture · ₹9.00 Cr

Meta (incl. Instagram placements)₹3.45 Cr

Tier 2
Role
The volume engine for Communities A and B, and the only affordable way to reach S3 and S7 by geography-plus-affinity.
Audience
S6 and S5 locally; S3 by US metro and origin-district affinity; S7 by Hyderabad geography; S8 in Telugu. Weak against S1, useless against S4.
Funnel stage
Interest (3) through Lead (5), with retargeting into Consideration (4)
Contribution
~68 bookings — the largest single marketing-sourced channel
KPI
CPL by community (A ₹350 · B ₹1,600 · C ₹900 · D ₹1,900); lead-to-qualified rate by campaign, not lead volume; cost per site visit
Measurement
Platform reporting for in-channel optimisation only. Contribution to bookings comes from the CRM source field, not from Meta's attribution window.
Strength
Precision and scale at once — origin-district affinity targeting for S3 is not available anywhere else at this price. Instagram placements carry the villa creative better than any other surface.
Weakness
Lead quality is the worst of the paid channels. A Meta budget judged on CPL will optimise toward exactly the leads Phase 8 showed are worth least.

Google Search₹2.30 Cr

Tier 2
Role
Intent capture. Someone typing “villas in Mangalagiri” or “3BHK Amaravati price” has done the work; the job is to be there and to answer.
Audience
S2 — who “will check RERA; most of the catchment will not” — plus S6, S5, S3 and S7. Also S1 in verification mode, searching the developer's name after a peer mentions it.
Funnel stage
Discovery (2) and Lead (5)
Contribution
~52 bookings, at the best lead quality in the plan
KPI
Cost per qualified lead, not cost per lead; impression share on a fixed ~20-query commercial set; branded versus non-branded split
Measurement
Search Console and Ads, reconciled to CRM qualification outcomes
Strength
The highest-intent traffic available, and the only paid channel that serves Journey 2's verification step. Phase 4 found KMV's search visibility thin — this is where that gap costs money directly.
Weakness
Volume is capped by demand. No budget creates more people searching “villas in Mangalagiri.” It saturates, and beyond that point the money is better spent elsewhere.

Website + landing pages₹0.60 Cr

Tier 2
Role
The verification surface. For Journey 2 — 24% of units and 30% of revenue — this is not a discovery tool. The buyer already knows about the project; the site decides whether they believe it.
Audience
All digitally-reachable segments. Most consequential for S2 and S3, who research in depth and at length.
Funnel stage
Discovery (2), Interest (3), Consideration (4), Lead (5)
Contribution
Not separately attributed. ~180 of the 201 marketing-sourced bookings pass through it.
KPI
Landing-page conversion by community; time on the price-sheet page; bounce rate on the four community pages separately
Measurement
GA4 with community as a content dimension; UTM discipline enforced by community
Strength
The one surface fully under the developer's control, and the cheapest place to publish what competitors will not — price per square foot, a unit-wise price sheet, the possession date, the RERA number.
Weakness
It converts nobody on its own. Over-investing in the site while under-investing in the reasons to visit it is a common and expensive error.

Outdoor / hoardings₹2.10 Cr

Tier 2
Role
Local legitimacy at the scale a 25-acre scheme needs. Phase 5 found S1 and S5 read a sustained hoarding presence as evidence of substance — a developer who can hold NH-16 for two years is a developer who will finish.
Audience
S1, S5, S6 on the Vijayawada–Guntur corridor; secondarily anyone driving to AIIMS, the Secretariat or the university cluster.
Funnel stage
Awareness (1), and directional traffic to Site Visit (8)
Contribution
Not attributable to bookings, and should not be asked to be. Its contribution shows up in the booking-stage first-touch field and in recall.
KPI
Prompted and unprompted recall in the catchment; share of category search; first-touch mentions at booking
Measurement
Quarterly recall survey, n ≈ 400. A geo or time-based holdout is the only honest way to test it.
Strength
Unmissable in a corridor where the target audience drives the same road daily. And it does what no digital channel does: it is seen by the buyer's peers — which matters for a segment whose first question is who else has bought.
Weakness
Expensive, committed long, genuinely hard to measure. The line most likely to be cut under last-click reporting, and the wrong cut to make.

CRM, martech, telephony (infrastructure)₹0.55 Cr

Tier 2
Role
Owns the lead-to-qualified rate. Not a channel — it reaches nobody — but Phase 8 values a single point at this stage at ₹17.2 crore.
Audience
Internal. Sales team, channel partners, and the relationship managers who own post-booking advocacy.
Funnel stage
Every stage from Lead (5) to Advocacy (12)
Contribution
Indirect and large. A published benchmark holds that developers without CRM automation lose 30–50% of qualified leads to follow-up gaps.
KPI
Speed to first contact; contact-establishment rate; stage-transition completeness — the share of bookings carrying a source, a referrer and a first-touch value
Measurement
The CRM measures itself. The audit that matters is field completeness, not report volume.
Strength
It is the only place the counted gates exist, and the only place the booking-stage first-touch field can live. Everything Phase 8 recommends depends on it.
Weakness
It fails quietly. A CRM with 40% of source fields blank produces reports that look fine and decide budget wrongly.
Tier 3

Necessary, but small

Each does one specific job for one or two segments · ₹3.34 Cr

YouTube₹1.30 Cr brand + ₹1.10 Cr demand

Tier 3
Role
Long-form proof for buyers who cannot visit, and the highest-consumption format in Telugu media.
Audience
S3 and S7 — “will watch a six-minute walkthrough” and “will do a video site walkthrough before a physical one.” Also S4 and S8 in Telugu, and S1 for long-form.
Funnel stage
Awareness (1), Interest (3) — and, uniquely, a partial substitute for Site Visit (8)
Contribution
~18 bookings directly, plus a disproportionate assist into Community B where the physical visit is the binding constraint
KPI
50% and 75% video completion; cost per completed view; for B, the share of qualified leads that watch a full walkthrough before a visit
Measurement
YouTube analytics for engagement; CRM for the B-specific correlation
Strength
The only channel that can partially do the site visit's job remotely — the single most valuable fix available in the plan.
Weakness
Production-heavy and slow to change. A walkthrough shot before construction ages badly, and re-shooting is a real cost, not a re-upload.

SEO₹0.40 Cr

Tier 3
Role
Loss prevention on demand that already exists. For Journey 2 the buyer arrives at search having already been referred; if the results are thin, a live prospect is lost silently.
Audience
S2, S3, S7, and S1 in verification mode.
Funnel stage
Discovery (2)
Contribution
~12 bookings at low marginal cost — but back-loaded, since organic authority accrues over quarters
KPI
Branded search volume; visibility on the ~20-query commercial set; Google Business Profile views and direction requests
Measurement
Search Console and rank tracking, baselined against the Phase 4 audit so improvement is provable
Strength
Compounds, costs nothing per click, and Phase 4 established the baseline is weak — which makes the gains available unusually large for a small budget.
Weakness
Slow. Nothing here helps in the first two quarters, which is exactly when the launch needs volume. It cannot be accelerated with money.

Print₹1.20 Cr

Tier 3
Role
Legitimacy, not response. Phase 5 found print retains real credibility with S5 and S1 — a full page in Eenadu is read as evidence of scale.
Audience
S5, S1, S6, and the older decision-influencers Phase 5 identified inside S1 households — the father or eldest brother.
Funnel stage
Awareness (1), with a launch-moment role
Contribution
Not attributable, and should not be bought on response. Its job is to make the project unmissable at launch and credible thereafter.
KPI
Recall; first-touch mentions at booking; not coupon codes or unique numbers, which measure the wrong thing here
Measurement
Recall survey. Response-tracked print measures the small fraction who respond immediately, not the majority who register the project and act later.
Strength
Reaches the household influencers no digital channel reaches, and confers a specific kind of local seriousness.
Weakness
Expensive per contact and genuinely unmeasurable. With outdoor it is ₹3.30 crore that cannot be defended under click-based reporting.

PR₹0.45 Cr

Tier 3
Role
Build the search results that Journey 2 verifies against. Its job is Discovery, not Awareness — a referred buyer who searches the name should find third-party coverage, not silence.
Audience
S1 and S2 in verification mode; S3 and S7 researching from a distance; local institutional and government audiences.
Funnel stage
Discovery (2), with a secondary Awareness role at launch
Contribution
Not attributable. Measured by what it puts on page one, not by leads.
KPI
Third-party coverage on page one for the developer and project names; publication quality; the AIIMS credential appearing in coverage the developer did not write
Measurement
Quarterly search-results audit; coverage log with publication tier
Strength
Cheapest way to fix a thin search footprint with third-party credibility — worth more than owned content to a verifying buyer.
Weakness
Not controllable and not schedulable. A PR budget judged on volume of coverage will buy the wrong coverage.

Email + marketing automation₹0.19 Cr

Tier 3
Role
Nurture and document delivery — for two segments only. Not an acquisition channel here.
Audience
S2, who “responds to email; often cannot take calls during the day”, and S3, reading across time zones. Effectively useless for S1, S4, S5 and S8.
Funnel stage
Consideration (4) and Consultation (7)
Contribution
No independent booking attribution. It shortens the consideration cycle for two segments.
KPI
Open and click rate for S2 and S3 cohorts separately; document-open rate on price sheets and the FEMA / PoA explainer

Almost free, asynchronous — the whole point for a buyer 10½ hours away — and the right medium for the long documents S3 needs before a remote purchase.

LinkedIn₹0.50 Cr · capped, S3 and S7 only

Tier 4
Role
The only precise way to reach US-based Telugu technology professionals by employer, seniority, education and geography.
Audience

Split verdict. On-site owner and milestone events are advocacy infrastructure. Off-site lead-generation events — diaspora roadshows, hotel expos — are Tier 4 and should not run in year one.

Read naively, this table says the wrong thing

It says: cut outdoor, print, PR, the website and the experience centre — they produce nothing — and move ₹10.25 crore into portals and referral, which look cheapest.

Every part of that conclusion is wrong. Portals look cheap because their leads are cheap, not because their buyers are; their leads carry the worst qualification rate in the plan. Referral looks cheap because 70 buyers were persuaded by a product, a site visit and a delivery record that cost ₹10.25 crore to build — costs that appear in the unattributable rows.

The experience centre "produces nothing" while carrying ₹39.4 crore per point of conversion.

Rule 1 · compare within a layer
  • Meta against Search against portals is fair — same layer, same job.
  • Meta against outdoor is not.
Rule 2 · judge demand gen on cost per qualified lead
  • Not cost per booking — that imports last-click distortion.
  • Not cost per lead — that rewards the volume Phase 8 showed is worth least.
Rule 3 · brand and infrastructure need a different measure
  • Judged by the booking-stage first-touch field and by holdout tests.
  • Never by a cost-per-booking figure they cannot produce.

The mix that follows

Journey 1 is a conventional paid-media problem and takes the majority of Meta, Search and portal budget. Journey 2, at 30.3% of revenue, takes almost no media at all — its budget is channel-partner commission, the referral programme and the experience centre. Journey 3, at 30.7%, is where the December–January concentration and the video-consultation capability live. Journey 4 has no media line; it has a headcount line.

Two of the four journeys — 36% of revenue — are served primarily by things that are not media buys. A channel strategy allocating only across media would leave them unresourced.

The year-one recommendation

Over-weight channel partners, not media.

A channel-partner network can be stood up in weeks and costs nothing until it sells. Buying 19 additional bookings through paid media costs ₹1.41 crore more for the same units.

And the advocacy budget's year-one job changes entirely. In year one, ₹1.20 crore of advocacy spend is not harvesting referrals — there is nothing to harvest. It is manufacturing the first fifteen visible, named local buyers whose existence answers S1's opening question and unlocks Journey 2 in year two. Phase 6 said the same thing in product terms: seed Community D's first tranche rather than advertise it.

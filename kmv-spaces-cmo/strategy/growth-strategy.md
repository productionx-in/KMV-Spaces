# Growth Strategy & Reverse-Funnel Model

## 1. How to plan a real-estate funnel: backwards

Most developer marketing starts with "what's the budget?" That produces activity, not bookings. The correct sequence starts with the inventory that must move and works backwards to media spend.

```
BOOKING TARGET  (set by sales/finance — inventory to move)
      ▼  ÷ Site visit → booking rate
SITE VISITS REQUIRED
      ▼  ÷ Site visit show rate
SITE VISITS TO SCHEDULE
      ▼  ÷ Qualified lead → site visit rate
QUALIFIED LEADS REQUIRED
      ▼  ÷ Lead qualification rate
TOTAL LEADS REQUIRED
      ▼  ÷ Landing page conversion rate
TRAFFIC REQUIRED
      ▼  × CPC
MEDIA SPEND REQUIRED
```

## 3. What the scenarios actually prove

**[INFERENCE] The spread between Conservative and Aggressive is ₹32 Cr vs ₹1.1 Cr of media spend — a 29× difference — for identical revenue.**

Not one rupee of that difference comes from better media buying. It comes entirely from:
- Lead qualification (15% → 35%)
- Site-visit conversion (25% → 45%)
- Show rate (70% → 85%)
- Sales closing (10% → 20%)
- Landing page conversion (2.5% → 5%)

**This is the single most important slide in the entire deck.** It reframes the marketing budget conversation from "how much should we spend?" to "how good is our funnel?" — and it demonstrates that in premium real estate, **funnel quality is the budget lever**, not a soft metric.

**[INFERENCE] Practical implication:** the first 90 days should be spent almost entirely on funnel quality — qualification criteria, speed-to-lead, landing pages, sales process, site-visit experience — because every point of improvement there reduces required media spend geometrically. Scaling spend on a Conservative-grade funnel is how developers burn money.

| 100 | ₹300 Cr | 9,524 | 667 | ₹8.2 Cr | 2.7% |

**[INFERENCE] The sales-capacity constraint.** At 60 bookings, the funnel needs **400 completed site visits a year — roughly 33 a month**. At 100 bookings it needs 56 a month. Whether KMV's sales team can host, and *convert well at*, that volume is *Not publicly available* and is a **hard ceiling on the plan**. Generating demand beyond sales capacity degrades response times, damages conversion, and wastes spend. **Marketing must be sized to sales capacity.**

## 6. The growth thesis in one paragraph

KMV does not have a demand problem it can solve by spending more. It has a **conversion and credibility problem** — genuinely rare product substance that almost nobody knows about, spread across fragmented channels, feeding a funnel of unknown quality. The fastest path to revenue is therefore not more leads: it is (1) consolidate the brand so equity compounds, (2) fix the funnel so each lead is worth several times more, (3) harvest the existing customer base that already trusts the company, and only then (4) scale paid acquisition against a funnel that can convert it. Executed in that order, ₹180 Cr of bookings needs roughly ₹1–3 Cr of media, not ₹32 Cr.

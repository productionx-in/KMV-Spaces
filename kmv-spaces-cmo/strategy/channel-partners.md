# Channel Partner Strategy

## 1. Why this matters more than it looks

**[OBSERVED]** KMV operates a **Business Partners** page offering commission on successful referrals.

**[OBSERVED]** A third-party broker (Prakruth Properties) published TRAYA's pre-launch information — location, product mix, March 2026 booking target — **before KMV's own channels visibly did**, listing its own phone number as the contact.

**[INFERENCE]** This is the finding that should concern leadership most in the entire audit. It means:
- Brokers are actively marketing KMV inventory **without KMV controlling the narrative**
- Leads generated by that activity land with the **broker**, not KMV — first-party data lost
- Pricing and positioning are being communicated by parties with **no accountability for brand consistency**
- KMV is paying full commission for demand it partly generated itself through the underlying product's reputation

The channel exists and is influential. It is simply not being managed. **[INFERENCE]** In Indian residential real estate, channel partners commonly drive a substantial share of premium bookings — often 30–50%, though KMV's actual share is *Not publicly available*. A channel of that magnitude operating without structure is the largest unmanaged commercial relationship in the company.

## 2. Segmentation

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Not all partners deserve the same treatment.

| Tier | Profile | Treatment |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1 — Strategic** (5–10) | Consistent premium closers, HNI networks, proven KMV performance | Highest commission, exclusive pre-launch inventory access, co-marketing budget, direct sales-head line, quarterly business reviews |
| **Tier 2 — Active** (20–40) | Regular producers, mixed segments | Standard commission + performance ladder, full digital kits, monthly updates, training |
| **Tier 3 — Registered** (long tail) | Occasional, opportunistic | Base commission, self-serve portal and assets, quarterly comms |
| **Specialist — NRI** | Diaspora-facing consultants, US/Gulf | NRI-specific kit, time-zone support, video-walkthrough enablement |
| **Specialist — Hyderabad** | Hyderabad brokers with AP-origin clients | Hyderabad office as hosting venue; targeted collateral |

## 3. Acquisition & onboarding

**Acquisition:** referrals from existing high performers · LinkedIn outreach to established regional consultancies · partner events at completed Vivaan · portal-active broker identification · Hyderabad and NRI-specialist recruitment for out-of-region segments.

**Onboarding — a structured 5-step process, not a phone call:**
1. **Registration & agreement** — commission structure, lead-registration rules, brand-usage terms
2. **Product immersion** — mandatory guided visit to completed Vivaan (the proof asset)
3. **Certification training** — product, pricing, USPs, objection handling, RERA and compliance
4. **Kit issuance** — digital and physical assets (§4)
5. **CRM access** — lead-registration portal login

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Make the **Vivaan site visit mandatory** for every partner. A broker who has walked a finished KMV community sells with conviction; one working from a brochure sells on price. This is the cheapest possible upgrade to channel sales quality.

## 4. The partner kit

**[INFERENCE]** Partners sell whatever is easiest to sell. Whoever gives them the best materials wins their attention — this is a genuine competitive lever against better-known developers.

**Digital:** project one-pagers · floor plans and master plans · price sheets with payment plans · walkthrough and drone videos · WhatsApp-ready image and video packs · **objection-handling scripts** (price, water, delay) · competitor comparison cards · RERA documents · **quantified net-zero savings sheet** · NRI process pack.

**Physical:** printed brochures · site signage co-branding where agreed · sample-unit access.

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Supply WhatsApp-ready assets specifically. Brokers sell on WhatsApp; assets not sized and formatted for it don't get used.

## 5. Lead registration — the governance that makes it work

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Disputes over lead ownership destroy channel relationships faster than commission rates. Rules must be explicit and enforced by system, not memory:

- Partner registers prospect (name + phone) in the portal **before** site visit
- Registration valid for a defined exclusivity window (e.g. 90 days)
- System auto-detects duplicates against existing CRM leads; **first registration wins**
- Direct KMV leads already in CRM are **not** eligible for partner commission
- Disputes resolved by CRM timestamp — not negotiation
- Partner sees their own pipeline status in real time

**[INFERENCE]** The transparency is the product. Partners work hardest for developers where they trust the attribution system. A visible, timestamped, self-serve portal is worth more to a good broker than a marginally higher commission from an opaque one.

## 6. Incentives

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Structure over flat rate. Actual commission percentages must be set against KMV's margin structure (*Not publicly available*), but the architecture should be:

| Mechanism | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Base commission** | Market-competitive floor |
| **Volume slab** | Higher rate above booking thresholds — rewards consistency |
| **Launch bonus** | Elevated rate during TRAYA launch window — drives urgency when it matters |
| **Speed bonus** | Additional % for bookings closed within 45 days of registration |
| **Quality gate** | Full commission released on booking confirmation; clawback on cancellation |
| **Non-cash recognition** | Annual awards, leaderboards, partner trips |

**[INFERENCE]** The **speed bonus** is deliberate: it aligns the channel with sales velocity and inventory turn, not just eventual volume. Cash flow matters as much as total bookings in development.

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Pay promptly and predictably. In this market, payment reliability is a genuine differentiator and travels by word of mouth among brokers faster than any incentive announcement.

## 7. Enablement rhythm

| Activity | Frequency |
|---|---|
| New-project briefing | At each launch, before public announcement |
| Product/objection training refresh | Quarterly |
| Site visit for partners | On launch + quarterly |
| Performance leaderboard circulation | Monthly |
| Tier 1 business review | Quarterly |
| Partner meet / recognition event | Half-yearly |
| Construction progress update | Monthly |

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Brief partners **before** public launch, not after. It creates advocacy, prevents the Prakruth scenario recurring, and gives the channel a reason to prioritise KMV inventory in the critical first weeks.

## 8. KPIs

| KPI | Formula | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Registered partners | Count | Reach |
| **Active partners** | Partners with ≥1 registration in 90 days | **The real number** — registration is vanity |
| Partner activation rate | Active ÷ Registered × 100 | Programme health |
| Leads per active partner | Registrations ÷ Active partners | Productivity |
| Site visits per partner | Visits ÷ Active partners | Quality of registration |
| **Bookings per partner** | Bookings ÷ Active partners | Outcome |
| **Revenue per partner** | Booking value ÷ Active partners | Value concentration |
| Partner lead → booking rate | Bookings ÷ Registrations × 100 | Compare vs direct channel |
| **Cost per booking (channel)** | Commission + enablement ÷ Bookings | **Compare against direct-marketing cost per booking** |
| Time to close | Registration → booking, median | Velocity |
| Partner concentration | % of bookings from top 5 partners | **Dependency risk** |

**[INFERENCE]** Two of these deserve board attention. **Cost per booking (channel) vs direct** settles the recurring "should we spend on marketing or just pay brokers?" argument with arithmetic rather than opinion — and note from `/strategy/marketing-economics.md` §5 that commissions commonly run 2%+ of unit value, so direct marketing at 1.5% is already competitive. **Partner concentration** flags the risk of a business dependent on a handful of brokers who can move their loyalty to a competitor overnight.

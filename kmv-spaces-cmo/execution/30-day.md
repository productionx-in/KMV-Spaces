# Days 1–30 — Audit & Foundation

**Objective:** replace assumption with fact, and fix the things that cost nothing but discipline.

**[RECOMMENDATION] Principle for month one: do not launch campaigns.** Scaling spend into an unmeasured funnel is how developers lose money without learning why. The exception is anything already committed — keep it running, but instrument it.

## Week 1 — Establish the baseline

| # | Action | Owner | Priority | Effort | Impact | KPI | Dependency |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Get the numbers**: bookings, revenue, inventory by project/phase | CMO + Finance | P0 | Low | High | Baseline established | Leadership access |
| 2 | **CRM audit**: does one exist, do all sources write to it, what is speed-to-lead | CMO + Sales | P0 | Med | **Very high** | Median speed-to-lead | CRM access |
| 3 | **Marketing spend audit**: spend by channel, what it produced | CMO + Finance | P0 | Med | High | Cost per booking baseline | Finance |
| 4 | **Sales capacity assessment**: RM headcount, site visits/month possible, conversion by RM | CMO + Sales Head | P0 | Low | **Very high** | Visits/month ceiling | Sales |
| 5 | **Sit with sales** — listen to 10 live calls | CMO | P0 | Low | High | Objection list | Sales |
| 6 | Confirm TRAYA facts: pricing, units, acreage, RERA, launch date | CMO + Projects | P0 | Low | High | Launch brief inputs | Leadership |

**[INFERENCE]** Items 2 and 4 are the two that most often change the entire plan. If speed-to-lead is hours rather than minutes, that is the largest available revenue gain in the company. If sales can only host 15 visits a month, no media budget can deliver 60 bookings and the conversation becomes a hiring conversation.

## Week 3 — Funnel diagnosis

| # | Action | Owner | Priority | Effort | Impact | KPI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 13 | Website audit: mobile speed, form conversion, project page completeness | Marketing + Dev | P0 | Med | High | Load time, CVR |
| 14 | Test every lead path end-to-end (form, WhatsApp, call, portal) — where do they land? | Marketing Ops | **P0** | Low | **Very high** | Leaks found |
| 15 | Agree MQL/SQL definitions with Sales; draft SLA | CMO + Sales Head | **P0** | Low | **Very high** | SLA signed |
| 16 | Define reason codes for rejection and loss | CMO + Sales | P0 | Low | High | Codes live in CRM |
| 17 | Channel-partner audit: who is registered, who is active, what have they produced | CMO + Sales | P0 | Med | High | Active partner count |
| 18 | Competitor deep-dive: Aparna Amaravati One pricing, positioning, sales process | Marketing | P1 | Med | Med | Battle card |

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Item 14 — personally submitting an enquiry through every channel and timing the response — routinely uncovers leads landing in unmonitored inboxes. It takes an afternoon and is often the highest-value thing done in month one.

## Deliverables by day 30

1. **Baseline report** — real numbers replacing every assumption in the model
2. **Funnel diagnostic** — where leads leak, with evidence
3. **Signed Marketing↔Sales SLA** with MQL/SQL definitions and reason codes
4. **Consolidated brand identity** — one Instagram, one Facebook, one domain plan
5. **Speed-to-lead under 5 minutes**, operating
6. **TRAYA launch brief**, approved
7. **60/90-day plan** with budget, priced against real capacity

## What I would explicitly *not* do in month one

**[RECOMMENDATION]** Not launching a rebrand, a new website, a big campaign, or an agency pitch. Each is a large, visible commitment made before the facts are in. **[INFERENCE]** A CMO who spends month one buying media is optimising for the appearance of momentum; the compounding wins here are in measurement and consolidation, and they are invisible until month three — which is exactly why they must be defended to leadership up front, in the week-one conversation.

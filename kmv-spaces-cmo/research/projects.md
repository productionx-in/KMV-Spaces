## 2. Does KMV market projects individually or as a master brand?

**Answer: individually — and the architecture actively works against the master brand.**

Evidence **[OBSERVED]**:
- A dedicated microsite exists at **kmvvivaan.com**, separate from kmvspaces.com
- A dedicated Facebook page **@kmvvivaan** exists alongside @kmvspaces and a third page @kmvspaceshyd
- TRAYA's own tagline — **"Makers of Vivaan"** — positions the new project as an extension of the *previous project*, not of the company
- Public review volume: **454** ratings for the project vs **33** for the company

**[INFERENCE]** This is a *house of brands* structure applied to a business that needs a *branded house*. The consequence is concrete and costly: **trust does not compound.** Every project restarts its credibility from near zero and has to borrow from Vivaan by name. With four projects live and a fifth inevitable, the company is paying this tax four times and will pay it five.

The counter-argument deserves a fair hearing: strong project brands can carry premium pricing, and "Vivaan" is genuinely well-regarded. **The resolution is not to kill Vivaan** — it is to make **KMV Spaces the guarantor and Vivaan/TRAYA the products**. Endorsed architecture: *KMV Spaces presents TRAYA.* That preserves project identity while making every future launch cheaper. Detailed in `/strategy/brand-strategy.md`.

## 3. Does the portfolio create synergy today?

| Mechanism | Present? | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Brand synergy | **Weak** | Fragmented architecture prevents equity transfer |
| Cross-selling | **Not visible** | No observable path from apartment buyer → villa upgrade |
| Trust accumulation | **Partial** | Accrues to "Vivaan," not to "KMV Spaces" |
| Customer lifetime value | **Under-exploited** | An upgrade ladder exists in the product mix but is not marketed |
| Referral | **Not visible publicly** | Whether a resident referral programme exists is *Not publicly available* |

**[INFERENCE] The unexploited ladder.** The portfolio accidentally contains a complete customer-lifetime architecture that nobody appears to be operating:

```
Apartment (entry)  →  Villa (upgrade)  →  Second home / TRAYA (investment)
                              ↓
                    Commercial space (business owner)
```

A Vivaan apartment owner who has lived in a KMV building for four years, watched it deliver, and now has more income is the **single highest-probability, lowest-cost buyer** for a TRAYA villa in the entire market. There is no public evidence this cohort is being marketed to at all.

**[INFERENCE] The referral gap is the biggest quiet number here.** ~66 villa families plus apartment owners — several hundred households who have already paid ₹1–4 Cr and are, per Justdial's 4.2/5 across 454 ratings, broadly satisfied. In premium real estate, referral and word-of-mouth are consistently among the highest-converting and cheapest sources of business. Whether KMV runs a structured referral programme is *Not publicly available* — and it is the first thing I would check, because if the answer is no, it is the cheapest revenue in the company.

## 4. The commercial fact that should reset the marketing model

**[VERIFIED]** Average booking value is approximately **₹3 crore** (Vivaan villas ₹2.95–3.8 Cr).

**[INFERENCE]** At that ticket size, this is a **low-volume, high-value** business, and almost every conventional digital-marketing instinct is wrong for it:

- Selling 60 villas a year is a **~₹180–200 Cr revenue** business.
- If marketing costs 1.5% of revenue — conservative for premium real estate — that is a **~₹3 Cr annual budget**, and a **~₹5 lakh allowable acquisition cost per booking**.
- At an assumed 1.5–3% qualified-lead-to-booking rate, 60 bookings require roughly **2,000–4,000 genuinely qualified leads a year** — *not* tens of thousands.
- **Therefore: lead volume is not the constraint. Lead quality and sales conversion are.** A campaign that halves CPL while halving lead quality destroys value at this AOV; one that doubles CPL while doubling site-visit rate creates it.

This single reframe — **optimise cost-per-booking, never cost-per-lead** — is the spine of the economics model in `/strategy/marketing-economics.md`, and it is the argument I would lead with in front of the promoters.

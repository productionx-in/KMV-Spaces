## 2. Where the highest revenue leakage probably sits

**[INFERENCE]** Ranked by expected value at ~₹3 Cr per booking:

### 1. Speed-to-lead — the largest single leak
**[VERIFIED]** <5 minute response converts to site visits at **4× the rate** of a one-hour response.
**[INFERENCE]** If KMV currently responds in hours, it may be losing up to three-quarters of the site-visit potential in leads already paid for. At ₹3 Cr AOV, recovering even a handful of bookings a year exceeds an entire quarter's media budget. **Costs process, not money.**

### 2. Referral — the biggest quiet loss
**[INFERENCE]** Several hundred delivered households, publicly rated 4.2/5 across 454 ratings, with no visible referral programme. In premium real estate, referral is consistently among the highest-converting and cheapest sources. If referral share is near zero today, this is likely the single largest untapped revenue pool in the company — and it requires a phone list, not a campaign.

### 3. Unrouted and unfollowed leads
**[INFERENCE]** With multiple entry points (three Facebook pages, two Instagram handles, WhatsApp, forms, phone, portals, brokers), the probability that some enquiries land in unmonitored inboxes is high. Every lost qualified lead is a potential ₹3 Cr event. Testing every path personally in week one is an afternoon's work.

### 4. Site-visit show rate and quality
**[INFERENCE]** The site visit is where a ₹3 Cr decision is actually made. Without reminder automation, no-shows waste the most expensive lead in the funnel; without a structured playbook, KMV's strongest asset — a *completed, occupied community* — may not even be used as the proof it is.

### 5. Post-booking silence during delays
**[VERIFIED]** Possession delay is a recurring Vivaan complaint. **[INFERENCE]** A buyer kept informed through a delay forgives it and still refers; one left in silence does not, and tells others. This stage doesn't just risk cancellations — it destroys the referral engine in leak #2.

### 6. Landing page conversion
**[VERIFIED]** Sub-2s pages typically cut CPL 20–35%; real-estate pages average ~4.7% conversion. **[INFERENCE]** If traffic lands on generic project pages rather than dedicated, fast, conversion-designed ones, a large share of paid traffic is wasted before a lead ever exists.

## 3. What to audit internally — in priority order

All *Not publicly available*:

1. **Median speed-to-lead**, and % of leads never contacted at all
2. **Referral share of past bookings** — sizes leak #2 immediately
3. **Lead source → booking traceability** — can any booking be attributed?
4. **Site-visit show rate and visit→booking rate**, by RM
5. **Reason-for-loss distribution** — which objection actually kills deals
6. **Every lead path tested end-to-end** — where does each one land?
7. **Landing page conversion rate and mobile load time**
8. **Post-booking communication** — does any programme exist?
9. **Follow-up attempts per lead** before "unreachable"
10. **Sales capacity** — visits per month possible (the hard ceiling on everything)

**[INFERENCE]** Items 1, 2 and 3 typically reveal the largest recoverable revenue in a mid-sized developer — and none of them require additional media spend to fix. That is the core argument for sequencing the funnel audit ahead of campaign investment.

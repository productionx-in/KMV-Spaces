## 1. Executive Decision

### 1.1 Should the company approve ₹31.75 crore?

# **CONDITIONAL — and not in that form.**

**RECOMMENDATION.** Do not approve ₹31.75 crore. Do not reject it either. **Approve a governed envelope and release ₹25 lakh against it.**

**Three reasons, in order of weight.**

**1 · The number is not a marketing budget. It is four kinds of money under one label.** *(INFERENCE, from the Budget Audit)* Approving it as presented would put channel-partner commission, a building and a technology platform inside a marketing operating budget. ## 2. Investment Thesis

### 2.1 What the money is buying

**FACT.** The corridor's political risk is resolved. Amaravati holds statutory status as the state's sole capital, with large-scale works funded and active.

**FACT.** Every corridor price visible to this analysis is an **asking** price. The locality average sits at ₹4,062/sq ft; a delivered apartment comparable at ₹5,100.

**ASSUMPTION.** The plan realises **₹6,098/sq ft blended** — a **50% premium to the locality average and 11% to the wider micromarket.**

**INFERENCE.** A 15-units-per-acre scheme with villas *should* price above a locality average dominated by towers. **But the premium has never been tested, and it is the single largest commercial assumption in the project** — larger than any marketing variable.

### 2.2 The strategic position

**RECOMMENDATION.** The corridor sells density. **This scheme sells the absence of it** — 15 homes per acre across 25 acres, where the nearest apartment comparable runs at multiples of that. Four communities differentiated **by buyer constraint, not by finish level.**

### 2.3 The thesis in one line

> **This is not a demand-generation problem. It is a price-validation problem with a demand-generation plan attached.**
>
> **INFERENCE.** ₹268 crore of the plan sits in a price band that eight rounds of analysis could not evidence as occupied. **No marketing budget corrects a price the market refuses.** The first rupee should be spent finding out — not on media.

### 5.4 Where the leverage actually is

**INFERENCE, and it reverses the earlier headline.**

The conversion rates are **multiplicative**, so a 25% move in any of them changes bookings by exactly 25% — **50 bookings, ₹67.7 crore.** They are arithmetically identical. **What separates them is cost to fix and who controls them.**

| Variable | Controlled by | Cost to move 25% | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CPL** | The auction and competitors | **₹2.46–4.92 Cr of media** | **Largest risk** — least controllable, entirely unverified |

| Qualified → site visit | Our follow-up and offer | Process. Near zero | Cheapest fix |
| **Site visit → booking** | Our sales team and the site | Training and site experience | **Largest opportunity** |

> **One point of visit-to-booking — 6.9% to 7.9% — is worth ₹39.4 crore at zero additional media.** No media buying produces that return.

### 9.1 Two community-level judgements

> **Community A is the exposure nobody flagged.** *(INFERENCE)* At ₹5,150/sq ft it prices **above a delivered apartment comparable at ₹5,100** and 27% above the locality average — with no delivery record to justify it. **A is 169 units, 45% of the scheme, and the community least able to command a premium**, because a garden apartment competes on rate. **RECOMMENDATION: test A's price before its media.**
>
> **Community B should be gated, not defended.** *(RECOMMENDATION)* B's constraint is that its buyer cannot casually visit — a product and release-schedule problem, not a media one. **₹1.05 crore of its budget funds a proxy-visit capability that has never been piloted.** Pilot it on an existing project first. **If B's qualified-to-visit rate does not reach 25% within two quarters, reduce B's inventory release rather than raise its budget.**

### 9.2 Allocation principle

**RECOMMENDATION.** Directly attributable costs — performance media by community, the agency fee pro-rata, and the NRI infrastructure that serves B alone — are traced. **Everything shared is allocated by revenue share**, because the brand, the site, the technology and the launch serve all four and cannot be traced to one. **Allocating by unit count instead would load Community A with 45% of the shared cost for 25% of the revenue.**

## 9. Creative as a Growth Function

**RECOMMENDATION.** Creative is not decoration and it is not a taste argument. **It is the only lever that improves every downstream metric at once, without buying more media.**

### 11.1 The commercial chain

> **Creative → attention → qualified demand → trust → site visit → conversion → revenue.**

Each arrow is a measurable step, and creative is accountable at each one:

| Link | What creative does | Metric | Target |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Attention** | Stops the scroll | **Hook rate** *(3-sec view / impression)* | ≥ 25% |
| | | **CTR** | ≥ 1.2% |
| **Qualified demand** | Attracts the right person, repels the wrong one | **Landing-page conversion** | ≥ 6% |
| | | **CPQL by variant** | ≤ ₹7,889 |
| **Trust** | Makes the claim checkable | **Lead quality — % passing the cash gate** | ≥ 45% |
| **Site visit** | Converts interest into travel | **Qualified-to-visit rate by creative** | ≥ 23% |
| **Conversion** | Arms the sales conversation | **Booking contribution by first-touch creative** | Tracked |

### 11.2 The discipline that makes creative commercial

**A creative variant is not judged on whether it is liked. It is judged on cost per qualified site visit.**

- **Twelve variants in Phase 0**, three per audience — price-led, proof-led, density-led. **The market decides which territory is right, not the room.**
- **A variant whose CPQL exceeds 2× the best variant is cut**, regardless of who made it.
- **Fatigue is a trigger, not an opinion:** frequency ceiling plus CPL drift >20% against a rolling 30-day baseline forces a refresh.
- **Attribution is installed before creative runs** — otherwise creative cannot be held to anything.

> **The strategic point.** *(INFERENCE)* The corridor's advertising is generic and proof-free. **In a market where nobody publishes evidence, the highest-performing creative territory is likely to be the one that shows a number rather than a lifestyle** — and that is a testable hypothesis, not a preference. **Phase 0 tests it for ₹25 lakh.**

## 12. ROI / ROMI Framework

### 14.1 The measure, and the one that must not be used

> **ROMI = (gross profit − marketing investment) ÷ marketing investment.**

**Revenue ÷ spend would read 16.0×.** *(FACT)* It is meaningless — most of that revenue pays for land, construction and finance. **Any property development deck showing 16× should be disbelieved, including this one.**

| Gross margin *(ASSUMPTION)* | Gross profit | Contribution | **ROMI** | CAC % of GP | Break-even |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| 25% | ₹126.7 Cr | ₹94.9 Cr | **2.99×** | 25.1% | **94 units** |
| **28%** | ₹141.9 Cr | ₹110.1 Cr | **3.47×** | 22.4% | **84 units** |
| 31% | ₹157.1 Cr | ₹125.3 Cr | **3.95×** | 20.2% | **76 units** |

### 14.2 The statement that survives every assumption

> **Marketing pays for itself at 76–94 bookings out of 376. The scheme is 4.0–4.9× past marketing break-even.**

**INFERENCE.** This is the number to defend, because **it holds at every margin in the range.** The ROMI figure does not — it moves 32% across the same range, on an assumption nobody has tested.

### Break-even and the ceilings

#### The core question: what must ₹1 crore of marketing return?

**The logic.** Marketing pays for itself when the gross profit on the units it generates exceeds its own cost. So the break-even test is not a percentage — it is **a number of incremental bookings per crore.**

| Gross margin *(ASSUMPTION)* | Gross profit per unit | **Incremental bookings needed per ₹1 Cr** | Equivalent revenue |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| 25% | ₹33.69 L | **2.97** | ₹4.0 Cr |
| **28%** | **₹37.73 L** | **2.65** | **₹3.6 Cr** |
| 31% | ₹41.78 L | **2.39** | ₹3.2 Cr |

> **Every additional ₹1 crore of marketing must produce roughly 2.7 incremental bookings to pay for itself.** That is the single sentence a CFO should hold the marketing function to. **Below it, the crore should not be spent.**

#### Ceilings and targets, with the reasoning

**The logic for the ceiling.** The absolute maximum CAC is the entire gross profit per unit — beyond that the unit loses money. But a project that hands all its margin to marketing is not a business. **RECOMMENDATION: a policy ceiling of 30% of gross profit per unit.** That leaves 70% for land, finance, overhead and profit.

| | Ceiling logic | @25% | @28% | @31% |
| --- | --- | --: | --: | --: |
| **Absolute maximum CAC** | All gross profit — loses money beyond | ₹33.69 L | ₹37.73 L | ₹41.78 L |
| **Maximum acceptable CAC** | **30% of gross profit — the policy** | **₹10.11 L** | **₹11.32 L** | **₹12.53 L** |
| **Target CAC** | Current model, all-in | ₹8.44 L | ₹8.44 L | ₹8.44 L |
| **Stretch CAC** | Marketing opex only | ₹6.33 L | ₹6.33 L | ₹6.33 L |

**Scaling the funnel ceilings by that headroom (1.34×):**

| Metric | **Target** | **Maximum acceptable** | Reasoning |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| **CPL** | ₹1,247 | **₹1,672** | Above this, no downstream conversion rate rescues the unit economics |
| **CPQL** | ₹7,889 | **₹10,576** | The metric campaigns should be optimised against |
| **CPSV** | ₹33,676 | **₹45,144** | The stage that carries the most value |
| **CPO** *(negotiation)* | ₹1,71,429 | **₹2,29,810** | Late-funnel diagnostic |
| **Marketing % of GDV — opex** | **4.70%** | — | The CMO's accountable line |
| **Marketing % of GDV — all-in** | **6.27%** | **8.40%** | Ceiling derived from the 30% GP policy, **not from an industry norm** |

**Why the % of GDV is stated last and smallest.** *(INFERENCE)* It is the least useful of these metrics: GDV arrives over four years while spend is front-loaded, so the ratio flatters a slow year and punishes a fast one. **It is included because it will be asked for, not because decisions should be made on it.**

### 17.2 "Why should I trust you with this, if you haven't personally run ₹30 crore of spend?"

> *The honest answer is that the question is slightly the wrong one. Nobody should be trusted with ₹30 crore on the strength of having previously spent ₹30 crore — plenty of people have spent it badly. What matters is whether the person asking has a method for not losing it.*
>
> *So here is what I'd point to instead.*
>
> ***I found the errors in my own plan before you did.*** *The headline number wasn't a marketing budget — it bundled commission, a building and a software platform under one label. It also breached a cost-per-unit ceiling the analysis itself had set, and nobody had noticed. And the funnel had no cancellation allowance, which quietly overstated revenue by ₹40 crore. I'd rather bring you those three things than have you find them.*
>
> ***I'm not asking for the money.*** *I'm asking for twenty-five lakh — about one percent of the envelope — and I've written down in advance what result would make me stop. If cost per lead comes back fifty percent worse than assumed, I don't return asking for more; the recommendation is to halt and re-plan. A budget with a pre-agreed kill condition is a different kind of ask.*
>
> ***I know what the test can't tell us.*** *Twenty-five lakh measures cost per lead and cost per qualified site visit properly. It cannot measure the booking rate — three or four bookings tells you nothing. That takes two quarters of real trading. I'd rather say that now than produce a confident number I can't stand behind.*
>
> ***I'd hold agencies to the same discipline.*** *The performance fee is twelve percent of media, so the agency's incentive is to spend more. The counterweight is that they're measured on cost per qualified site visit, not on leads or impressions, with a fatigue trigger that forces creative refresh automatically rather than by request.*
>
> ***And I know where the money shouldn't go first.*** *Not into a large outdoor commitment, not into a launch event, not into broad awareness — none of which can be evaluated before the measurement system exists. That system is the first thing built, and it's why the first real phase isn't a media phase at all.*
>
> *What I'd ask you to judge is whether the framework protects your capital. If a quarter goes badly, you'll know within weeks rather than at the end of the year, and the response is written down before the money moves.*

### 17.3 What this document does not claim

**Stated plainly, because a strategy that hides its own limits is not one.**

- **No estimate of the developer's actual marketing budget, media spend, headcount, historical CPL, sales velocity or inventory position** is offered anywhere. None is available, and inventing them would be worse than naming the gap.
- **No industry benchmark has been manufactured.** Where a published rate band exists it is used and labelled; where none exists, a range is used and labelled as an assumption.
- **The revenue base itself is unproven.** A 50% premium to the locality asking price sits underneath every figure here. **If it does not hold, this entire document re-bases.**

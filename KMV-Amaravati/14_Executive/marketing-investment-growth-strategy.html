Three rules that hold across every phase

1 · A saving is never banked
  • Underspend moves to the highest-leverage stage — the site visit.
  • A returned crore is a crore that was mis-planned, not a crore earned.
2 · A missed gate cuts inventory first
  • It cuts inventory before it cuts price, and cuts inventory before it raises budget.
  • This is the rule that stops a bad quarter turning into a bigger budget.
3 · Twice missed releases nothing
  • A gate missed two consecutive times releases no capital, regardless of explanation.
  • Written down now, because it will not be agreeable later.

A caution about the MQL / SQL split

The MQL and SQL rates are a decomposition, not new evidence. 45% × 55% × 63.9% = 15.81%, which is precisely the original assumed lead-to-qualified rate. Splitting it makes the funnel diagnosable; it does not make it better evidenced.

The cancellation correction

The model had no cancellation allowance at all

Fact The existing model contains no cancellation line. 376 bookings were treated as 376 units sold.

Inference At an 8% cancellation assumption, 409 gross bookings are required to net 376 — 33 more bookings, roughly ₹2.76 crore of additional acquisition cost at the modelled CAC.

Recommendation Either the revenue target falls to ₹466 crore at the current booking plan, or the marketing requirement rises. §6 models it as revenue falling, because that is the disciplined reading.

The two findings that matter

1 · The base case no longer reaches ₹506.7 crore

Inference Once an 8% cancellation allowance is applied, the same funnel delivers 346 net units and ₹466.2 crore — a ₹40.5 crore shortfall against the plan’s own target. The gap was invisible because cancellation was never modelled.

2 · The conservative case breaches the CAC policy ceiling and should not be funded as planned

Inference At 46.3% of gross profit, CAC is well past the 30% ceiling proposed in §12. ROMI of 1.16× means marketing barely returns its own cost.

This is not a scenario to accept — it is a scenario to detect early and stop.

Which is why ₹25 lakh comes first

Revenue spread between conservative and upside is ₹552.6 crore — 109% of the base case.

Inference No marketing decision in this document is as consequential as which scenario is true, and that is a pricing and market question, not a media question.

Recommendation The purpose of Phase 0 is to establish, for ₹25 lakh, which of these three worlds the project is actually in — before ₹23.80 crore is committed to the assumption that it is the middle one.

Two community-level judgements

Community A is the exposure nobody flagged

Inference At ₹5,150/sq ft it prices above a delivered apartment comparable at ₹5,100 and 27% above the locality average — with no delivery record to justify it.

A is 169 units, 45% of the scheme, and the community least able to command a premium, because a garden apartment competes on rate.

Recommendation Test A’s price before its media.

Community B should be gated, not defended

Recommendation B’s constraint is that its buyer cannot casually visit — a product and release-schedule problem, not a media one.

₹1.05 crore of its budget funds a proxy-visit capability that has never been piloted. Pilot it on an existing project first.

If B’s qualified-to-visit rate does not reach 25% within two quarters, reduce B’s inventory release rather than raise its budget.

Allocation principle

Recommendation

Directly attributable costs are traced — performance media by community, the agency fee pro-rata, and the NRI infrastructure that serves B alone.

Everything shared is allocated by revenue share, because the brand, the site, the technology and the launch serve all four and cannot be traced to one.

Allocating by unit count instead would load Community A with 45% of the shared cost for 25% of the revenue — and A is already the community with the least pricing headroom.

The discipline that makes creative commercial

The rule

A creative variant is not judged on whether it is liked. It is judged on cost per qualified site visit.

Twelve variants in Phase 0
  • Three per audience — price-led, proof-led, density-led.
  • The market decides which territory is right, not the room.
A hard cut rule
  • A variant whose CPQL exceeds 2× the best variant is cut — regardless of who made it.
  • Agreed in writing before the first variant runs.
Fatigue is a trigger, not an opinion
  • Frequency ceiling plus CPL drift >20% against a rolling 30-day baseline forces a refresh.
  • Automatic — not by request.
Attribution comes first
  • Attribution is installed before creative runs — otherwise creative cannot be held to anything.
  • This is why Phase 1 precedes any spend at scale.
The strategic point

Inference The corridor’s advertising is generic and proof-free. In a market where nobody publishes evidence, the highest-performing creative territory is likely to be the one that shows a number rather than a lifestyle.

That is a testable hypothesis, not a preference. Phase 0 tests it for ₹25 lakh.

Three reading rules, in the template before the first review

1 · Compare within a layer, never across
  • Judging brand against performance on cost per booking defunds ₹10.25 crore of the highest-value spend.
  • It is the most common way a good budget is destroyed by a reasonable-sounding question.
2 · Judge demand generation on CPQL, not CPL
  • Cheap leads that fail the cash gate are a cost, not a result.
  • CPL is a buying metric. CPQL is a business metric.
3 · Judge brand, outdoor and the site on holdouts
  • First-touch and geo holdouts — never last click.
  • Last click will always find in favour of the search line and against everything that made the search happen.
Deliberately absent

Impressions, reach, followers, page views. Recommendation None of them can change a decision, and each of them can be used to defend a bad quarter.

ROI / ROMI framework

On gross profit — and the ratio that must never be used

The measure

ROMI = (gross profit − marketing investment) ÷ marketing investment.

Revenue ÷ spend would read 16.0×. Fact It is meaningless — most of that revenue pays for land, construction and finance. Any property development deck showing 16× should be disbelieved, including this one.

The statement that survives every assumption

Marketing pays for itself at 76–94 bookings out of 376. The scheme is 4.0–4.9× past marketing break-even.

Inference This is the number to defend, because it holds at every margin in the range. The ROMI figure does not — it moves 32% across the same range, on an assumption nobody has tested.

Break-even: what ₹1 crore of marketing must return

The logic. Marketing pays for itself when the gross profit on the units it generates exceeds its own cost. So the break-even test is not a percentage — it is a number of incremental bookings per crore.

Gross margin (assumption)Gross profit per unitIncremental bookings needed per ₹1 CrEquivalent revenue

The single sentence to hold marketing to

Every additional ₹1 crore of marketing must produce roughly 2.7 incremental bookings to pay for itself.

Below that, the crore should not be spent. It converts an argument about budget size into an arithmetic test that either passes or fails.

Ceilings and targets, with the reasoning

The logic for the ceiling. The absolute maximum CAC is the entire gross profit per unit — beyond that the unit loses money. But a project that hands all its margin to marketing is not a business. Recommendation a policy ceiling of 30% of gross profit per unit, leaving 70% for land, finance, overhead and profit.

Final recommendation

The ask, the answer to the obvious objection, and the limits

The ask

A governed envelope of ₹25.33–26.86 crore

Marketing operating expense — the ₹23.80 crore reclassified figure plus the internal team line the model was missing — against a total commercial budget of ₹33.28–34.81 crore including cost of sale and capex.

And what is actually released today

₹25 lakh. One percent of the envelope. The remainder in five further tranches, each against a KPI gate with a named decision-maker and a written stop condition.

“Why should I trust you with this, if you haven’t personally run ₹30 crore of spend?”

Q

Why should I trust you with this, if you haven’t personally run ₹30 crore of spend?

Answer

The honest answer is that the question is slightly the wrong one. Nobody should be trusted with ₹30 crore on the strength of having previously spent ₹30 crore — plenty of people have spent it badly. What matters is whether the person asking has a method for not losing it.

I found the errors in my own plan before you did
  • The headline number wasn’t a marketing budget — it bundled commission, a building and a software platform under one label.
  • It breached a cost-per-unit ceiling the analysis itself had set, and nobody had noticed.
  • The funnel had no cancellation allowance, which quietly overstated revenue by ₹40 crore.
  • I’d rather bring you those three things than have you find them.
I’m not asking for the money
  • I’m asking for twenty-five lakh — about one percent of the envelope — and I’ve written down in advance what result would make me stop.
  • If cost per lead comes back fifty percent worse than assumed, I don’t return asking for more; the recommendation is to halt and re-plan.
  • A budget with a pre-agreed kill condition is a different kind of ask.
I know what the test can’t tell us
  • Twenty-five lakh measures cost per lead and cost per qualified site visit properly.
  • It cannot measure the booking rate — three or four bookings tells you nothing. That takes two quarters of real trading.
  • I’d rather say that now than produce a confident number I can’t stand behind.
I’d hold agencies to the same discipline
  • The performance fee is twelve percent of media, so the agency’s incentive is to spend more.
  • The counterweight is that they’re measured on cost per qualified site visit — not leads, not impressions — with a fatigue trigger that forces creative refresh automatically rather than by request.
And I know where the money shouldn’t go first
  • Not into a large outdoor commitment, not into a launch event, not into broad awareness — none of which can be evaluated before the measurement system exists.
  • That system is the first thing built, and it’s why the first real phase isn’t a media phase at all.
What I’d ask you to judge
  • Whether the framework protects your capital.
  • If a quarter goes badly, you’ll know within weeks rather than at the end of the year, and the response is written down before the money moves.

What this document does not claim

Stated plainly, because a strategy that hides its own limits is not one

Data required No estimate of the developer’s actual marketing budget, media spend, headcount, historical CPL, sales velocity or inventory position is offered anywhere in this document. None is available, and inventing them would be worse than naming the gap.

Fact No industry benchmark has been manufactured. Where a published rate band exists it is used and labelled; where none exists, a range is used and labelled as an assumption.

Assumption The revenue base itself is unproven. A 50% premium to the locality asking price sits underneath every figure here. If it does not hold, this entire document re-bases.

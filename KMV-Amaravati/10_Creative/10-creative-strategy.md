# Phase 10 — Creative Strategy

## An idea built from the one number no competitor in this corridor can say

**Prepared:** August 2026
**Purpose:** Creative platform, narrative system and execution direction for the Phase 6 architecture
**Method note:** The brief says *avoid generic real-estate clichés unless research proves they work.* §16 answers both halves of that instruction — the clichés cut, and the four the research does support. Every creative decision here traces to a specific finding in Phases 1–9; where it doesn't, it is flagged as taste rather than evidence.

---

## 0. What the creative work has to carry

Nine phases produced a set of facts. Three of them are the whole brief.

**1 · The product is defined by what is not on the land.**

**Computed** — Phase 6 §2.2, using the **lowest apartment density observed anywhere in this corridor** (Jayabheri, 52.9 units/acre) as the benchmark:

| | Homes | Site per home |
| --- | --: | --: |
| What 25 acres could hold at the corridor's **lowest** density | **874** | 823 sq ft |
| At Aparna's density | 1,068 | 661 sq ft |
| At Manjeera Monarch's density | 1,781 | 384 sq ft |
| **What this scheme builds** | **376** | **2,904 sq ft** |

> **498 homes were not built.** At the corridor's own lowest benchmark. Against Manjeera Monarch — a real project a buyer can drive to — this scheme gives **7.6× more site per home.**

Phase 6 §2.3 priced that restraint: roughly **₹344–500 crore of forgone gross development value.** The premium is not a markup. It is the visible cost of what was left out.

**2 · The developer built the hospital.**

**Established — Phase 4.** KMV Projects built **AIIMS Mangalagiri**, three kilometres from the recommended micro-location. Phase 5 found delivery anxiety is the one concern shared by all eight segments. This is the only proof point in the exercise that answers it without asking for trust.

**3 · The site visit is where the money is.**

**Phase 8:** a point of visit-to-booking conversion is worth **₹39.4 crore** — 2.3× a point at the lead stage. **Phase 9** put ₹4.10 crore on the experience centre, the largest single line in the plan.

> **The creative brief that follows from those three facts:** the work is not there to make people feel something about a home. **It is there to get people onto 25 acres and let the land do the arguing.**

### 0.1 The cliché test

**Established.** Indian real-estate advertising guidance identifies the specific failures as **superlatives without evidence** — *"India's most luxurious project"* — and **general lifestyle statements** — *"live your dream."* And the working test for a line is that it should be **hard to swap onto a competitor without losing meaning.**

That test is the one this phase is built to pass. §16 runs every conventional device through it.

## 1. Master brand idea

> ## The most valuable thing on these twenty-five acres is what isn't on them.

**The organising thought, stated plainly:** every developer in this corridor sells how much they built. **This one sells how much it didn't.**

Three properties make it usable rather than merely clever:

**It is true and computable.** 376 homes where 874 would fit. 15 to the acre where the corridor builds 53 to 113. A buyer can verify it from a public FAR calculation, or by standing at the gate and counting.

**It is unownable by anyone else.** Every competitor in the Phase 3 set is denser. To attack the claim, they must state their own number — which is the argument this brand wants them to have.

**It generates work across four communities and eight segments.** The idea is arithmetic, and arithmetic scales: each community has its own number, and the master number is their average.

### 1.1 The signature line

> # Fifteen to the acre.
> ## Everything else is open.

**Telugu:** **ఎకరాకు పదిహేను.** *Ekaraaku padihenu.*

**Why this line and not a warmer one.** It is a number, so it cannot be exaggerated, cannot be matched, and cannot be swapped onto a competitor. It states the product's defining fact in four words. **And it is strange enough for real estate to be remembered** — the category does not use numbers as headlines, which is precisely why one works.

The second line does the emotional work the first refuses to do. *Everything else is open* is literally true — 25 acres minus 376 building footprints — and it is what the buyer is actually purchasing.

### 1.2 What the idea is not

It is **not minimalism**, which is a design style and would read as cold to S1's trade families. It is **not exclusivity** — Phase 5 established ₹2.74 crore is an upper-affluent ticket, not a high-net-worth one, and claiming otherwise promises a tier the price sheet does not deliver. It is **not sustainability** — the open space is a commercial choice, not an environmental one, and dressing it as green would be the exact kind of unevidenced claim §0.1 rules out.

**It is restraint, presented as arithmetic.**

## 2. Campaign territory

**The territory: verification.**

Where the category asks to be believed, this brand asks to be checked. Every execution carries something countable — a number, a document, a name, a distance, a date. The creative device is **specificity where competitors use adjectives.**

| The category does | This brand does |
| --- | --- |
| "Spacious living" | **2,904 sq ft of site per home** |
| "Trusted developer" | **The people who built AIIMS Mangalagiri** |
| "Premium location" | **12 minutes to AIIMS. Timed, not estimated.** |
| "Attractive pricing" | **The full price sheet, published, ungated** |
| "Limited units" | **Sixty villas. There is no phase two on this land.** |
| "Ready to move in 2029" | **RERA P0712…, and a construction feed updated monthly** |

**Why this territory and not aspiration.** Phase 5 found the buyers who carry 53% of revenue — S1 trade families, S2 doctors, S3 diaspora — are all, in different ways, **verifiers**. S2 *"will check RERA; most of the catchment will not."* S3's stated primary concern is fraud. S1's opening question is *"who else has bought?"* **Aspirational creative asks all three of them to do the one thing they are least willing to do: take a developer's word for it.**

## 3. Brand narrative

*The long-form statement. Used at the experience centre, on the website's About page, as the launch film's script, and as the internal north star.*

> **Twenty-five acres on the Mangalagiri corridor.**
>
> The rules would have let us put eight hundred and seventy-four homes here. The developer down the road managed a hundred and thirteen to the acre, and nobody stopped him.
>
> **We built three hundred and seventy-six.**
>
> That is fifteen homes to an acre. It means two thousand nine hundred square feet of this land belongs to each home — nearly eight times what you get in the tallest tower on this road. It means the distance between your window and the next one is a distance, not a gap. It means the children's play area is a field. It means your car is parked where you left it, not behind three others.
>
> It also means four hundred and ninety-eight homes that could have been sold, were not.
>
> **We know what that cost.** It is the reason this address is priced where it is. Not a markup — the visible price of what we left out.
>
> The people who built this are the people who built AIIMS Mangalagiri, three kilometres from here. You can drive past it on the way in. **We would rather you did.**
>
> Come and count.

**Reasoned — why this narrative works on this audience.** It is written the way this catchment actually talks about property: in numbers, land measures and named landmarks. It contains no adjective that cannot be checked. And its closing instruction is an invitation to the site visit — the stage worth ₹39.4 crore a point.

## 4. The four community narratives

**The system.** Each community owns its own number. The master number is their average. **This falls directly out of Phase 6's architecture — it was not invented for the campaign.**

| | Homes to the acre | Site per home | The claim |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| **A** The Apartments | **26** | 1,675 sq ft | Half the density of the least dense apartment block in the corridor |
| **B** The Managed Residences | **24** | 1,815 sq ft | A home built to be empty |
| **C** The Compact Villas | **10** | 4,356 sq ft | A villa inside a masterplan, not a villa on a plot |
| **D** The Estate Villas | **7** | 6,223 sq ft | You are not buying a bigger house. You are buying more ground |
| **The whole** | **15** | 2,904 sq ft | Everything else is open |

### 4.1 Community A — The Apartments

**Primary audience:** S5, government and the relocating capital workforce. **Ticket ₹59–90 L.**

> ### Twenty-six to the acre.
> ### The lowest number on this road.

**The narrative.** *The corridor builds apartments at fifty-three to a hundred and thirteen homes an acre. This is twenty-six. It is the least expensive address on these twenty-five acres, and it is on the same twenty-five acres. The clubhouse you can walk to is the same clubhouse. The open space is the same open space. You are not buying the cheap corner of a good place — you are buying into it, at the point where it starts.*

**The emotional truth being served** — Phase 5, S5: *ending a long rental period*, and *respectability of address*. The insight is that this buyer's fear is not price. **It is being the poor relation inside a premium scheme.** The narrative's whole job is to say the shared spine is genuinely shared.

**The objection it answers** — *"Is this priced above Vijayawada for the same thing?"* No: it is priced above Vijayawada for half the density.

**Trigger creative.** Phase 5 established the pay-revision arrears window as this segment's purchase trigger. The arrears campaign is a distinct flight: *"Your arrears are the down payment. Here is the arithmetic."* — with the actual working shown.

### 4.2 Community B — The Managed Residences

**Primary audience:** S3, US and developed-market diaspora. **Ticket ₹1.23–1.45 Cr.**

> ### Built to be empty eleven months a year.
> ### That is not a flaw. It is the specification.

**The narrative.** *Most homes punish you for leaving. Dust, damp, a garden that dies, a lock nobody checks. This one is built the other way round — single level, lift-served, managed maintenance on a schedule, security designed for homes with nobody in them, and a rental desk if you want one. You can buy it from New Jersey under a registered Power of Attorney without boarding a plane. You can hand the keys to your parents in the same week. And when you come in December, it will be exactly as you left it.*

**The emotional truth** — Phase 5, S3: *guilt and duty toward parents*, and the stated primary concern, **fraud**. The narrative answers the second with process, not reassurance: PoA, RERA, published documents, a named person.

**The objection it answers** — *"How do I know this is real?"* By being the only project in the corridor that publishes the whole file.

**And the one it must not pretend away** — Phase 5: *"Why Amaravati and not Hyderabad?"* The competitor is a city, not a project. The honest answer is roots and price, and it should be said plainly rather than dodged.

### 4.3 Community C — The Compact Villas

**Primary audience:** S1 trade and business families; S2 senior medical. **Ticket ₹1.69–1.95 Cr.**

> ### Ten to the acre.
> ### The villa down the road is twelve.

**The narrative.** *There is a good villa community ten minutes from here, on ten acres, at roughly twelve homes an acre, for about four hundred rupees a foot less. It is a real choice and you should look at it. Then look at what twenty-five acres buys that ten cannot: a central open space large enough to be a place rather than a gap, a clubhouse that serves three hundred and seventy-six homes instead of a hundred and nineteen, a retail row, a school site. You are not paying twenty per cent more for a villa. You are paying it for what surrounds the villa.*

**Reasoned — why naming the competitor works here.** Phase 5 found S1's information source is peers and brokers who **will name Prime Grandeur in the conversation anyway.** A brand that pretends the alternative does not exist loses credibility with the exact segment whose first instinct is to verify. **Naming it, fairly, and then answering it is the stronger position** — and it is only available to the project that wins the comparison on masterplan.

**The emotional truth** — Phase 5, S1: *standing, permanence, legacy.* And the rational driver: *land appreciation logic applied to built product.*

### 4.4 Community D — The Estate Villas

**Primary audience:** S1 trade families. **Ticket ₹2.30–2.74 Cr.**

> ### Seven to the acre.
> ### Six thousand two hundred square feet of this land is yours.

**The narrative.** *The house is about six hundred square feet bigger than the one in Community C. The ground under it is a hundred and twenty-five square yards bigger. Two-thirds of what you are paying is land, not construction — which is the calculation you were going to do anyway. Sixty-three homes on nine acres. There is no phase two on this land, because there is no more land.*

**Reasoned — this is the sharpest audience-message fit in the document.** Phase 5 found S1's default alternative is *"why not just buy land?"* **Community D answers that objection inside the project rather than losing to it outside** — and it does so by speaking the segment's own unit of account. A segment that thinks in square yards is being sold square yards, in the one form that also comes with a house on it.

**The launch rule that overrides all creative.** Phase 6: **D's first tranche is seeded, not advertised.** Phase 5: S1 *"does not buy a project; it buys the other buyers."* No paid media runs against Community D until fifteen named local households have bought. The creative for D in year one is not a campaign — **it is a list of names.**

## 5. Messaging hierarchy

Four levels. **Nothing at a lower level may contradict a higher one, and nothing at any level may be an adjective without a number behind it.**

| Level | What it is | Content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Master claim** | One thing, everywhere, unchanged | **Twenty-five acres. Fifteen homes to the acre. Everything else is open.** |
| **2 · Master proof** | The three things that make level 1 credible | **The builders of AIIMS Mangalagiri** · **RERA-registered, number published** · **498 homes we did not build** |
| **3 · Community claim** | One per community, the number first | **26 · 24 · 10 · 7 to the acre**, each with its proposition (§4) |
| **4 · Segment proof** | Swapped by audience, never by channel | Commute times, timed · payment plans and the arrears arithmetic · PoA and FEMA process · the exchange term sheet · Vaastu orientation as spec · possession date |

### 5.1 The rule that keeps it honest

> **Every claim must survive the question "what is the number?"**
>
> If a line cannot answer it, the line does not run. *Spacious* fails. *2,904 square feet of site per home* passes. *Trusted builder* fails. *Built AIIMS Mangalagiri* passes. *Prime location* fails. *Twelve minutes to AIIMS, timed at 8am on a Tuesday* passes.

This is a production rule, not a philosophy. It should sit at the top of every creative brief and every agency scope.

## 6. Creative pillars

Five. Each one generates work independently, and every asset in the plan belongs to exactly one.

| Pillar | What it does | Serves | Typical output |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · The Number** | Carries the master claim. The arithmetic of density in every form | All eight segments | Outdoor, launch film, the price-per-foot-of-land graphic, the two-model comparison at the site |
| **2 · The Builder** | Delivery credibility, unclaimed by any competitor | **S1, S2, S3, S7** — everyone with delivery anxiety | The AIIMS film, PR, the construction feed, the specification document |
| **3 · The Open** | What the density actually buys, at human scale | **S6, S5, S1** — the families who will live in it | Photography of the space between buildings; the school-run film; the monsoon-and-summer series |
| **4 · The Neighbours** | Answers *"who else has bought?"* structurally | **S1 above all**, then S4 and S7 | Owner films, milestone events, the buyer wall at the experience centre, the referral programme |
| **5 · The Document** | Publishes what competitors gate | **S2, S3, S5, S4** | The ungated price sheet, RERA file, PoA explainer, exchange term sheet, EMI working |

> **Reasoned — pillar 5 is the unusual one, and the cheapest.** Phase 3 found no competitor in the screened set publishes a unit-wise price sheet. Phase 5 found S5 *"will ask for the all-in number including registration, and should be given it"* and S2 *"builds a spreadsheet."* **Publishing the price sheet costs nothing, cannot be copied without conceding the argument, and converts the project's least glamorous asset — a table of numbers — into its most persuasive.**

## 7. Visual direction

### 7.1 The one decision that determines everything else

> **The hero image is a plan view, not a facade.**

Density is only legible from above. A photograph of a building elevation tells a buyer nothing about how many other buildings there are — which is the entire proposition. **A vertical aerial of 25 acres, with 376 roofs visible against everything that is not a roof, is the single most persuasive image this project can produce**, and no competitor on 5–11 acres can shoot its equivalent.

Every other visual decision follows from making that image the centre of the system.

### 7.2 Palette

Drawn from the site rather than from a mood board.

| | Source |
| --- | --- |
| **Black cotton soil** — deep, warm-black | The Guntur belt's own ground, and the colour of the site before anything is built |
| **Krishna silt** — pale grey-buff | The river four kilometres north |
| **November green** | The specific, unsaturated green of this district after the monsoon — not the impossible emerald of CGI |
| **Granite grey** | The Mangalagiri stone in the hill temple |
| **One accent: a burnt ochre** | Drawn from Mangalagiri handloom, used sparingly and only on the numbers |

**Reasoned.** A palette taken from the district reads as belonging to it — which matters for a segment (S1, S4, S5) whose scepticism is partly about outsiders arriving to sell them their own land back. And it rules out the category default of navy-and-gold, which signals a generic luxury tier the price sheet does not support.

### 7.3 Typography

**The numbers are the typography.** Set large, set alone, set in a face with genuine authority in figures. Body copy in a Telugu-and-Latin pair that sets both scripts at equal weight — not Telugu as a translated afterthought at 80% size, which is what most of the corridor's advertising does and which reads exactly as it is.

**A production rule:** the number is never smaller than the words around it.

### 7.4 Photography style

**Documentary. Natural light. Real people from this catchment. No models.**

| Do | Do not |
| --- | --- |
| **Photograph the space between buildings** — that is the product | Photograph the buildings as objects |
| Shoot in real seasons, including a hot April and a wet August | Shoot one permanent golden hour |
| Real families from Vijayawada, Guntur and Mangalagiri, cast locally | Aspirational-looking models, or families of exactly four |
| Wide frames that include ground and sky | Tight lifestyle vignettes that could be anywhere |
| Show the scheme with people in it — untidy, mid-life, in use | Show empty perfection |
| Photograph the AIIMS building, factually | Imply an association that isn't stated |

**Reasoned.** Phase 5 found S1 visits with three to six family members and S6 with children — the audience is families in motion, and the imagery should look like the site on a Sunday, because that is when they will see it. **Empty, perfect, golden-hour photography sets up a disappointment the site visit then has to survive.**

### 7.5 CGI

Unavoidable pre-construction, and the place where the category's credibility collapses fastest. **Three rules:**

1. **Every CGI carries a visible label and a date.** *Artist's impression, March 2027. Trees shown at eight years.*
2. **Skies are photographed from the site, not generated.**
3. **Every CGI has a real-photograph counterpart published beside it as construction proceeds** — the same frame, same angle, month by month. **The comparison becomes pillar 2's proof asset**, and the discipline of knowing the real photograph is coming keeps the CGI honest.

## 8. Film direction

### 8.1 The launch film — *The Count*

**One shot. Ninety seconds. No music.**

Open tight on a single window in Community A, at the height of a person standing at it. The camera begins to rise and pull back. In the top-left corner, a counter reads **1**.

As the frame widens, the counter climbs — 12, 40, 96 — and the roofs accumulate. At **169** the counter pauses on *Community A · twenty-six to the acre*. It resumes. At **253**, *B · twenty-four*. At **313**, *C · ten*. At **376**, the frame finally holds all twenty-five acres, and the counter stops.

Then, after a beat, a second number fades up beside it: **874**.

Cut to black. Two lines:

> **We could have built eight hundred and seventy-four.**
> **Fifteen to the acre. Everything else is open.**

**Sound design:** ambient only. Birds, distance, a scooter somewhere. **No orchestral swell** — the film's whole argument is that the project does not need to persuade you, and a score would contradict it.

**Reasoned — why this film and not a lifestyle film.** It is the master idea executed literally rather than symbolically. It is impossible for a competitor to remake, because remaking it requires their counter to reach a number that defeats them. And it is the only film in the category that a buyer might watch twice to check the arithmetic.

### 8.2 The supporting films

| Film | Length | Job | Segment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **The Hospital** | 3 min | KMV Projects built AIIMS Mangalagiri. Shot at the building, with the people who built it. No claim beyond the fact | S1, S2, S3, S7 |
| **Twelve Minutes** | 90 sec | A single unbroken drive from the AIIMS gate to the site, in real time, timestamped, at 8am on a working Tuesday | **S2 above all** — Phase 5: they want the commute *measured*, not described |
| **The Walkthrough** | 6–8 min | Room by room, specification by specification, in one take, with a person talking. Filmed per community | **S3 and S8** — the partial substitute for a site visit |
| **The Month** | 60 sec, monthly | Construction progress from three fixed camera positions, unedited | All — delivery anxiety, the concern shared by all eight segments |
| **The Neighbours** | 2–4 min each | Owners, in their own words, in Telugu, unscripted | **S1** — the answer to *"who else has bought?"* |

**Production discipline:** the walkthrough and progress films are **operational assets, not campaigns.** They must be produced on a schedule by a small permanent crew, not commissioned as a shoot. Phase 9 put the cost of that inside the ₹1.25 crore brand-content line.

## 9. The digital creative system

**A modular kit, not a set of layouts.** Four communities × two languages × six formats is 48 permutations before a single message changes — a system is the only way to produce that without it degrading.

**The constant across every asset:** the number, top-left, at a fixed size relative to the frame. It is the brand's recognition device, the way a logo usually is.

| Layer | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| **The number** | Community density or master density. Fixed position, fixed relative scale, ochre |
| **The claim** | One line, ≤ 8 words, Telugu and English set at equal weight |
| **The proof** | One countable fact, small, bottom-left. Never an adjective |
| **The image** | Plan view, the space between buildings, or documentary people. **Never a facade hero** |
| **The mark** | Bottom-right. RERA number always adjacent to it |
| **The CTA** | Verb + object. *See the price sheet.* *Time the drive.* *Count the homes.* |

**The website** is the verification surface (Phase 9). Its structure follows pillar 5: the price sheet is a page, not a download form. The RERA file is a page. The construction feed is a page. **Nothing that a buyer would use to check the project is gated behind a lead form** — Phase 9 established that gating price is what sends a buyer to a competitor who publishes it.

**Landing pages: one per community, never one per campaign.** The community is the unit of interest (Phase 8, Stage 3), so a buyer who arrives from a Community D ad must land on Community D — with its own number, its own narrative and its own price sheet.

## 10. The performance creative system

**Different discipline, same rules.** Performance creative must stop a thumb; brand creative must survive a second viewing. Both must answer *what is the number?*

**The test matrix — 4 communities × 3 hook types × 2 languages = 24 live variants**, refreshed monthly.

| Hook type | Construction | Example, Community C |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The number hook** | Lead with the count | *"Ten homes to the acre. The corridor average is sixty-one."* |
| **The objection hook** | State the buyer's own doubt first | *"There is a cheaper villa ten minutes from here. Here is what it doesn't have."* |
| **The proof hook** | Lead with the credential or document | *"Built by the people who built AIIMS Mangalagiri. Here is the price sheet."* |

**Measurement, per Phase 9:** variants are judged on **cost per qualified lead, not cost per lead.** A hook that produces cheap leads which fail qualification is producing the volume Phase 8 valued least — and the Meta budget will optimise straight into it unless the objective is set correctly.

**One creative rule specific to this plan.** Phase 7 found Community B consumes 65% of the lead-generation budget and its binding constraint is the site-visit stage, not lead cost. **So B's performance creative should not optimise for leads at all — it should optimise for booked video walkthroughs.** That is a different conversion event, a different creative, and a different call to action: *"Walk it with us on Saturday morning, Eastern Time."*

## 11. Outdoor

**₹2.10 crore, the largest brand line (Phase 9), on a corridor where the target audience drives the same road every day.**

**The constraint that shapes everything:** a hoarding on NH-16 is read at 80 km/h in about two seconds. That is one number and up to four words.

### 11.1 The sequence

A run of six hoardings along the Vijayawada–Guntur corridor and the Mangalagiri bypass, read in order by anyone driving toward the site. Each carries a single figure, unexplained, in the same typeface at the same size:

> **113.** &nbsp;&nbsp;→&nbsp;&nbsp; **78.** &nbsp;&nbsp;→&nbsp;&nbsp; **66.** &nbsp;&nbsp;→&nbsp;&nbsp; **53.** &nbsp;&nbsp;→&nbsp;&nbsp; **15.** &nbsp;&nbsp;→&nbsp;&nbsp; *the site entrance*

The first four are the observed densities of real corridor projects (Phase 2 §5), unattributed. **The fifth is this one.** The sixth board, at the gate, resolves it:

> **Homes to the acre.**
> **You just drove past the difference.**

**Reasoned — why this works and what it risks.** It is the only outdoor idea available that uses the medium's one real strength — sequence over distance — and it makes the competitive argument without naming anyone, which keeps it legally clean. **The risk is that an unexplained number reads as noise to a first-time viewer.** The mitigation is frequency: this is a two-year buy on a route the target audience drives daily, and the payoff board is at the destination.

### 11.2 The standing boards

Away from the sequence, single boards carry one claim each, rotated quarterly:

- **376 homes. 874 would have fit.**
- **Fifteen to the acre. Everything else is open.**
- **The people who built AIIMS built this.**
- **The price sheet is on the website. All of it.**
- **2,904 sq ft of this land belongs to each home.**

**None of them contains an adjective.** That is the point, and it is what will make them look unlike every other board on the road.

## 12. The campaign in three phases

**Timing is set by three dated facts, not by convention:** the pay-revision arrears window (S5), the December–January and summer diaspora travel windows (S3, S7, S8), and the fact that referral cannot exist in year one (Phase 9 §6).

### 12.1 Pre-launch — months −6 to 0

**Objective: exist, verifiably, before anyone is asked to buy.**

| Workstream | What runs | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Seed Community D** | No media. Fifteen named local households approached directly, through introductions | Phase 6's rule. Phase 5: S1 buys the other buyers. **This is the single most important pre-launch activity and it involves no advertising at all** |
| **Land the credential** | PR placing the AIIMS build and the group's airport work in traceable third-party coverage | Phase 4 found search visibility thin. **When Journey 2's referred buyer searches the name, the results must already exist** |
| **Build the site** | Experience centre, two show homes, the two-model comparison, the counted walk | ₹4.10 Cr, and it must be finished *before* launch, not during |
| **Publish** | Website live with the full price sheet, RERA file and specification, ungated | Pillar 5. It also builds the organic authority SEO cannot produce quickly (Phase 9) |
| **Recruit partners** | Channel-partner network stood up and briefed | Phase 9: year one over-weights partners, saving ₹1.41 Cr against media |
| **Land-owner relations** | Village-level work in Telugu, exchange term sheet drafted | Journey 4. 6–24 month cycle — **it must start now to deliver in year three** |

### 12.2 Launch — months 0 to 6

**Objective: make fifteen to the acre a known number in this catchment.**

- **The Count** releases across YouTube, OTT and cinema in Vijayawada and Guntur.
- **The hoarding sequence** goes live on the corridor — all six boards simultaneously, because the sequence only works whole.
- **Print** takes the launch moment: a full page carrying the brand narrative (§3) in Telugu, entire, with no image. **A page of text in a category of pictures is the most conspicuous thing available**, and it is the only format that can carry the argument at length to S1's household influencers.
- **Performance** opens on Communities A and C — A because it needs velocity and cash flow, C because it is the most efficient product in the scheme and the earliest test of the premium thesis.
- **Community B holds.** Its first flight waits for the December window.
- **Community D still runs no media.**

### 12.3 Post-launch — months 6 to 45

**Objective: convert proof into velocity, and buyers into the next campaign.**

| Flight | When | What |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The Arrears window** | On the pay-revision timeline | Community A only. *"Your arrears are the down payment."* The working shown, not asserted |
| **The December window** | Dec–Jan, every year | Community B and D to S3 and S7. Concentrated, not spread. **Phase 8: spending evenly across twelve months on an audience that can transact in four is the most avoidable waste in the plan** |
| **The Summer window** | May–June | The second diaspora flight, plus S8's annual-leave window |
| **The Month films** | Monthly, permanently | Construction progress. Delivery anxiety is the one concern shared by all eight segments |
| **The Neighbours** | From month 12 | Owner films and milestone events. **This is when Community D's media finally starts**, because by then the fifteen names exist |
| **The Comparison** | From month 18 | The CGI-versus-photograph pairs, published as construction reaches each frame |

> **Reasoned — the shape of it.** The campaign gets *quieter* and more evidential over time, not louder. Year one argues from arithmetic because that is all it has. Year three argues from a built place and the people in it, which is stronger. **A plan that front-loads emotion and back-loads proof has it exactly the wrong way round for a market whose defining anxiety is delivery.**

## 13. Site experience

**₹4.10 crore — the largest single line in the plan, serving the stage worth ₹39.4 crore a point.** This is where creative strategy stops being communication and becomes staging.

### 13.1 The five moments

**1 · The drive in.** The approach road is the first execution. A single marker at the turn-off with the drive time to AIIMS on it. **The host times the drive with the visitor on the way in** — Phase 5 found S2 wants it measured.

**2 · The two models.** Two physical masterplan models, side by side, built to the same scale. The first is this scheme at fifteen to the acre. **The second is a corridor-typical block at a hundred and thirteen.** No commentary needed; the models are the argument. **Reasoned: this is the cheapest high-impact element in the entire ₹4.10 crore line, and the one a competitor cannot answer without building a model that defeats them.**

**3 · The counted walk.** A marked route through the site with numbered posts. A visitor can walk it and count the homes. **The brand's central claim is the only one in the corridor that survives being checked on foot** — so the site invites exactly that.

**4 · The show homes.** One in C, one in D — the two communities carrying 53% of revenue. Furnished, lived-in, not staged empty. **A Telugu-speaking host, always.**

**5 · The wall.** A permanent wall listing every household that has bought, by name, with permission. Phase 5: S1's first question is *"who else has bought?"* — **the wall answers it before it is asked.** It is also the referral programme's most persuasive asset and costs almost nothing.

### 13.2 The capacity rule

**Phase 8 §5.1:** S1, S5 and S6 all visit on Sundays, and S1 arrives unannounced with three to six family members. Roughly **eleven parties and 35–45 people on a peak Sunday.**

> **The experience centre is sized for peak Sunday, not for the monthly average.** And S2 and S3 — who visit by appointment and by video — are actively routed to weekday evenings, to keep Sunday capacity for the families who arrive without warning. **Under-sizing this caps the highest-leverage stage in the plan, and no amount of upstream media can push more people through a constrained Sunday.**

## 14. Sales collateral

**Governed by pillar 5: publish what competitors gate.**

| Piece | Content | Segment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **The price sheet** | Every unit, every rate, every all-in figure including registration. **Ungated, on the website and in the hand** | S5, S6, S2 — Phase 5: *"will ask for the all-in number, and should be given it"* |
| **The arithmetic sheet** | One page. 25 acres → 376 homes → 15/acre → 2,904 sq ft per home → 874 that would have fit | All. The master idea, portable |
| **The specification** | Materials, brands, tolerances. Written as a schedule, not as prose | S2, S1 |
| **The payment structures** | Construction-linked and possession-linked plans, side by side, with EMI working | **S2** — Phase 5: clears the EMI, struggles with the cash. **A plan is worth more to them than a discount** |
| **The remote-purchase file** | PoA, FEMA, NRE/NRO, the step-by-step of buying without flying | **S3** |
| **The exchange term sheet** | Land-for-home structures, legally drafted | **S4** — Phase 5: *"responds to a document, not to advertising"* |
| **The comparison sheet** | This project against three named corridor projects, on density, land per home, parcel size and price. **Accurate, including where we lose** | S6, S2 — the segments who will build this sheet themselves anyway |

> **Reasoned — the comparison sheet is the risky one and the most valuable.** Publishing a competitor comparison that admits Prime Grandeur is cheaper per square foot costs a small credibility loss on price and buys a large one on honesty, with an audience Phase 5 characterised as verifiers. **Every one of these buyers is going to make this comparison. The only question is whether they make it with our numbers or someone else's.**

## 15. NRI communication

**Two segments, not one.** Phase 5's finding, and Phase 9 reclassified "NRI marketing" as an audience discipline rather than a channel for exactly this reason.

| | **S3 · US and developed markets** | **S8 · Gulf** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Buys** | Communities C and D — ₹1.7–2.7 Cr | Communities A and B — ₹59 L–1.45 Cr |
| **Language** | English | **Telugu** |
| **Format** | Long video, documents, LinkedIn, email | **WhatsApp, vertical video, voice notes** |
| **The message** | *A home that doesn't need you.* Lock-and-leave, managed, remote purchase | *A home she can manage while you're away.* Security, school access, possession certainty |
| **Who is actually being addressed** | The buyer, **and the relative in India who will inspect it** — who holds effective veto and is almost never who the media targets | **The spouse in India**, who will live there and who decides |
| **The window** | December–January, and summer | **Annual leave** |
| **The objection** | *"How do I know this is real?"* — answered with the document file, not reassurance | *"Can my wife handle this alone?"* — answered with security specification and a named contact |
| **The competitor** | **Hyderabad**, not another project | Rented accommodation, and time |

> **Reasoned — the single most actionable NRI insight in this exercise.** Phase 5 found the inspecting relative in India holds veto and is *"almost never the person the marketing is aimed at."* **Marketing to that relative is local media, at local CPL, against a lead that behaves like a resident.** It is the cheapest available fix for Community B's economics — the community consuming 65% of the lead-generation budget — and it requires a creative asset nobody in the corridor produces: **a proxy-visit pack, written for the cousin who has been asked to go and look.**

## 16. The cliché register

The brief said to avoid generic real-estate clichés **unless research proves they work.** Both halves, answered.

### 16.1 Cut, with the reason

| Device | Why it is cut |
| --- | --- |
| **"Luxury redefined" and every superlative** | Established: *superlatives without evidence* is the named failure mode of Indian real-estate advertising. And Phase 5: ₹2.74 Cr is an upper-affluent ticket, not a high-net-worth one — **the word promises a tier the price sheet does not deliver** |
| **"Live your dream" and lifestyle abstraction** | Same source names *general lifestyle statements* as the second failure. It also fails the swap test: it fits any project in India |
| **The amenity icon grid** | Phase 5 found amenity lists rank **low** for S1 and near-irrelevant for S3, who will not use them. It is a page of claims with no numbers |
| **Sunset silhouettes, slow-motion children, the family of exactly four** | Fails the swap test completely. And it sets an expectation the site visit must then survive |
| **Orchestral CGI flythroughs** | The category's least credible format, in a market whose defining anxiety is delivery. **Replaced by CGI that is labelled, dated and paired with the real photograph** |
| **"Nestled in the heart of…"** | There is a measured distance available. Use it |
| **Manufactured urgency — countdown timers, "3 units left!"** | Phase 5: S1 takes 3–9 months, S3 takes 6–18. **Urgency theatre aimed at buyers on that timeline reads as desperation**, and it is the tell of a project that is not selling |
| **A festive-season launch calendar** | Phase 8: ₹155.4 crore of demand buys in **December–January and summer travel windows**, not Dasara–Diwali |

### 16.2 Kept, because the research supports it

**Four conventional devices survive the test — and it matters that they are kept for stated reasons rather than out of habit.**

| Device | The evidence |
| --- | --- |
| **Vaastu compliance, stated as specification** | Phase 5, S1: *"Vaastu compliance is close to non-negotiable."* **Kept as a spec line — orientation, entrance, kitchen placement — not as a mystical claim.** The device is supported; the mysticism is not |
| **Scarcity** | Phase 2 found **no villa community above ~70 units** in the corridor; Phase 6 made villa scarcity the deliberate pricing mechanism. **The scarcity here is a fact, not a technique** — so it is stated as a number (*sixty villas; there is no phase two on this land*) rather than as a countdown |
| **Family imagery** | Phase 5: S6's driver is *doing better for the children*; S1's is legacy. **Supported — but documentary and locally cast**, which is what separates it from the cliché |
| **Print at launch** | Phase 5 found print retains real credibility with S5 and S1, and reaches the household influencers — the father, the eldest brother — that no digital channel reaches. **Kept, and used unconventionally: a full page of text with no image** |

> **The distinction that runs through this whole register.** A cliché is not a device that is common. **It is a device used without evidence.** Vaastu, scarcity, family imagery and print are all category conventions — and all four have specific support in this catchment's research. Superlatives, lifestyle abstraction and amenity grids do not, in this market, for these buyers.

## 17. Risks

| # | Risk | Why it matters | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **The number is too cold for S1 and S7** | 30% and 11% of revenue respectively, both driven by standing, roots and legacy rather than arithmetic | Test the launch film and two print routes with 20–30 respondents from S1 before production. **The narrative in §3 is the warm route; if arithmetic alone under-performs, it leads instead** |
| **2** | **The hoarding sequence reads as noise** | ₹2.10 Cr, the largest brand line, on an idea that requires a viewer to see boards in order | The payoff board sits at the gate, and it is a two-year buy on a daily commute. **Prompted recall of the number "fifteen" at three months is the test** |
| **3** | **The comparison sheet is used against us** | Naming Prime Grandeur invites a competitor response | It is already in the conversation (Phase 5: brokers name it). **The comparison must be scrupulously accurate, including where we lose on rate** — a single unfair line destroys the pillar |
| **4** | **CGI-versus-photograph pairing backfires** | If the built result is worse than the render, the brand has published the evidence against itself | **That is the point, and it is the discipline.** Publishing the pair from month one is what forces the render to be honest. Do not start the series unless the intention is to finish it |
| **5** | **Telugu treated as translation** | Half the audience, and the segment least served by the corridor's existing advertising | Telugu is written first for S1, S4, S5 and S8 material — **not translated from English.** Equal type weight is a production rule, checked at approval |
| **6** | **The site is not ready at launch** | The whole strategy routes to a site visit worth ₹39.4 Cr a point | The experience centre, two show homes and the two models are **pre-launch deliverables**, not launch-phase ones. If they slip, the launch slips |
| **7** | **Phase 6's prices do not hold** | Unchanged since Phase 6: **31% of revenue sits in a band nobody has proven is occupied** | Not a creative question. **No idea, however well made, sells a price the market refuses** |

## 18. Carry-forward

**The idea.**
> **The most valuable thing on these twenty-five acres is what isn't on them.**
> **Fifteen to the acre. Everything else is open.**

**Why it holds.** 376 homes where 874 would fit at the corridor's own lowest density. **2,904 sq ft of site per home against 384 at Manjeera Monarch — 7.6×.** ₹344–500 crore of gross development value deliberately forgone. Every figure computable from public data, and **no competitor in the corridor can say the line without stating a number that defeats them.**

**The system.** Four communities, four numbers — **26 · 24 · 10 · 7**, averaging the master 15. It was not invented for the campaign; it fell out of Phase 6's land model.

**The five pillars.** The Number · The Builder · The Open · The Neighbours · The Document.

**The production rule that governs everything.** *Every claim must survive the question "what is the number?"* If a line cannot answer it, the line does not run.

**Four things this phase decides that a conventional creative plan would not.**
1. **The hero image is a plan view, not a facade.** Density is only legible from above, and it is the entire proposition.
2. **Community D runs no paid media in year one.** Its creative is a list of fifteen names.
3. **The price sheet is published ungated**, and a comparison against three named competitors is published with it — including where this project loses on rate.
4. **The campaign gets quieter and more evidential over time**, because year three has a built place and real neighbours, and those are stronger arguments than any film.

**Four clichés kept, with evidence** — Vaastu as specification, scarcity as a number, documentary family imagery, and a full page of text in print at launch. **Eight cut**, each with the finding that rules it out.

**Open.** The seven risks in §17 — of which risk 1 is worth testing before production (₹5–8 lakh of qualitative work against a ₹6.30 crore brand budget), and risk 7 is what it has been since Phase 6: **not a creative question, and larger than all of them.**

§0

How to read this

Why the evidence comes before the strategy, and what the grades mean.

The Amaravati corridor is the most narrative-heavy real-estate market in India right now. Almost every number in circulation originates from a party with a position — a broker, a developer, a political actor, or a government promoting an investment case. A marketing strategy built on that ambient narrative would inherit its errors.

So this document does one job: separate what is true and load-bearing from what is announced, projected, or sold. Where the evidence does not support a claim, that is recorded as a finding, not smoothed over.

Three source-quality caveats that apply throughout

Listing portals report asking prices, not transactions. In a market where sellers are anchored to a capital-city narrative, asking prices run ahead of clearing prices. Treat every ₹/sq ft figure in §6 as an upper bound on realisable price.

Much of the available "market data" is published by brokers who profit from the numbers being high. The most prolific publishers of Amaravati price data are interested parties. Their figures are used only where a directionally consistent second source exists, and are flagged where relied on.

This research was conducted through search-surfaced reporting. Direct retrieval of primary documents — APCRDA orders, IGRS registration data, RERA filings, agency city reports — was not possible. §12 lists exactly which primary sources a real project would need. Nothing here substitutes for that diligence.

The four strongest price signals

Portal averages are not the best evidence available. These are, because they are transactions or official rates:

  1. APCRDA's own allotment rates — ₹8.20 crore/acre for star-hotel and private-school land; ₹4.10 crore/acre for 33-year school leases. Prices the state itself transacts at.
  2. The government's auction reserve — ₹10 crore/acre offset for 248 acres.
  3. The February 2026 guideline-value revision — the state raising official rates explicitly to close the gap with real transaction prices.
  4. Institutional land commitments — KIMS taking a 60-year lease on 25 acres at Nidamarru is a balance-sheet decision, not an announcement.
§1

What actually changed, and when

Four events since mid-2024 changed the corridor's risk profile. They are of very different weight, and conflating them is the most common analytical error in this market.

The political reversal — June 2024 Confirmed

The TDP-led NDA returned to power having campaigned on restoring Amaravati as sole capital. Between 2019 and 2024 the previous government pursued a three-capital model and construction was substantially halted; the three-capital legislation was withdrawn in 2022 with no replacement enacted before the election.

This matters because it establishes the precedent: this project has already been stopped once by an election. That is the central political risk (§9.1).

The relaunch — 2 May 2025 Confirmed

Capital construction was formally relaunched with the Prime Minister laying foundations for works valued at over ₹58,000 crore.

The statutory settlement — 6 April 2026 Confirmed

The Union Government notified the Andhra Pradesh Reorganisation (Amendment) Act, 2026 in the Gazette of India on 6 April 2026, designating Amaravati the sole and permanent capital, with retrospective effect from 2 June 2024. The Lok Sabha passed the Bill on 1 April, the Rajya Sabha on 2 April, and the President assented before gazetting.

The single most important fact in this document

Until April 2026, Amaravati's status was state policy — reversible by a state government with a majority, as had already happened once. It is now central statute. Reversal would require an Act of Parliament.

The practical effect on a buyer's risk calculus is specific: the question changes from "will this be the capital?" to "how fast will it get built?" Those are different questions with different answers, and the second is far easier to sell against.

The money — 2024 to 2026

The structural feature that matters: both multilateral loans are results-based instruments. Disbursement is tied to achieved milestones, not elapsed time. That is a genuine discipline — money is harder to divert and progress harder to fake — but it also means a slowdown in execution directly throttles the funding. The two are coupled, in both directions.

What has not changed

Amaravati is still, physically, mostly a construction site. Reporting through the revival describes half-built and deserted high-rise complexes, incomplete utilities, and minimal residential occupancy — secretariat staff commuting in and leaving. Work on 73 bungalows, 288 MLA/MLC quarters and 144 bureaucrats' units was abandoned at 70–80% completion during the hiatus and is being resumed. Population of the capital city area is around 100,000, against a master plan targeting 3.5 million by 2050.

The honest reading

The decision has been de-risked. The place has not been built. A residential product launching now is selling into the gap between those two facts — and the entire marketing problem is how to price and narrate that gap without either overclaiming or apologising for it.

The structural insight

The Vijayawada–Tadepalli–Mangalagiri–Guntur agglomeration had a population of approximately 3.3 million as of 2017. That agglomeration exists, earns, and buys homes today — entirely independent of whether Amaravati is completed on schedule.

A project here can be underwritten on the existing agglomeration and treat Amaravati as upside — or underwritten on Amaravati and treat the agglomeration as incidental. The first is a business. The second is a bet.

Material finding — the schedule slippage

Balance works on the MLA/MLC/AIS residential towers were reported at 58.14% physical progress against a target of 78.56%, with financial progress at 57.48% — roughly 20 percentage points behind plan on the government's own flagship residential build.

This is the most useful single data point in the section, and it cuts against the prevailing narrative. Any marketing implying "Amaravati is arriving on schedule" is contradicted by the government's own progress reporting.

A claim surfaced in research that High Court and Secretariat tower construction was "already completed as of October 2024" directly contradicts the December 2028 High Court deadline and the observed state of the site. Graded Speculative / unreliable. Not used.

The ORR deserves specific caution

It is the most frequently cited item in Amaravati property marketing and the least advanced. A 189.9 km, ₹24,791 crore expressway just entering land acquisition across five districts is realistically a decade from full operation — Hyderabad's shorter 158 km ORR took roughly that long. Any material implying ORR proximity as a present-day amenity is selling a road that does not exist. Its legitimate use is as a labelled long-horizon land argument.

The under-discussed asset

The Western Bypass is nearly finished, carrying traffic, and its southern terminus lands at Chinakakani/Kaza — inside the Mangalagiri belt. It is real infrastructure delivering real time savings today, and it is barely used in local marketing. A genuine, defensible connectivity claim available now — and a significant input to §11.

Rail

The Errupalem–Amaravati–Nambur line — 56.53 km broad gauge, ₹2,047–2,600 crore, nine new stations, a 3 km bridge over the Krishna between Kothapeta and Vaddamanu — is Approved with land acquisition in progress: notification issued 18 June 2024, Section 20A issued and 20E underway, a further 300 acres notified across eight villages in Veerullapadu and Kanchikacherla mandals. A new Amaravati railway station is Proposed under second-phase pooling.

Sequential land acquisition notifications are a reliable indicator of real intent — far more so than press announcements. But the line is years from operation. Meanwhile the existing Vijayawada–Guntur line, with a functioning station at Mangalagiri Confirmed, is a present-day asset the corridor's marketing consistently ignores in favour of the unbuilt one.

Metro and public transport

The corridor's most common factual error

"Metro connectivity" is routinely cited in Amaravati property marketing. The approved Phase I metro does not go to Amaravati.

Vijayawada Metro Phase I — ₹11,009 crore total, 38.40 km, 34 stations — runs Gannavaram to PNBS and PNBS to Penamaluru, entirely within Vijayawada and toward the airport. Bids for a ₹4,150 crore initial civil contract were invited 28 July 2025 Approved. The Amaravati corridor is a later, unfunded phase Proposed.

Marketing an approved Vijayawada metro as an Amaravati amenity is not aggressive positioning — it is a misstatement of fact, and the kind of claim that becomes a RERA and consumer-protection liability once a buyer checks. It is also unnecessary: 750 e-buses sanctioned for AP under PM e-Bus Sewa Tender-II include Amaravati, Vijayawada and Guntur Approved, and the BRTS corridor inside the NH-16 arterial is Under construction. Both are real.

Utilities

Trunk services — storm-water drains, water supply, sewerage, utility ducts, reuse water lines, STPs — are being tendered and built alongside the road network, water sourced from the Krishna. Under construction, partial.

For a private developer this is a live commercial question, not a background detail: a 25-acre scheme delivering before trunk services reach the site carries its own STP, water and power-backup capex, and that cost must sit in the project model from day one rather than emerging as a variation later.

§4

Employment drivers

A residential market needs people with incomes and a reason to live nearby. This section separates payroll that exists from payroll that has been announced.

Government — the anchor, and its real size

The 2016 relocation from Hyderabad involved approximately 25,000 employees across 115 directorates and 32 departments; the Secretariat itself ~2,500 across ~500 sections Confirmed for 2016. No reliable 2026 headcount surfaced. A financial city cluster has foundation stones laid for 15 public sector banks and insurance companies, stated employment potential 6,570 Approved. 72 institutions have been allotted 948 acres, with the Chief Minister directing completion in 2.5–3 years and no extensions.

Government employment is the most durable driver — it does not leave in a downturn — but it is the wrong income profile for the top of a premium product. A ₹3 crore villa is not bought on a state government salary. Government is the reason the corridor has a floor; it is not the reason it has a ceiling. The commercially relevant read: government drives 2BHK/3BHK demand, and it drives the services economy — the lawyers, contractors, consultants and traders who service a capital. That secondary economy is where the premium buyers come from, and it is systematically under-analysed here.

Healthcare

The highest-quality forward signal available

KIMS Hospitals has taken a 60-year lease with APCRDA over 25 acres near Nidamarru, Mangalagiri mandal — 23 acres for the KIMS Skill University (medical college, 1,000-bed teaching hospital, nursing, pharmacy, dentistry) plus 2 acres for a 500-bed super-specialty hospital. Groundbreaking performed by the Chief Minister; completion expected in 2–3 years. Approved

Press releases are free. Sixty-year leases are not.

Its location is not incidental. Read alongside SRM at Neerukonda, AIIMS at Mangalagiri and NRI at Chinakakani, it shows sophisticated institutional capital repeatedly choosing the Mangalagiri mandal belt rather than the capital seed area — a revealed preference by parties with better information and longer horizons than any residential developer.

Technology — the largest gap between narrative and payroll

The Amaravati Quantum Valley Tech Park is a 50-acre development anchored on an intended IBM Quantum System Two with a 156-qubit Heron processor, with TCS, L&T, LTIMindtree and Microsoft attached — ₹40 crore government plus ₹200 crore private investment Under construction. It was targeted operational 1 January 2026; the foundation stone was laid 7 February 2026 — the build began after the date it was meant to open.

Two corrections that recur constantly in this market

"90,000 professionals" / "100,000 jobs by 2026" Speculative — against a 50-acre park with ₹240 crore committed. The arithmetic does not support it, and the claim collapses under the first informed question.

Google's USD 15 billion, ~600-acre, 1 GW AI data centre is in Visakhapatnam — roughly 350 km away — not Amaravati. Using it would be a straightforward misrepresentation.

The genuinely defensible technology claim is smaller and much more solid: India's first quantum computing park is being built in this capital, with IBM, TCS, L&T and Microsoft attached. That is impressive on its own and survives scrutiny.

State investment pipeline — how much to discount

Since June 2024 the State Investment Promotion Board has approved 329 projects worth ₹11.77 lakh crore with a stated potential of 10.32 lakh jobs Approved. The CII Partnership Summit 2025 produced MoUs with 400 companies, over ₹11 lakh crore Reported. A claim that AP accounts for 25.3% of all investment proposed in India in the first nine months of 2026 is state-sourced Speculative.

MoU-to-commissioned-project conversion in Indian state investment summits is historically low — the state has itself acknowledged the gap by constituting a monitoring committee. These figures indicate genuine momentum and genuine investor attention. They should not be converted into projected homebuyers. Discount heavily.

Commercial and retail

Mangalagiri holds Mayuri Tech Park — the first IT park in the Vijayawada–Guntur area, ~4 lakh sq ft, with reported Q4 2025 price movement from ₹5,000 to ₹7,500/sq ft. Retail remains concentrated in Vijayawada: PVP Square, LEPL Centro, Trendset, LEPL Icon. The capital's own hospitality layer is at tender stage — 9.19 acres for three 3-star and two 4-star hotels, 15.80 acres for five private schools on 33-year leases Approved.

For a residential product launching now, Vijayawada is the lifestyle infrastructure — and access to it is a live selling point in a way that in-capital amenity is not yet.

The 3.5 million figure, checked

It is a planning input, not a forecast — the capacity the master plan was designed to hold. Reaching it requires roughly 3.4 million net in-migrants over 24 years, or ~142,000 a year. For scale: Amaravati adding more than its entire current population, every year, for a quarter century. Not impossible; not a base case; and never something to present to a buyer as an expectation.

The defensible demographic claim is different and stronger: there is already a 3.3 million-person agglomeration here, it is urbanising, it has a fast-growing airport, and it is now the permanent seat of state government by Act of Parliament. That claim does not need a 2050 projection to work.

Migration evidence is thin. No reliable current in-migration data surfaced. With residential population still ~100,000 and secretariat staff reported to commute rather than live locally, the honest position is that large-scale in-migration has not yet begun. A genuine evidence gap (§12).

The distinction that governs everything downstream

Land re-rated. Housing did not.

The dramatic price movement — the reported post-relaunch doubling, the ₹23,000–70,000/sq yd plot range, the ₹8.20 crore/acre allotments — is in land, quoted per square yard or acre. That is the unit of land speculation.

Built residential product tells a much calmer story. Mangalagiri apartments drifted down from ₹4,098 to ₹4,062/sq ft between June and September 2025 — during the same window capital land was reportedly re-rating. Tadepalli apartments appreciated 2.99%, roughly at or below inflation. Only Tadepalli villas at +8.39% show real momentum in built product.

  1. The apartment market here is not hot. It is stable and competitive. Sub-₹5,000/sq ft apartments in Mangalagiri are a volume business, not a margin business.
  2. The villa market is where genuine demand pressure exists — appreciating nearly 3× faster than apartments in the corridor's most established premium micro-market. This directly validates a villa component.
  3. Land speculation and home buying are different markets with different customers. Someone buying a ₹30 lakh plot for appreciation is not a candidate for a ₹2.5 crore villa. Any strategy treating land momentum as evidence of housing demand is reasoning from the wrong dataset.

A blended ~33,000 sq ft per acre is consistent with roughly a 50:50 to 60:40 land split between apartments and villas — exactly what a "2BHK through premium residences and villas" spectrum across four communities implies. The brief is internally coherent. Now the uncomfortable part.

The commercial problem, stated in one line

The job is not to sell 25 acres of homes. It is to manufacture and defend a price premium of roughly 45% over the surrounding market, sustained across four communities and a full product spectrum, for the duration of the sell-out.

Volume marketing does not do that. Positioning, product differentiation, brand credibility and pricing discipline do.

Three routes exist, and they are not equally good

  1. Justify the premium — through location, product, design, amenity, certification and brand. Highest margin, highest marketing dependency, and the only route that also builds a durable brand asset. This is the route the evidence supports.
  2. Build more density — push toward 40,000+ sq ft/acre so ₹500 crore is achievable nearer ₹5,000/sq ft. Lowers the marketing burden but compresses the villa component, which is the segment with real demand pressure. Trades margin and differentiation for volume risk.
  3. Phase the ₹500 crore over 5–7 years — treat it as programme GDV, not launch pricing, and let appreciation close part of the gap. Tadepalli's 3-year +34.2% makes this plausible. Most likely the realistic answer — and it should be stated explicitly rather than assumed.

Transaction costs and finance

Stamp duty 5% + registration 1% + transfer duty 1.5% = 7.5% of the higher of consideration or guideline value. The 1 February 2026 guideline revision therefore raised actual buyer cost as well as recorded values — a real, if modest, affordability drag that took effect this year. Home loan rates in the region start around 7.15–7.35% p.a., LTV to 90%, tenures to 30 years — a supportive rate environment.

§7

Who is actually buying

Demand structure — and why the argument that is moving plots cannot be the argument that sells this product.

Investor versus end-user

The evidence points to a market currently dominated by investors, not end-users: price movement concentrated in land not homes; buyer interest characterised as "NRIs and institutional investors" treating Amaravati as "Hyderabad in its early phase"; preference shifts toward plotted developments and CRDA-approved layouts — the classic signature of appreciation-seeking capital; and a residential population still ~100,000 with staff commuting rather than relocating.

The demand thesis

A ₹500 crore development spanning 2BHKs to villas is fundamentally an end-user product. It cannot be sold on the argument moving plots, and should not try — a finished home is a worse pure appreciation vehicle than raw land (construction depreciates; land does not), so competing on appreciation means competing on the one dimension where the product is structurally weaker.

Amaravati does not have to motivate the purchase. It has to de-risk it.

The statutory settlement removed the reason people were not buying homes here. The motivation comes from where it always does in a Tier-2 Indian market — space, schools, safety, status, and an upgrade from a cramped city apartment.

"NRI" is not one segment, and treating it as one is a common and expensive error. US-based Telugu NRIs skew toward high-income technology professionals — genuine candidates for premium residences and villas. Gulf-based NRIs skew toward remittance-led, mid-income profiles — candidates for 2BHK and 3BHK product, often bought for family occupation rather than personal use. Two different customers, two different propositions. The four-community structure is well suited to serving both, but only if that distinction is made deliberately.

One genuinely useful precedent is local: NRI Academy of Medical Sciences at Chinakakani has existed since 2003 — proof that NRI capital has been committing to this specific belt for over two decades, long before the capital was announced.

Local buyer behaviour

Direct behavioural research was not available and is flagged in §12. What the available evidence supports: a land-ownership bias (the strong plot market) — a headwind for apartments and a tailwind for villas, which offer land ownership with a finished product; villas outperforming apartments ~3:1 on appreciation, consistent with that preference; trading and agricultural wealth centres, which typically implies significant undocumented income, a preference for tangible assets and lower reliance on institutional finance; and extended-family living patterns favouring larger units and 3BHK+ configurations.

9.1 Political reversal

Amaravati has been stopped once already. The YSRCP has since abandoned the three-capital plan and backs Amaravati, but has raised a MAVIGUN coastal corridor agenda for coming elections and alleges corruption in the project. The 6 April 2026 gazette notification materially reduces this: undoing it requires Parliament, not a state cabinet.

Residual risk: a hostile state government cannot un-capital Amaravati, but it can slow it — deprioritising funding, letting tenders lapse, changing APCRDA leadership. Given the results-based structure of the multilateral loans, a state slowdown throttles disbursement too. The next state election falls in 2029 — inside this project's likely sell-out window.

Falsified by: a reduced Amaravati allocation in a state budget; APCRDA tenders lapsing without re-issue; multilateral disbursement pausing against unmet milestones.

9.2 The premium-pricing gap

This is the most probable failure mode of the project — not the capital going unbuilt, but the premium going unachieved.

§6 establishes a required blended ~₹5,900–6,000/sq ft against a Mangalagiri average of ₹4,062. §7 shows national ₹2–5 crore inventory up 43% YoY.

Falsified by: sustained corridor appreciation in built product of 12%+ annually; comparable schemes demonstrably clearing above ₹5,500/sq ft; premium absorption improving nationally.

9.3 Delivery slippage in the capital

Documented, not hypothetical: MLA/MLC/AIS towers at 58.14% against a 78.56% target; Quantum Valley's foundation stone laid after its operational date; the Gannavaram terminal past 31 March 2026; trunk roads targeted April 2027. If a residential project completes before trunk infrastructure and the capital's social layers arrive, early buyers occupy a well-built community in an unfinished city — and the reputational cost lands on the developer, not the government.

Mitigation is structural, not communicational: phase delivery to track the capital's actual progress, and specify the project to be self-sufficient on utilities rather than dependent on trunk services arriving on time.

9.4 Speculative land pricing correcting

Capital land reportedly doubled within six months of relaunch, with commentary explicitly identifying speculation and Hyderabad-analogy reasoning as drivers. The nuance that matters: a correction affects land acquisition cost (arguably favourably, for a project not yet bought) and corridor sentiment (unfavourably) — but not built-product pricing, which never re-rated in the first place. Paradoxically, a project underwritten on end-user demand is less exposed here than a plot venture.

9.5 Flood exposure

From 31 August 2024, Vijayawada recorded over 29 cm of rain in a single day; at least 35 deaths in NTR district and ~270,000 people affected. The Budameru rivulet discharged roughly 70,000 cusecs against a design maximum of 11,000. Worst-hit: Krishnalanka, Ibrahimpatnam, Ferry, Jupudi, Moolapadu, low-lying Payakapuram.

The cause is itself a site-selection lesson

Reporting attributes the severity substantially to encroachment narrowing the Budameru — illegal construction driven by capital-region housing demand. The capital boom itself contributed to the flood damage.

Mangalagiri sits on hilly terrain at ~43 m (avg ~29 m), materially above the Krishna floodplain and away from the Budameru catchment; flood-wall and lift-scheme works have been launched there. The riverfront belt — Undavalli, Penumaka, low-lying Tadepalli — does not share that advantage.

This makes elevation and drainage a first-order site-selection criterion in this corridor, not a technical footnote. Handled honestly it is also a genuine differentiator: after 2024, flood safety is a live concern for every buyer here, and very few developers are addressing it explicitly.

9.7 Absorption risk at scale  ·  9.8 Execution risk

Roughly 600 units requiring 10–12 sales a month for 4–5 years, in a corridor where the largest premium competitors sit on 5–7 acres, is a demanding absorption profile. Achievable — but only with disciplined phasing, and only if the premium in 9.2 holds. If pricing is cut to drive volume, the ₹500 crore target fails arithmetically.

Separately, ₹57,821 crore of concurrent regional works creates competition for contractors, labour and materials. The Gannavaram terminal delay was attributed specifically to contractor appointment difficulties — direct evidence of capacity constraints in this market.

Reading the result

C (83.0) and B (80.5) are separated by 2.5 points on a 97.5-point scale. Given the precision of the underlying data, that is not a meaningful gap — they are tied. That tie is the finding, not a failure to resolve one.

B wins on future infrastructure and customer profile — it is inside the capital city area, holds the KIMS campus and the Health City designation, and commands the full 2BHK-to-villa spectrum. C wins on residential demand, accessibility, pricing and site-visit potential — the functioning market with real end-users today, direct NH-16 access, and land at ₹4–8 crore/acre rather than ₹24,000+/sq yd. They are not competing answers. They are two halves of one answer.

Recommendation

The southern gateway of Mangalagiri mandal — the belt running from Neerukonda, Kuragallu and Nidamarru south-eastward toward Chinakakani and Kaza, in the seam between the capital city boundary and NH-16.

No specific plot or address is proposed. Acquisition search parameters are set out below instead.

Why this belt, specifically

1 · It is the only location that carries a capital address without capital land economics. Kuragallu, Neerukonda and Nidamarru are among the seventeen villages inside the Amaravati capital city area. A project here is legitimately in the capital — but land trades against Mangalagiri comparables (₹4–8 cr/acre) rather than seed-area plot rates. Zone A is arithmetically impossible: at ₹33,000–44,000/sq yd, 25 acres costs ₹160–215 crore — 32–43% of a ₹500 crore GDV, before a single foundation.

2 · Sophisticated institutional capital has already chosen this belt, repeatedly. This is revealed preference, not opinion:

§13

What carries into the next phase

Phase 1 is deliberately not a strategy. These are the findings that constrain what any strategy can credibly say — carried forward as inputs, not conclusions.

The five facts that survive scrutiny and can be used publicly

  1. Amaravati is the sole and permanent capital of Andhra Pradesh by Act of Parliament, gazetted 6 April 2026, retrospective to 2 June 2024.
  2. ~USD 1.6 billion of World Bank and ADB funding is committed, milestone-linked, with USD 340 million released; ₹57,821 crore of works are physically in progress.
  3. KIMS has taken a 60-year lease on 25 acres at Nidamarru for a 1,000-bed teaching hospital and 500-bed super-specialty hospital — alongside SRM's 100-acre campus at Neerukonda and AIIMS at Mangalagiri.
  4. Vijayawada airport carried 1.61 million passengers in FY2025–26, up 28.9%, after 30.2% the year before.
  5. A six-lane, 47.8 km bypass now carries traffic from north Vijayawada to the Mangalagiri belt.

The five constraints that shape everything downstream

  1. The premium gap. ₹500 crore on 25 acres requires a blended ~₹5,900–6,000/sq ft against a Mangalagiri market of ₹4,062. Manufacturing and defending a ~45% premium is the commercial problem — and it is a positioning problem before it is a media problem.
  2. The corridor's momentum is in land, not homes. Apartments drifted down over the same period land reportedly doubled. Appreciation is the wrong argument for this product.
  3. Villas are the demand engine; premium inventory is the national risk. Corridor villas appreciate ~3× faster than apartments — while nationally, unsold ₹2–5 crore inventory rose 43%. Both are true. Phasing and absorption discipline are load-bearing.
  4. Amaravati de-risks the decision; it does not motivate the purchase. The reason people buy remains space, schools, safety and status — and it comes from the existing 3.3 million-person agglomeration, not the capital's ~100,000 residents.
  5. The sales task is ~600 units at 10–12 bookings a month for 4–5 years, against an allowable marketing cost of ₹3.3–4.2 lakh per unit sold at 4–5% of GDV.
The competitive opening

The corridor is being sold on claims that do not survive a checkable question: a metro that does not go there, a Google data centre 350 km away, 100,000 quantum jobs, an ORR a decade out.

The true facts are strong enough without any of it. In a market this noisy, verifiable precision is not a compliance constraint — it is the differentiator, and it is currently unoccupied.

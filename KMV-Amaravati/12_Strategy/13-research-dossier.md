**What this means for every evidence table below, without exception:**

- **The URLs are real.** They were returned by a search engine and they resolve to pages that exist.

- **Therefore no row below is independently verified**, however authoritative the domain looks.

> **This is why Appendix A exists and why it is the most useful part of this document.** The twenty facts listed there are the ones where you opening the page yourself changes a decision. Treat this dossier as a *map of where the evidence is*, not as the evidence.

## 02. Amaravati–Mangalagiri market

### 02.1 What changed, and it is the most favourable finding in the dossier

The capital question that hung over the corridor from 2019 to 2024 is **resolved by statute**, not by announcement. That is a different kind of fact from anything Phase 1 could establish, and it removes the single largest political risk from the demand thesis.

**But three cautions apply, and the brief is explicit that future infrastructure does not automatically create demand:**

1. **Statutory capital status is not population.** No source found gives a verified count of people *living* in the capital region as a result. Phase 1's in-migration gap is unclosed.
2. **Government relocation is only partly evidenced.** The figures found — ~2,000 secretariat employees, a 15,000 deadline — are from **2016–2017 reporting**, not 2026. A decade-old number is not a current one.
3. **Construction spend is not consumer demand.** ₹57,821 crore of works generates contractor and labour activity. It does not establish that anyone will buy a ₹2.5 crore villa.

### 05.2 What the comparison actually says

**The plan asks a 50% premium to the Mangalagiri locality average and an 11% premium to the micromarket.**

**That is not automatically wrong.** A 15-units-per-acre scheme with villas should price above a locality average dominated by apartments — Phase 2 established that logic and it holds. **But three things follow:**

1. **The premium is now sized, and it never was before.** Phase 6 built a ladder bottom-up from competitor tickets. It never asked "how far above the locality are we?" The answer is 50%.
2. **Community A at ₹5,150 is priced above Manjeera Monarch at ₹5,100** — a delivered apartment project in the same locality. A is the volume community, 169 of 376 units. **It is priced at a premium to the nearest comparable with no delivery record to justify it.**
3. **Two of the six locality reference points moved in opposite directions** in 2025 — Mangalagiri drifted *down* ₹4,098 → ₹4,062 while the micromarket rose ₹4,805 → ₹5,489. **A source showing a locality falling while its own micromarket rises deserves scrutiny before either figure is used.**

### 05.3 Conflict: which price series is stronger?

| Series | Figure | Strength | Weakness |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mangalagiri locality | ₹4,062 | Locality-specific; the correct geography | Portal asking prices; drifting down against the trend of its own micromarket |
| Micromarket | ₹5,489 | Rising trend consistent with capital activity | **Geography undefined in the snippet** — "wider micromarket" may include Tadepalli and Patamata, both higher |
| Project-level | ₹4,210–5,100 | **Specific, named, checkable** | Only three projects; all asking |

> **Verdict: the project-level figures are the strongest of the three**, because they are named and checkable. **The locality average is the weakest**, because it aggregates unknown product mix. **Phase 2's method — derive ₹/sq ft from published ticket ÷ published size, project by project — was the right one**, and this pass corroborates its outputs at the two points it could check.
>
> **What should be treated as uncertain: everything.** All of it is asking price. **The registration data that would settle it remains unobtained** — Phase 12 G1, unchanged.

### 06.2 The density claim, and why Phase 12 G7 was right to flag it

## 07. KMV Spaces brand audit

### 07.1 The finding that matters most in this document

**Phase 4 recorded as FACT that KMV Projects built AIIMS Mangalagiri, and the entire brand position was built on it.** Phase 5 found delivery anxiety is the one concern shared by all eight segments; Phase 10 made "The Builder" a creative pillar; Phase 12 G13 proposed a delivery charter resting on it.

**Verification produces three sources that do not agree on KMV's role:**

| Source | What it indicates | Tier |

| **A video title** references *"L & T Mangalagiri L/T KMV Construction"* | L&T may hold a contractor role | **8 — user-generated, weakest** |

**Reconciling them.** Under the PMSSY structure, HSCC (India) Ltd is routinely the government's executing agency for AIIMS projects, with civil contractors appointed beneath it. **KMV being a contractor on AIIMS Mangalagiri is entirely consistent with HSCC being the executing agency** — the two are not contradictory. **What is not established by any source found is KMV's scope**: main civil contractor for the whole campus, contractor for certain packages, or sub-contractor.

> **Why this is decisive rather than pedantic.** The strategy does not need KMV to have been the executing agency. **It needs a defensible sentence.** *"KMV Projects built AIIMS Mangalagiri"* may be true, overstated, or misleading depending on scope — and it will be published to a market whose top complaint is misrepresentation. **A work-order or completion certificate resolves it in one document.** Until then it is Appendix F item 1.

**This pass found no corroboration** — not in the two KMV Spaces project pages surfaced, not in the portal listings, not in the 2016 environment-clearance filing, not in news. **Absence in search is not disproof.** But a superlative first-in-India claim that does not surface in a direct search is exactly the claim that must not be published without the certificate in hand.

# PART II — STRATEGIC RECOMMENDATION

> **Read this as hypothesis, not conclusion.** Every answer below is **STRATEGIC HYPOTHESIS** unless marked otherwise, and each names what would falsify it. **Four of the sixteen change materially if §07.1 (the AIIMS scope) resolves badly** — they are marked ⚠.

### 1 · Where should the project be positioned?

**Against the corridor's density, not its price.** Fifteen homes per acre across twenty-five acres, where the nearest apartment comparable runs at multiples of that. **Not "luxury," not "premium," not "close to the capital"** — Phase 3 established all three are universal and meaningless here.

**The position in one line:** *the corridor's only development you can move within, at a density you can count.*

**Falsified by:** the density comparators failing to source (§06.2), or the locality proving unwilling to pay a 50% premium (§05.2).

### 2 · Who should it target?

**Not one audience — four, mapped to four communities.** Priority order from Phase 5, adjusted by verification:

| Priority | Segment | Community |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **S4 landowners** — best-evidenced population, no competitor contesting | D and C, via exchange |
| **2** | **S1 trade families** — largest revenue, cash-capable | C and D |
| **3** | **S3 US diaspora** — highest priority score | B and C |
| **4** | **S2 senior medical** — institution confirmed, ladder maps to pay scale | A through C |
| **5** | S7 Hyderabad, S6 upgraders | A and B |
| **De-weight** | **S5 government** — sizing is nine years stale | A |

### 3 · Why would those people buy?

**Different reasons per segment, and conflating them is the error the brief warns against.**

- **S4** — converts an illiquid returnable plot into a built, income-capable asset without finding cash.
- **S1** — a verifiable, low-density asset in a corridor where everything else is a tower.
- **S3** — a managed, remotely-purchasable home in the origin district, with someone answering in their time zone.
- **S2** — proximity to AIIMS, and a ladder that matches a published pay scale rung for rung.
- **All eight** — **delivery certainty**, which Phase 5 found is the one shared anxiety. ⚠ *This is the answer that depends on §07.1.*

### 4 · What should KMV own in the customer's mind? ⚠

**"The builder who shows you the evidence."**

Not luxury, not scale. The construction lineage, published delivery performance, ungated pricing, carpet area declared unprompted, and a live construction feed. **Phase 3 found not one competitor markets against the category's own top complaint.**

> ⚠ **This is the recommendation most exposed by verification.** It rests on institutional credentials whose scope is undocumented (§07.1) and a certification that could not be corroborated (§07.2). **If neither documents, the position must fall back to what is provable: the density arithmetic and the ungated price sheet** — both of which are verifiable without any credential at all.

### 5 · How should A/B/C/D be differentiated?

**By buyer constraint, not by finish level.**

| | Differentiated by | Primary buyer | The question it answers |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **A** | Entry price and rental logic | S2 junior, S6, S5 | "Can I own here at all?" |
| **B** | **Management and absence** | S3, S8 | "Can I own this from 12,000 km away?" |
| **C** | Land and privacy at a reachable ticket | S1, S2 senior | "Can I have a plot without ₹2.5 crore?" |
| **D** | Scale, and permanence | S1, S4, S3 senior | "Is this the last house I buy?" |

**The anti-cannibalisation rules are structural, not promotional:** B never above ₹1.50 Cr, C never below ₹1.65 Cr, and **B is defined by service while C is defined by land** — a buyer choosing between them is choosing between two different products, not two price points.

### 6 · What should the master campaign communicate?

**One computable fact, and an invitation to check it.** Fifteen to the acre; everything else is open.

**What it must not do:** claim superlatives, claim future infrastructure as present (§03), or lead with the AIIMS credential until §07.1 resolves. ⚠

### 7 · Which channels should drive awareness?

**Outdoor on the corridor, Telugu OTT and regional cinema, and PR against the capital narrative.** These reach the domestic segments in-market and cannot be judged on click metrics — Phase 9 risk 7.

**Plus one that verification made urgent: branded and category search.** Broker interception is live today (§11).

### 8 · Which channels should drive demand?

**Meta and Google Search carry it; portals are capped; LinkedIn is S3/S7 only.**

**The correction verification forces:** portals are the corridor's dominant organic surface and they are rented. **Capping portal spend at ₹1.00 Cr only works if owned search is defended simultaneously** — otherwise capping the rented channel while leaving the owned one intercepted loses both.

### 9 · How should leads be qualified?

**On cash at purchase, not income** — Phase 5's central finding. The gate is ₹17.6 L for A rising to ₹74.1 L for D.

**Three required fields at capture:** budget band, timeline, and **country/origin district** (§12 G12). **Qualification criteria must be written down and owned by a named person** — Phase 12 G32 found neither exists.

### 10 · How should site visits be generated?

**The site visit is the ₹39.4-crore-per-point stage; treat it as the product.**

Domestic: the counted walk, with an accessible route. Overseas: the **proxy-visit protocol and video walkthrough — piloted on the existing project before launch**, because ₹12.63 Cr of Community B budget depends on a capability never once tried (Phase 12 G27).

### 11 · How should marketing work with sales?

**On one shared number: site-visit-to-booking rate, weekly, by community and by consultant.**

Marketing owns everything up to the booked visit; sales owns the visit onward; **both are read on the same metric** so neither can blame the other's stage. A structured visit debrief with a closed objection taxonomy is the handover artefact.

### 12 · What should the CRM journey look like?

**Twelve stages, of which two are currently fictional.** Consultation and negotiation are modelled but have no defined trigger (Phase 12 G26) — define both before go-live.

**Non-negotiable on day one:** WhatsApp Business API integrated (un-integrated it is a liability, not a channel), first-touch attribution as a mandatory picklist, and a named CRM owner.

### 13 · What should be measured weekly?

| Metric | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **Site-visit-to-booking rate**, by community and consultant | ₹39.4 Cr per point. **The single number** |
| Site visits booked and held | The leading indicator |
| Lead-to-qualified rate | ₹17.2 Cr per point |
| CPL and CPQL by community, **media-only** | Campaign optimisation |
| Follow-up latency after visit | Cheapest controllable variable |
| Discount register, **by channel partner** | Pricing rules are load-bearing |

### 14 · What should be measured monthly?

| Metric | Why |
| --- | --- |
| **All-in CAC**, and CAC by source | The board number |
| Bookings by source, **first-touch** | Retires the largest Reasoned assumption |
| Inventory released vs sold, per community | B's gate at 25% qualified-to-visit |
| **Achieved price vs list, per community** | The only internal check on §05 |
| Creative frequency and CPL drift | Fatigue trigger (Phase 12 G23) |
| Referral bookings per owner | The compounding asset |

### 15 · What should determine marketing budget allocation?

**Four rules, in order:**

1. **Bottom-up, never a percentage.** Phase 11 showed top-down under-funds everything that is not media by 28%.
2. **By structural class, not by total.** Only ₹21.17 Cr of ₹31.75 Cr is genuinely flexible.
3. **By marginal revenue at risk.** ₹1 cut from performance media risks ₹24.60 — *while the CPLs hold*, which is untested.
4. **Re-based annually**, falling toward the 4–5% norm as referral compounds.

### 16 · How do we optimise toward revenue rather than vanity metrics?

**Five rules.**

1. **Never optimise the top of the funnel.** 66,406 leads are lost at the top and they are the least valuable losses. **2,348 lost site visits are worth more.**
2. **Compare within a layer, never across.** The naive cost-per-booking-by-channel reading defunds ₹10.25 Cr of the highest-value spend — put the rule in the reporting template before the first review.
3. **Judge brand, outdoor and the experience centre on holdouts and first-touch**, never on last click.
4. **Report revenue at risk, not spend efficiency.** "CPL improved 12%" is not a result; "site-visit conversion moved 0.4 points, worth ₹15.8 Cr" is.
5. **Kill the metrics that cannot change a decision.** Impressions, reach, followers, and page views appear nowhere in §13 or §14 deliberately.

> **The measurement instruction that matters most.** Of the six weekly metrics, **five require CRM discipline that does not currently exist** — a named owner, written qualification criteria, WhatsApp integration, defined stage triggers and a mandatory first-touch field. **Building the measurement system is a pre-launch dependency, not a reporting task.**

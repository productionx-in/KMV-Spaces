# 02 — Infrastructure Status

## Every corridor asset, graded — and the three the marketing must never claim

**Source phases:** `01_Market/01-market-research.md` (original grading) and `12_Strategy/13-research-dossier.md` §03 (verification, August 2026).

**Why this file exists.** Infrastructure claims are where Indian real-estate marketing most reliably overstates, and the brief was explicit: **do not treat future announcements as existing infrastructure.** This consolidates every asset onto one grade so a claim can be checked in one place before it is published.

---

## 1. The grading system

| Grade | Means | May be claimed as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **CONFIRMED** | Built, operational, or enacted in law | An existing fact |
| **UNDER CONSTRUCTION** | Physical work verifiably in progress | "Under construction" — never "complete" |
| **APPROVED** | Sanctioned by the competent authority; **not yet built** | "Approved" — never "coming" or "under construction" |
| **PROPOSED** | Announced or planned; no sanction verified | **Do not use in marketing** |
| **REPORTED** | Appears in reporting; no primary source | **Do not use in marketing** |
| **SPECULATIVE** | Figures circulating without a traceable document | **Never use** |

## 3. The three that must never be claimed

> **1 · The airport.** Phase 1 graded it SPECULATIVE on social-media-sourced acreage figures. Phase 13 searched again and **found no DPR, no site notification, nothing.** Two failed attempts is not proof of absence — but it is more than enough to keep it out of marketing.
>
> **2 · The metro.** No 2026 status exists in anything retrieved.
>
> **3 · The ORR as an amenity.** This is the discipline test, and it is the one most likely to be failed.

### 3.1 Why the ORR is the test

The ORR is **genuinely approved**, genuinely 189 km, genuinely ₹16,000 crore — and genuinely **not built**, with land acquisition only beginning in 2026.

**Indian real-estate advertising routinely presents exactly this status as an existing amenity.** A brand positioned on verifiability cannot, and the distinction is one word: *approved*, not *coming*.

## 5. The caution that goes with the good news

**Statutory capital status is the most favourable finding in the whole exercise. It still does not establish demand.**

1. **Statutory status is not population.** No verified count exists of people living in the capital region as a result.
2. **Government relocation is only partly evidenced.** The figures found — ~2,000 secretariat staff, a 15,000 deadline — are **2016–17 reporting**, now nine years stale. The segment sized against them (S5, ~₹52 Cr) should be de-weighted until a current headcount exists.
3. **Construction spend is not consumer demand.** ₹57,821 crore of works generates contractor and labour activity. It does not establish that anyone will buy a ₹2.5 crore villa.

> **The brief's instruction, restated because this is the file where it matters most:** *do not assume that future infrastructure automatically creates demand.*

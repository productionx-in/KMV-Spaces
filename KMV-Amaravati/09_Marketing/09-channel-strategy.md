# Phase 9 — Channel Strategy

## Twenty-two channels evaluated, five cut or reclassified, and the budget that follows

**Prepared:** August 2026
**Purpose:** Channel roles, allocation and measurement for the Phase 8 journey design
**Method note:** The brief is explicit — *do not assume every channel is necessary.* §1 gives the verdict on all twenty-two before any of them is described, because a channel list where everything is "important" is not a strategy. §0.2 also names **four category errors in the list as given**, which would produce double-counted budget if carried through unexamined.

---

## 0. How each channel was judged

### 0.1 The test

A channel earns budget here only if it can answer three questions with evidence from Phases 5–8:

1. **Which segment does it reach that another channel does not reach more cheaply?** Phase 5 established eight segments with almost non-overlapping media behaviour. A channel that only duplicates reach is a cost, not a channel.
2. **Which funnel stage does it move?** Phase 8 established that a point at the site-visit stage is worth **₹39.4 crore** against **₹17.2 crore** at the lead stage. A channel that adds volume to the cheapest stage is worth less than one that improves the most expensive.
3. **Can its contribution be established at all?** Phase 8 established that with a 3–18 month cycle and 47% of bookings arriving without a click trail, **most channel-level attribution here is fiction.** Channels are judged on the job they do, not on the leads they claim.

### 0.2 Four category errors in the list as given

Stated plainly, because carrying them through would produce a budget with duplicated lines and double-counted leads.

| In the brief | What it actually is | Consequence if not corrected |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Instagram** listed separately from **Meta** | **Instagram is a placement inside Meta**, bought in the same auction from the same account | Two budget lines, two agency reports, the same leads counted twice |
| **Outdoor** and **Hoardings** listed separately | **The same channel.** Hoardings are the dominant outdoor format in this corridor | Duplicated line, inflated apparent breadth |
| **NRI marketing** listed as a channel | **An audience, not a channel.** Phase 5 split it into two segments — S3 (US, Communities C and D) and S8 (Gulf, A and B) — reached through Meta, YouTube, LinkedIn, WhatsApp, email and events | An "NRI budget" that cannot be optimised, because it is a slice of six other channels |
| **CRM** listed as a channel | **Infrastructure, not a channel.** It reaches nobody. But it determines the lead-to-qualified rate, which Phase 8 values at ₹17.2 crore per point | Under-funded because it produces no leads of its own |

**CRM stays in the list below** despite not being a channel, because dropping it would be worse than misclassifying it. **Instagram, Hoardings and NRI marketing are folded into Meta, Outdoor and the audience plan respectively**, and are addressed there.

## 1. The verdict, before the detail

**Reasoned** — every channel placed into one of five tiers, with the reason it sits there.

### Tier 1 — Load-bearing. The plan fails without these.

| Channel | Why it is load-bearing |
| --- | --- |
| **Experiential / site marketing** | Phase 8: **a point at the site-visit stage is worth ₹39.4 crore.** The highest-return channel in the plan, and the one conventionally budgeted as "collateral" |
| **Referral marketing** | **70 bookings, ~₹94.5 crore, at ₹1.71 lakh each — 6.1× cheaper than marketing-sourced.** Phase 7 found the cheapest revenue comes from the segment that cannot be bought with advertising |
| **Channel partners / brokers** | **105 bookings — 28% of the scheme** — at ₹3.18 lakh each, paid only on performance |
| **WhatsApp** | Phase 5's primary channel for S1, S4 and S8, and the follow-up channel for all eight. Usually treated as a tool; here it is a channel |

### Tier 2 — Necessary at scale.

| Channel | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Meta** (incl. Instagram placements) | The volume engine for Communities A and B, and the only affordable way to reach S3 and S7 by affinity |
| **Google Search** | Intent capture, and the highest-quality lead in the plan |
| **Website + landing pages** | The verification surface for Journey 2, where demand already exists |
| **Outdoor / hoardings** | Local legitimacy at the scale a 25-acre scheme needs. Phase 5 found S1 and S5 read hoardings as proof of substance |
| **CRM** *(infrastructure)* | Owns the lead-to-qualified rate — **₹17.2 crore per point** |

### Tier 3 — Necessary, but small.

**YouTube** · **SEO** · **Print** · **PR** · **Email**. Each does one specific job for one or two segments. None deserves a large budget; all four would be missed if removed.

### Tier 4 — Presence only, capped.

| Channel | The cap, and why |
| --- | --- |
| **Property portals** | **Presence is mandatory, lead volume is not.** Portals sell the same enquiry to several developers, and Phase 8 established the top-of-funnel leak is the least valuable one. Capped at ₹1.00 Cr |
| **LinkedIn** | Reaches S3 and S7 precisely and nobody else. Expensive per lead in B2C real estate. Capped at ₹0.50 Cr |
| **Events** | **Split verdict.** On-site owner and milestone events are Tier 1 advocacy work. Off-site lead-generation events are Tier 4 and should not run in year one |

### Tier 5 — Cut, or not a channel.

| Item | Verdict |
| --- | --- |
| **Google Display — prospecting** | **Cut.** Indian real-estate display prospecting produces very high volumes of accidental in-app clicks. It would inflate the lead count and depress the lead-to-qualified rate — the one metric worth ₹17.2 crore per point. **Retargeting only, ₹0.30 Cr** |
| **Instagram as a separate line** | **Cut as a line.** A Meta placement, budgeted inside Meta |
| **Hoardings as a separate line** | **Cut as a line.** Budgeted inside Outdoor |
| **NRI marketing as a separate line** | **Cut as a line.** An audience, executed across six channels |
| **Broad email acquisition** | **Cut.** Email is a nurture and document-delivery channel here, for S2 and S3 only |

## 3. The channels

### TIER 1 — Load-bearing

#### Experiential / site marketing — ₹4.10 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Make the density visible.** Phase 6's entire price premium rests on 15 homes per acre against a corridor norm of 53–113, and Phase 2 §5.1 called that *"a difference visible from the site gate."* The experience centre and show homes are the only place that argument can actually be made |
| **Audience** | All eight segments — but note Phase 5's visit patterns diverge completely. **S1** unannounced Sunday, 3–6 family. **S2** by appointment, couple only, weekday evening. **S5 / S6** Sunday with children, comparing two or three projects. **S3** proxy relative first, then one compressed trip. **S7** Friday-to-Sunday |
| **Funnel stage** | **Site Visit (8)**, and it carries Consultation (7) and Negotiation (9) |
| **Expected contribution** | Not a lead source. It converts. **Every one of the 376 bookings passes through it** except the S4 exchange route |
| **KPI** | Visit-to-booking 6.9% blended (A 8.1 · B 6.0 · C 7.0 · D 5.6); repeat-visit rate; **peak-Sunday capacity utilisation** |
| **Measurement** | Attendance logged with party size and segment; 24-hour follow-up logged; conversion by community *and* by salesperson |
| **Strength** | **The highest-return channel in the plan — ₹39.4 crore per point of conversion.** It is also the only channel a 5-to-11-acre competitor cannot replicate, because the asset being shown is the land itself |
| **Weakness** | **Fixed capital, spent before the first booking**, and its capacity is set by peak Sunday, not by monthly average — Phase 8 §5.1 estimated 35–45 people on site on a busy Sunday. Under-size it and no upstream spend can compensate |

#### Referral marketing — ₹0.70 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | Convert buyers into introducers, and **answer S1's first objection structurally.** Phase 5: the opening question from trade families is *"who else has bought?"* |
| **Audience** | Every booked buyer, weighted to **S1** — who frequently buys as multiple related households — and **S4**, where *"the first neighbour to complete a transaction becomes the single most persuasive asset available"* |
| **Funnel stage** | **Referral (11)** — and, uniquely, it feeds back into **Awareness (1)** for Journey 2 |
| **Expected contribution** | **70 bookings — 18.5% of the scheme, ~₹94.5 crore** |
| **KPI** | Referral-sourced bookings against 70; referrals per booked buyer; cost per referral booking |
| **Measurement** | A **referrer field on every booking**, reconciled monthly against the 18.5% assumption |
| **Strength** | **₹1.71 lakh per booking against ₹10.52 lakh marketing-sourced — 6.1× cheaper.** It also compounds: each cohort of buyers enlarges the next |
| **Weakness** | **It cannot exist in year one.** Referral requires prior buyers, and at launch there are none. §7 handles the ramp |

#### Channel partners / brokers — ₹3.34 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | Distributed reach into networks the developer cannot address directly — particularly S1's trade circles and the Vijayawada–Guntur broker network Phase 5 identified as one of S1's core information sources |
| **Audience** | Primarily **S1** and **S6**; secondarily S5. Weakest with S3 and S4 |
| **Funnel stage** | **Lead (5) through Booking (10)** — partners run their own mini-funnel |
| **Expected contribution** | **105 bookings — 28% of the scheme, ~₹142 crore** |
| **KPI** | Bookings per active partner; partner-sourced visit-to-booking rate; **commission as a share of agreement value against the 2.5% assumption** |
| **Measurement** | Partner code on every lead and booking; a monthly active-partner count; conversion tracked separately from direct |
| **Strength** | **Pay-on-performance.** No working capital, no cost until a unit sells, and it scales without a media plan |
| **Weakness** | **The developer does not control the message.** A partner selling on discount rather than on density erodes the Phase 6 pricing rules, and partners routinely present competing projects in the same conversation. Requires a briefing and monitoring discipline that is itself a cost |

#### WhatsApp — inside CRM and demand-gen budgets

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **The primary conversational channel in this catchment**, not a follow-up tool. Phase 5 found it primary for S1, S4 and S8, and the follow-up channel for all eight segments |
| **Audience** | All eight. Decisive for **S8** — *"mobile-only, Telugu-language, WhatsApp-centric, video over text… will not fill a desktop web form"* — and for **S3**, whose form-fill rates are very low but who will respond on WhatsApp |
| **Funnel stage** | **Lead (5), Qualified Lead (6), Consultation (7)**, and post-booking through Referral (11) |
| **Expected contribution** | Not separately attributed. **It is the capture and contact layer under Meta, portals and referral alike** |
| **KPI** | Response time to inbound; contact-establishment rate versus phone; **lead-to-qualified rate for WhatsApp-originated leads versus form-originated** |
| **Measurement** | WhatsApp Business API integrated into the CRM so conversations are logged as activity, not held on a salesperson's handset |
| **Strength** | Highest contact rate of any channel here, works across literacy and language, and it is the only channel that spans a Gulf earner and his wife in Vijayawada in one thread |
| **Weakness** | **Ungoverned by default.** Conversations live on personal phones, leave no CRM trail, and walk out of the door when a salesperson does. Without API integration it is not a channel — it is a liability |

### TIER 2 — Necessary at scale

#### Meta — ₹3.45 Cr *(includes Instagram placements)*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | The volume engine for Communities A and B, and the only affordable way to reach S3 and S7 by geography-plus-affinity |
| **Audience** | **S6** and **S5** locally; **S3** by US metro and origin-district affinity; **S7** by Hyderabad geography; **S8** in Telugu. Weak against **S1**, which Phase 5 found treats digital as verification not discovery, and useless against **S4** |
| **Funnel stage** | **Interest (3) through Lead (5)**, with retargeting into Consideration (4) |
| **Expected contribution** | **~68 bookings** — the largest single marketing-sourced channel |
| **KPI** | CPL by community (A ₹350 · B ₹1,600 · C ₹900 · D ₹1,900); **lead-to-qualified rate by campaign**, not lead volume; cost per site visit |
| **Measurement** | Platform reporting for in-channel optimisation only. **Contribution to bookings comes from the CRM source field, not from Meta's attribution window** |
| **Strength** | Precision and scale at the same time — origin-district affinity targeting for S3 is not available anywhere else at this price. Instagram placements carry the villa creative better than any other surface |
| **Weakness** | **Lead quality is the worst of the paid channels.** Form-fill leads convert at the bottom of the range, and Phase 8 established that top-of-funnel volume is the least valuable thing to buy. A Meta budget judged on CPL will optimise toward exactly the wrong leads |

#### Google Search — ₹2.30 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Intent capture.** Someone typing "villas in Mangalagiri" or "3BHK Amaravati price" has done the work; the job is to be there and to answer |
| **Audience** | **S2** — who Phase 5 found *"will check RERA; most of the catchment will not"* — plus S6, S5, S3 and S7. Also **S1 in verification mode**, searching the developer's name after a peer mentions it |
| **Funnel stage** | **Discovery (2) and Lead (5)** |
| **Expected contribution** | **~52 bookings**, at the best lead quality in the plan |
| **KPI** | Cost per qualified lead, not cost per lead; impression share on a fixed ~20-query commercial set; branded versus non-branded split |
| **Measurement** | Search Console and Ads, reconciled to CRM qualification outcomes |
| **Strength** | **The highest-intent traffic available**, and the only paid channel that serves Journey 2's verification step. Phase 4 found KMV's search visibility thin — this is where that gap costs money directly |
| **Weakness** | **Volume is capped by demand.** No amount of budget creates more people searching "villas in Mangalagiri." It can be saturated, and beyond that point the money is better spent elsewhere |

#### Website + landing pages — ₹0.60 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **The verification surface.** For Journey 2 — 24% of units and 30% of revenue — this is not a discovery tool. The buyer already knows about the project; the site decides whether they believe it |
| **Audience** | All digitally-reachable segments. **Most consequential for S2 and S3**, who research in depth and at length |
| **Funnel stage** | **Discovery (2), Interest (3), Consideration (4), Lead (5)** |
| **Expected contribution** | Not separately attributed. It sits under every digital channel — **~180 of the 201 marketing-sourced bookings pass through it** |
| **KPI** | Landing-page conversion rate by community; time on the price-sheet page; **bounce rate on the four community pages separately** |
| **Measurement** | GA4 with community as a content dimension; UTM discipline enforced by community |
| **Strength** | The one surface fully under the developer's control, and the cheapest place to publish the things competitors will not — **price per square foot, a unit-wise price sheet, the possession date, the RERA number** |
| **Weakness** | **It converts nobody on its own.** Its value is entirely dependent on traffic bought elsewhere and on trust built elsewhere. Over-investing in the site while under-investing in the reasons to visit it is a common and expensive error |

#### Outdoor / hoardings — ₹2.10 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Local legitimacy at the scale a 25-acre scheme needs.** Phase 5 found S1 and S5 read a sustained hoarding presence as evidence of substance — a developer who can hold NH-16 for two years is a developer who will finish |
| **Audience** | **S1, S5, S6** on the Vijayawada–Guntur corridor; secondarily anyone driving to AIIMS, the Secretariat or the university cluster |
| **Funnel stage** | **Awareness (1)**, and directional traffic to Site Visit (8) |
| **Expected contribution** | **Not attributable to bookings and should not be asked to be.** Its contribution shows up in the booking-stage first-touch field and in recall |
| **KPI** | Prompted and unprompted recall in the catchment; share of category search; **first-touch mentions at booking** |
| **Measurement** | Quarterly recall survey, n ≈ 400. **A geo or time-based holdout is the only honest way to test it** |
| **Strength** | Unmissable in a corridor where the target audience drives the same road daily. It also does something no digital channel does: **it is seen by the buyer's peers**, which matters for a segment whose first question is who else has bought |
| **Weakness** | **Expensive, committed long, and genuinely hard to measure.** It is the line most likely to be cut under last-click reporting, and Phase 8 §4.3 explains why that would be the wrong cut |

#### CRM, martech and telephony — ₹0.55 Cr *(infrastructure, not a channel)*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Owns the lead-to-qualified rate.** Not a channel — it reaches nobody — but Phase 8 values a single point at this stage at **₹17.2 crore** |
| **Audience** | Internal. Sales team, channel partners, and the relationship managers who own post-booking advocacy |
| **Funnel stage** | **Every stage from Lead (5) to Advocacy (12)** |
| **Expected contribution** | Indirect and large. A published benchmark holds that developers without CRM automation **lose 30–50% of qualified leads to follow-up gaps** |
| **KPI** | Speed to first contact; contact-establishment rate; **stage-transition completeness — the share of bookings carrying a source, a referrer and a first-touch value** |
| **Measurement** | The CRM measures itself. The audit that matters is **field completeness**, not report volume |
| **Strength** | It is the only place the four counted gates exist, and the only place the booking-stage first-touch field can live. **Everything Phase 8 recommends depends on it** |
| **Weakness** | **It fails quietly.** A CRM with 40% of source fields blank produces reports that look fine and decide budget wrongly. Requires enforcement, not just purchase |

### TIER 3 — Necessary, but small

#### YouTube — ₹1.30 Cr brand + ₹1.10 Cr demand generation

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Long-form proof for buyers who cannot visit**, and the highest-consumption format in Telugu media |
| **Audience** | **S3 and S7** — Phase 5: *"will watch a six-minute walkthrough"* and *"will do a video site walkthrough before a physical one."* Also **S4 and S8**, where Telugu-language YouTube consumption is genuinely high, and **S1**, which uses YouTube for long-form |
| **Funnel stage** | **Awareness (1), Interest (3), and — uniquely — a partial substitute for Site Visit (8)** |
| **Expected contribution** | **~18 bookings** directly, and a disproportionate assist into Community B, where the physical visit is the binding constraint |
| **KPI** | 50% and 75% video completion; cost per completed view; **for B, the share of qualified leads that watch a full walkthrough before a visit** |
| **Measurement** | YouTube analytics for engagement; CRM for the B-specific correlation |
| **Strength** | **The only channel that can partially do the site visit's job remotely**, which is the single most valuable fix available in the plan (Phase 8, breakage 1) |
| **Weakness** | Production-heavy and slow to change. A walkthrough film shot before construction ages badly, and re-shooting is a real cost, not a re-upload |

#### SEO — ₹0.40 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Loss prevention on demand that already exists.** For Journey 2, the buyer arrives at search having already been referred; if the results are thin, a live prospect is lost silently |
| **Audience** | **S2, S3, S7**, and **S1 in verification mode** |
| **Funnel stage** | **Discovery (2)** |
| **Expected contribution** | **~12 bookings**, at low marginal cost — but back-loaded, since organic authority accrues over quarters |
| **KPI** | Branded search volume; visibility on the ~20-query commercial set; Google Business Profile views and direction requests |
| **Measurement** | Search Console and rank tracking, **baselined against the Phase 4 audit** so improvement is provable |
| **Strength** | Compounds, costs nothing per click, and Phase 4 established the current baseline is weak — **which means the gains available are unusually large for a small budget** |
| **Weakness** | **Slow.** Nothing here helps in the first two quarters, which is exactly when the launch needs volume. It cannot be accelerated with money |

#### Print — ₹1.20 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Legitimacy, not response.** Phase 5 found print retains real credibility with S5 and S1 in this market — a full page in Eenadu is read as evidence of scale |
| **Audience** | **S5, S1, S6**, and the older decision-influencers Phase 5 identified inside S1 households — the father or eldest brother |
| **Funnel stage** | **Awareness (1)**, with a launch-moment role |
| **Expected contribution** | **Not attributable, and should not be bought on response.** Its job is to make the project unmissable at launch and credible thereafter |
| **KPI** | Recall; first-touch mentions at booking; **not coupon codes or unique numbers**, which measure the wrong thing here |
| **Measurement** | Recall survey. Response-tracked print in this market measures the small fraction who respond immediately, not the majority who register the project and act later |
| **Strength** | Reaches the household influencers no digital channel reaches, and confers a specific kind of local seriousness |
| **Weakness** | **Expensive per contact and genuinely unmeasurable.** Together with outdoor it is ₹3.30 crore of the brand budget that cannot be defended under click-based reporting |

#### PR — ₹0.45 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Build the search results that Journey 2 verifies against.** Its job is Discovery, not Awareness — Phase 4 found the developer's public record thin, and a referred buyer who searches the name should find third-party coverage, not silence |
| **Audience** | **S1 and S2** in verification mode; **S3 and S7** researching from a distance; local institutional and government audiences |
| **Funnel stage** | **Discovery (2)**, with a secondary Awareness role at launch |
| **Expected contribution** | Not attributable. **Measured by what it puts on page one, not by leads** |
| **KPI** | Third-party coverage appearing on page one for the developer and project names; quality of publication; **the AIIMS credential appearing in coverage the developer did not write** |
| **Measurement** | Search-results audit quarterly; coverage log with publication tier |
| **Strength** | Cheapest way to fix a thin search footprint with third-party credibility, which is worth more than owned content to a verifying buyer |
| **Weakness** | **Not controllable and not schedulable.** It cannot be relied on to land at a specific moment, and a PR budget judged on volume of coverage will buy the wrong coverage |

#### Email + marketing automation — ₹0.19 Cr

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Nurture and document delivery — for two segments only.** Not an acquisition channel here |
| **Audience** | **S2**, who Phase 5 found *"responds to email; often cannot take calls during the day"*, and **S3**, reading across time zones. Effectively useless for S1, S4, S5 and S8 |
| **Funnel stage** | **Consideration (4) and Consultation (7)** |
| **Expected contribution** | No independent booking attribution. It shortens the consideration cycle for two segments |
| **KPI** | Open and click rate for S2 and S3 cohorts separately; **document-open rate on price sheets and the FEMA/PoA explainer** |
| **Measurement** | Automation platform, integrated to the CRM |
| **Strength** | Almost free, asynchronous — which is the whole point for a buyer 10½ hours away — and the right medium for the long documents S3 needs before a remote purchase |
| **Weakness** | **Narrow.** Applied broadly it produces poor engagement and trains the team to distrust it. It is a two-segment tool, and should be budgeted as one |

### TIER 4 — Presence only, capped

#### Property portals — ₹1.00 Cr *(capped)*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Presence is mandatory; lead volume is optional.** A premium project absent from 99acres, MagicBricks and Housing reads as either unserious or already sold out. But portal leads are the lowest-quality in the plan |
| **Audience** | **S5 and S6** predominantly — Phase 5 found both shortlist on portals. Marginal for S1, S2, S3 |
| **Funnel stage** | **Discovery (2) and Lead (5)** |
| **Expected contribution** | **~34 bookings**, concentrated in Communities A and B |
| **KPI** | **Lead-to-qualified rate, not lead count.** Cost per qualified lead, benchmarked against Meta and Search |
| **Measurement** | CRM, with portal leads tagged and their qualification rate reported separately every month |
| **Strength** | High-intent shoppers actively in market, at moderate cost, with no creative production required |
| **Weakness** | **The same enquiry is sold to several developers**, so the buyer arrives already comparing on price — the one dimension where Phase 6 says this project should not compete. **Uncapped, portals would inflate the lead count and depress the metric worth ₹17.2 crore per point** |

#### LinkedIn — ₹0.50 Cr *(capped, S3 and S7 only)*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **The only precise way to reach US-based Telugu technology professionals** by employer, seniority, education and geography |
| **Audience** | **S3** — 1.23 million Telugu-origin people in the US, technology-weighted — and **S7** in Hyderabad. Nobody else |
| **Funnel stage** | **Interest (3)** |
| **Expected contribution** | **~9 bookings** — but in Communities B and D, so ~₹15–20 crore of revenue from a ₹0.50 crore line |
| **KPI** | Cost per qualified lead from the S3 and S7 cohorts specifically; **not CPL, which will look poor and be judged wrongly** |
| **Measurement** | CRM cohort tracking. Judge it on booked revenue over 18 months, not on quarterly lead cost |
| **Strength** | Targeting precision unavailable anywhere else, against the segment with the highest revenue per unit |
| **Weakness** | **The most expensive cost per lead in the plan, in a channel built for B2B.** It will look bad on every standard report. Capping it is what makes it defensible — at ₹0.50 crore it is affordable to be wrong about |

#### Events — ₹0.30 Cr on-site *(off-site: not in year one)*

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Role** | **Split verdict.** On-site owner and milestone events are advocacy infrastructure and belong in Tier 1 thinking. Off-site lead-generation events — diaspora roadshows, hotel expos — are Tier 4 and should not run in year one |
| **Audience** | On-site: booked buyers and the people they bring. Off-site: **S3 and S7**, and only inside the December–January window |
| **Funnel stage** | On-site: **Referral (11), Advocacy (12)**. Off-site: Interest (3), Lead (5) |
| **Expected contribution** | On-site events are the delivery mechanism for a meaningful share of the 70 referral bookings. Off-site events: unproven, and unfunded until the referral engine exists |
| **KPI** | On-site: attendance, guests brought per owner, **referrals generated per event**. Off-site: cost per qualified lead against the LinkedIn and Meta benchmark |
| **Measurement** | Event attendance logged to CRM; referrals attributed to the event that produced them |
| **Strength** | On-site events do something no media buy can: **they let a prospect meet an existing buyer**, which is the direct answer to S1's opening objection |
| **Weakness** | Off-site events are expensive per contact, hard to fill without an existing brand, and easy to justify emotionally. **In year one there is nothing to show at a roadshow that a video call cannot show more cheaply** |

## 4. Cost per booking by channel — and why this table is a trap

**Computed.** Direct spend divided by attributed bookings, excluding the brand and infrastructure layers that serve every channel.

| Channel | Spend | Bookings | **Direct cost per booking** |
| --- | --: | --: | --: |
| **Referral** | ₹1.20 Cr | 70 | **₹1.71 L** |
| Property portals | ₹1.00 Cr | 34 | ₹2.94 L |
| **Channel partners** | ₹3.34 Cr | 105 | **₹3.18 L** |
| SEO / organic | ₹0.40 Cr | 12 | ₹3.33 L |
| Google Display retargeting | ₹0.30 Cr | 8 | ₹3.75 L |
| Google Search | ₹2.30 Cr | 52 | ₹4.42 L |
| Meta | ₹3.45 Cr | 68 | ₹5.07 L |
| LinkedIn | ₹0.50 Cr | 9 | ₹5.56 L |
| YouTube demand gen | ₹1.10 Cr | 18 | ₹6.11 L |
| **Outdoor · Print · PR · brand content** | **₹5.00 Cr** | **—** | **not attributable** |
| **Website · CRM · experience centre** | **₹5.25 Cr** | **—** | **serves all channels** |

> **This is precisely the table Phase 8 §4.3 warned about, and it is included here so that it can be read correctly rather than discovered later on a dashboard.**
>
> Read naively, it says: cut outdoor, print, PR, the website and the experience centre — they produce nothing — and move ₹10.25 crore into portals and referral, which look cheapest.
>
> **Every part of that conclusion is wrong.** Portals look cheap because their leads are cheap, not because their buyers are; their leads carry the worst qualification rate in the plan. Referral looks cheap because 70 buyers were persuaded by a product, a site visit and a delivery record that cost ₹10.25 crore to build — costs that appear in the unattributable rows. **The experience centre "produces nothing" while carrying ₹39.4 crore per point of conversion.**

**Three rules for using this table.**

1. **Compare within a layer, never across layers.** Meta against Search against portals is a fair comparison — same layer, same job. Meta against outdoor is not.
2. **Judge demand-generation channels on cost per *qualified* lead, not cost per booking or cost per lead.** Cost per booking imports last-click distortion; cost per lead rewards the volume Phase 8 showed is worth least.
3. **The brand, website, CRM and experience-centre lines are judged by the booking-stage first-touch field and by holdout tests** — never by a cost-per-booking figure they cannot produce.

## 5. Channel plan by journey

Phase 8 established four journeys carrying very different shares of revenue. **The channel mix differs sharply between them**, and a single blended plan would serve none of them well.

| | **Journey 1 · Linear** | **Journey 2 · Referral-entry** | **Journey 3 · Window** | **Journey 4 · Document** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Revenue** | ₹169.2 Cr · 33.4% | ₹153.5 Cr · 30.3% | ₹155.4 Cr · 30.7% | ₹27.9 Cr · 5.5% |
| **Segments** | S5 · S6 · most of S2 | S1 · connected S2 | S3 · S7 · S8 | S4 |
| **Primary channels** | Meta · Google Search · portals · website | **Channel partners · referral · site visit** | **YouTube · Meta · LinkedIn · WhatsApp · video consultation** | **In-person village relations · Telugu print and YouTube** |
| **Supporting** | Outdoor · print · CRM · WhatsApp | Search *(verification)* · PR · outdoor · the CA and advocate | Email · SEO · December–January events · portals *(NRI listings)* | The term sheet · the local advocate · a completed precedent |
| **Channels that do not work** | — | **Meta and portals as discovery** — Phase 5: *digital is a verification channel, not a discovery channel* | **Anything requiring a physical visit** — the buyer is 13,000 km away for eleven months | **All media.** *"Does not respond to advertising; it responds to a document"* |
| **The binding constraint** | Cost per qualified lead in a market with ~3,269 competing units | **Whether fifteen visible local buyers exist yet** | **Qualified-lead-to-visit at 18.2%** — the worst stage in the model | **Whether one neighbour has completed an exchange and been treated as promised** |

> **Reasoned — the mix that follows.** Journey 1 is a conventional paid-media problem and takes the majority of Meta, Search and portal budget. **Journey 2, at 30.3% of revenue, takes almost no media at all** — its budget is channel-partner commission, the referral programme and the experience centre. **Journey 3, at 30.7%, is where the December–January concentration and the video-consultation capability live.** Journey 4 has no media line; it has a headcount line.
>
> **Two of the four journeys — 36% of revenue — are served primarily by things that are not media buys.** A channel strategy that allocates only across media would leave them unresourced.

## 6. Year one is not steady state

**Established — Phase 8, risk 2.** Referral requires prior buyers. **At launch there are none.** The 18.5% referral share is a steady-state figure, and treating it as a flat annual assumption would leave a hole in year one.

**Computed.**

| | Year one |
| --- | --- |
| Bookings at base run-rate | ~101 |
| The 18.5% that cannot come from referral | **~19 bookings** |
| Cost to re-source through media at ₹10.52 L CAC | **₹2.01 Cr** |
| Cost to re-source through channel partners at ₹3.18 L | **₹0.61 Cr** |
| **Saving from routing the gap to channel partners rather than media** | **₹1.41 Cr** |

> **The year-one recommendation follows directly: over-weight channel partners, not media.** A channel-partner network can be stood up in weeks and costs nothing until it sells; buying 19 additional bookings through paid media costs ₹1.41 crore more for the same units.

**And the advocacy budget's year-one job changes.** In year one, ₹1.20 crore of advocacy spend is not harvesting referrals — there is nothing to harvest. **It is manufacturing the first fifteen visible, named local buyers** whose existence answers S1's opening question and unlocks Journey 2 in year two. Phase 6 said the same thing in product terms: seed Community D's first tranche rather than advertise it.

| Phase | Referral share | What the advocacy budget is doing |
| --- | --: | --- |
| **Year 1** | ~0% | Manufacturing advocates. Seeding D. Owner events with the first cohort |
| **Year 2** | ~15% | First referrals landing; the programme formalised |
| **Years 3–4** | **25%+** | Compounding. Above the 18.5% average, which is what makes the average work |

## 7. Risks

| # | Risk | Why it matters | Test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1** | **The per-channel booking split is Reasoned, not observed** | It sets ₹9.84 Cr of demand-generation allocation | The booking-stage first-touch field. **Two quarters settles it** |
| **2** | **Cost per booking by channel will be produced by someone, and read naively** | §4 shows the naive reading defunds ₹10.25 Cr of the highest-value spend | Agree the three reading rules in §4 **before** the first quarterly review, not during it |
| **3** | **CPLs are Phase 7 benchmarks from agency sources, adjusted by judgement** | Every channel budget scales off them | A **₹15–20 lakh paid test across all four community audiences before the main budget commits.** Six weeks, and it repositions the whole allocation if wrong |
| **4** | **The experience centre is sized for average, not peak** | ₹4.10 Cr, the largest line, serving the ₹39.4 Cr-per-point stage | Size for **peak Sunday** — Phase 8 estimated 35–45 people on site. Appointment system for S2 and S3 to keep Sunday free for walk-in families |
| **5** | **WhatsApp runs outside the CRM** | It is the primary conversational channel for three segments, and by default it lives on personal phones | API integration on day one. **Un-integrated, it is not a channel — it is a liability** |
| **6** | **Channel partners sell on discount** | 28% of bookings sit with people the developer does not control, and Phase 6's two pricing rules are the architecture's load-bearing constraint | The monthly discount register, **segmented by partner**. A partner whose average discount exceeds the direct team's is a pricing problem, not a sales asset |
| **7** | **Outdoor and print — ₹3.30 Cr — cannot be defended under click reporting** | The two lines most likely to be cut for looking unproductive | Recall survey and a geo or time-based holdout, **agreed before the spend** |
| **8** | **This phase assumes Phase 6's prices hold** | Unchanged through Phases 6, 7 and 8: **31% of revenue sits in a band nobody has proven is occupied** | Not a channel question. **No channel mix compensates for a price the market refuses** |

## 8. Carry-forward

**The verdict on twenty-two channels.**
- **Load-bearing (4):** experiential/site marketing · referral · channel partners · WhatsApp
- **Necessary at scale (5):** Meta · Google Search · website and landing pages · outdoor · CRM
- **Necessary but small (5):** YouTube · SEO · print · PR · email
- **Presence only, capped (3):** property portals · LinkedIn · events
- **Cut or reclassified (5):** Google Display prospecting *(cut; retargeting retained)* · Instagram *(a Meta placement)* · hoardings *(the same as outdoor)* · NRI marketing *(an audience, not a channel)* · broad email acquisition *(cut)*

**The budget — ₹25.68 Cr.**

| Layer | Spend | Largest line within it |
| --- | --: | --- |
| Brand building | ₹6.30 Cr | Outdoor ₹2.10 Cr |
| Demand generation | ₹9.84 Cr | Meta ₹3.45 Cr |
| Sales conversion | ₹8.34 Cr | **Experience centre + show homes ₹4.10 Cr** |
| Customer advocacy | ₹1.20 Cr | Referral programme ₹0.70 Cr |

**Four positions carried forward.**
1. **The largest single channel line is the experience centre, not Meta.** That follows from Phase 8's finding that a point at the site-visit stage is worth ₹39.4 crore, and from Phase 7's finding that conversion beats media price. **This allocation is what those findings look like as a budget.**
2. **Two of the four journeys — 36% of revenue — are served primarily by things that are not media buys.** Journey 2 runs on channel partners, referral and the site. Journey 4 runs on a term sheet and a village relationship. A channel plan allocating only across media leaves them unresourced.
3. **Year one over-weights channel partners, not media.** Routing the year-one referral gap of ~19 bookings to partners rather than to paid media saves **₹1.41 crore** for the same units — and the advocacy budget's year-one job is manufacturing advocates, not harvesting referrals.
4. **Cost per booking by channel is a trap, and it will be produced anyway.** Compare within a layer, never across. Judge demand generation on cost per *qualified* lead. Judge brand, website, CRM and the experience centre on the booking-stage first-touch field and on holdouts.

**One correction to Phase 8.** Referral is **70 bookings, not 71** — the source split derives as 200.8 marketing-sourced, 105.5 channel partner and 69.7 referral, which rounds to 201 / 105 / 70. That makes referral **₹1.71 lakh per booking** and **~₹94.5 crore of revenue**, at **6.1× cheaper** than marketing-sourced rather than 6.2×.

**Open.** The eight risks in §7 — of which risk 3 (a ₹15–20 lakh pre-launch media test) is the cheapest and highest-value thing to do before committing the budget, and risk 8 remains what it has been since Phase 6: **not a marketing question, and larger than all the others.**

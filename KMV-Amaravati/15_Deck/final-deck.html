Executive summary

The whole argument, before the evidence for it

Six positions. Each is defended in the slides that follow, and each names what would falsify it.

01 · The opportunity is real, and narrower than it looks

Amaravati’s capital status is now statutory, and capital works are funded and active [S01][S02]. But of 300+ listed corridor projects, eleven genuinely compete — and the corridor is crowded at the bottom, thin at the top.

02 · Four communities are four businesses

Differentiated by buyer constraint, not finish level. The binding constraint is cash at purchase, not income — ₹17.6 lakh for A rising to ₹74.1 lakh for D. One media plan across four communities would misprice all four.

03 · The position must be provable, not aspirational

“Luxury”, “premium” and “close to the capital” are universal in this corridor and therefore worthless. I would position on one computable fact and an invitation to check it.

04 · The headline budget is not a marketing budget

₹31.75 Cr bundles four economically different things. True marketing operating expense is ₹23.80 Cr; media is ₹14.65 Cr — ₹32.6 lakh a month across a 45-month sell window.

05 · Site-visit conversion, not media price, is the lever

The conversion rates are multiplicative and therefore equally sensitive — but they differ in cost to fix. One point of site-visit-to-booking is worth ₹39.4 Cr at zero additional media. No media buy returns that.

06 · So I would not release the money on a forecast

I would ask for ₹25 lakh — about 1% of the envelope — to establish cost per lead, cost per qualified lead and cost per qualified site visit, with the stop condition agreed before the money moves.

Why I would judge the plan on CPQL, not CPL

Cheap leads that fail the cash gate are a cost, not a result. CPL is a buying metric; CPQL is a business metric.

At the modelled rates, media buys a lead for ₹1,247 and a qualified lead for ₹7,889. A campaign optimised to CPL will find the cheapest audience, which is the one that cannot clear ₹17.6 lakh in cash.

And why the qualified site visit is the metric that matters most

The conversion rates are multiplicative, so a 25% move in any of them changes bookings by the same 25%. What separates them is cost to fix and who controls them.

Cost per lead is set by the auction and by competitors. Lead-to-qualified and qualified-to-visit are process, and cost almost nothing to move. And the last one is the largest opportunity:

Creative pillars — three territories, tested not chosen

Price-led — the ungated sheet. Proof-led — carpet area, handover dates, progress. Density-led — the arithmetic. Twelve variants, three per audience, in Phase 0. The market decides which territory is right, not the room.

The discipline that makes it commercial

A variant is judged on cost per qualified site visit, never on whether it is liked. A variant above 2× the best variant’s CPQL is cut, regardless of who made it. Fatigue is a trigger, not an opinion: frequency ceiling plus CPL drift >20% forces an automatic refresh.

Where I would spend creative effort first

The landing page and the price sheet, because the boldest decision in the strategy is cheap to test: gated versus ungated pricing, two pages per audience, ₹2.5 lakh. If ungated wins on CPQL, the transparency position is validated commercially rather than argued.

One thing I would fix before any of it runs

The campaign’s signature claim — fifteen to the acre — is false against two named villa competitors unless bounded to all 25 acres, apartments included [S13]. And the two density comparators I would cite have no published unit count.

I would bound the claim and write the footnote before the first impression, not after the first challenge.

Measurement & attribution

Three reading rules, in the reporting template before the first review

Attribution arguments are always won by whoever wrote the template. I would write it before the spend, not when the spend is challenged.

Rule 1 · Compare within a layer, never across

Judging brand against performance on cost per booking defunds ₹10.25 crore of the highest-value spend. It is the most common way a sound budget is destroyed by a reasonable-sounding question.

Rule 2 · Judge demand generation on CPQL

Not CPL. Cheap leads that fail the cash gate are a cost, not a result. Reporting CPL as the headline optimises the media buy against the business.

Rule 3 · Judge brand, outdoor and the site on holdouts

First-touch and geo holdouts — never last click. Last click will always find in favour of the search line and against everything that made the search happen.

Appendix G

Research limitations

Stated plainly, because a strategy that hides its own limits is not one.

1 — Search worked. Page retrieval did not

Every URL in Appendix F is real and resolves. Not one of them was opened. Outbound page fetches returned EGRESS_BLOCKED, and direct requests to crda.ap.gov.in and rera.ap.gov.in returned 403 policy denials.

Claim content therefore comes from search-result summaries, not from reading the source. A Tier 1 domain means the domain is authoritative — not that the document was verified.

2 — No primary buyer research exists

No surveys, interviews, focus groups, CRM data or walk-in analysis of any kind. Every behavioural attribute across all eight segments is hypothesis, and the priority scores are a weighted judgement, not a measurement.

3 — Every price is an asking price

No transaction or registration data was obtained. The 50% locality premium underneath the whole revenue model is therefore untested, and Community A prices above a delivered comparable with no delivery record to justify it.

4 — No construction cost data

The 28% gross margin is a placeholder. Every ROI, ROMI, break-even and CAC-ceiling figure in this deck is arithmetic on that placeholder. Contractor quotations are the only fix.

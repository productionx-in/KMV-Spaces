The honest position on benchmarks

Phases 1–5 could reach government registries, RERA filings, developer price lists and published pay scales. Phase 7 needs a different class of data — funnel conversion rates, cost per lead, channel-partner economics — and no authoritative body in India publishes it.

What exists is published by agencies selling lead generation, CRM vendors selling CRM, and consultancies selling consulting. They are used here because they are the only published figures available, labelled with that caveat, and not upgraded to fact by repetition.

Two consequences run through this document. Funnel rates are presented as scenario ranges rather than point estimates, because a single number would imply a precision the sources do not support. And the model is calibrated against one external anchor — industry-normal marketing spend of 4–5% of gross development value — with the base case required to land inside it. A model producing a far cheaper answer than the industry norm would be telling me my assumptions were wrong, not that I had found an efficiency.

Where the published sources conflict

The single most important input in the model — site-visit-to-booking conversion — has published figures that disagree by a factor of four.

8–20% typical, 32–51% for high performers Benchmark, interested party · versus 3–8% for the ₹50 lakh – ₹3 crore band Benchmark, interested party — and that lower figure is segment-specific to exactly the band this project sits in.

This is not resolvable from published data. The model uses 5.0% / 7.0% / 8.5% at project level — inside the segment-specific band for conservative and base, reaching its top for aggressive. It does not use the 32–51% figure at all. A model that assumed high-performer conversion for a first-time launch would be assuming away the hardest part of the problem.

The cost lines that are not lead generation

Omitting these is what makes a real-estate marketing model look implausibly cheap.

Brand & content — brand campaign, print, hoardings, PR, film, collateral, launch events: ₹9.50 / ₹7.50 / ₹6.00 Cr Assumption

Sales infrastructure — experience centre, two show homes, CRM and martech stack: ₹6.20 / ₹5.00 / ₹4.00 Cr Assumption

Phase 5 found show-flat quality "matters disproportionately because it is the only tangible evidence available", and that Community C and D's buyers "visit two or three times before committing" with 3–6 family members. A scheme selling a ₹2.5 crore villa on the strength of a masterplan that does not exist yet needs a built experience centre and a furnished villa. Treating that as anything other than a marketing cost hides it.

Channel partner commission is a separate cost class. Benchmark Published Indian rates: 1–3% of agreement value typically, 2–3% for luxury and plotted development. Assumption 3.0% / 2.5% / 2.0%, applied only to channel-partner-sourced bookings. It is pay-on-performance — it costs nothing until a unit sells and needs no working capital. Reporting a "marketing cost" that excludes it understates acquisition; blending it in overstates media efficiency. This model reports both, separately.

§5

The finding the strategy did not anticipate

Phase 5 ranked the diaspora as the highest-priority segment. It was right about the value and silent about the cost of reaching it. The funnel supplies the missing half.

Three reasonable inputs, compounding into one problem

1 · B has the highest marketing dependency — 70% of its bookings must be marketing-sourced, because Phase 5 established the diaspora is reachable "digitally, entirely."

2 · B has the highest CPL — ₹1,600 base, because overseas geo-targeting of a narrow affinity audience is the most expensive media in the plan.

3 · B has the worst qualified-lead-to-visit rate — a ×0.70 modifier, because the buyer often physically cannot visit: a proxy relative goes first, and the personal visit is compressed into a single India trip.

Multiply the three and B needs 682 leads per booking against Community A's 219.

Phase 5 ranked the US diaspora the highest-priority segment at 4.20, on revenue potential, cash-gate clearance and low competition. That ranking was right about the value of the segment and silent about the cost of reaching it.

What follows — and what does not

What does not follow: abandoning Community B. It is 22% of revenue, it serves the 45% of top-end demand Phase 6 built it for, and its CAC is still only 8.1% of ticket in the base case — high, but not disqualifying.

1 · Move B off paid media
  • Every point of B's marketing-sourced share that shifts to referral or institutional channels removes roughly ₹9 lakh of acquisition cost per unit
  • Phase 5 named the channels no competitor was using: APNRTS, headquartered in Guntur; diaspora associations; origin-district rather than current-city targeting
  • And the inspecting relative in India — who holds effective veto and is almost never the person the media is aimed at. Marketing to that relative is local media at local CPL against a lead that converts like a resident
2 · Fix the visit stage, not the lead stage
  • B's binding constraint is qualified-lead-to-visit at 18.2%, not CPL
  • A credible remote alternative — a scheduled live video walkthrough with the sales lead, a proxy-visit protocol for the relative, a recorded specification walk — attacks the ×0.70 modifier directly
  • Moving B's Q→V from 18.2% to 25% cuts its lead requirement by 27% and its lead-gen spend by roughly ₹1.7 crore, for the cost of a process
3 · Concentrate B's media in the windows that exist
  • Phase 5: this segment buys during the December–January and summer India visits, and "if the site is not ready to close in that window, the sale is lost for a year"
  • Spending evenly across twelve months on an audience that can only transact in four is the single most avoidable waste in this plan
The mirror finding

Community C is the most efficient product in the scheme — 21.6% of revenue for 7.2% of the lead-generation budget, at a CAC of ₹2.6 lakh on a ₹1.82 crore ticket. 1.4%.

The reason is structural, not tactical: 55% of C's bookings arrive through channel partners and referral, which cost commission and reputation rather than media. Phase 5 found S1 "runs on referral… digital is a verification channel, not a discovery channel."

The cheapest revenue in this project comes from the segment that cannot be bought with advertising.

That is an instruction, not an observation. The referral and channel-partner engine is worth more to this project's economics than any media efficiency available — and it is built with delivery, seeding and relationships, the things Phase 5 said to start early because they compound slowly.

ROAS is not a return

Calculated ROAS on gross development value: 14.5× / 22.7× / 32.7×. Those numbers are real and close to meaningless as a measure of return.

ROAS here divides gross sales value by marketing spend. It takes no account of land, construction, finance or approvals — which in a residential development are the overwhelming majority of the cost.

A 22.7× ROAS does not mean marketing returns 22.7 rupees for every rupee. It means every rupee of marketing is associated with 22.7 rupees of turnover, most of which is spent building the thing that was sold. Quoting it as a return would be the single most misleading number available in this document — which is why it appears here with the caveat attached rather than in a headline.

To express a return, marketing cost has to be set against margin, not turnover. Assumption — development gross margin of 25% / 28% / 31% of GDV, before land finance. What the ranking says

A 2-point fall in site-visit-to-booking conversion costs more than a 25% rise in media prices.

The model says the sales gallery, the show homes and the follow-up discipline are worth more than media buying — which is consistent with Phase 5's finding that this catchment's most valuable segments do not discover projects through advertising at all.

The honest hierarchy

Items 1–5 are marketing questions, and all of them are answerable with 60–90 days of live data plus one pre-launch media test.

Item 8 is not a marketing question, and it is larger than all the others combined.

This model is precise about the cost of selling 376 units at these prices. It takes no position on whether the prices hold. Phase 6 does that, and it says 31% of revenue sits in a band nobody has proven is occupied.

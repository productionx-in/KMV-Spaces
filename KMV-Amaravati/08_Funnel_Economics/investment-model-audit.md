# Marketing Investment Model — Audit

## The ₹31.75 crore is not too big. It is four different kinds of money wearing one label — and it breaches a ceiling this research set for itself

**Purpose:** an adversarial audit of the investment model, commissioned to test whether it is defensible rather than to defend it.
**Method:** every figure re-derived from source; prior conclusions treated as claims, not findings.
**Outcome:** the headline number survives as a *lifecycle total*. It does **not** survive as a marketing budget, and the research contains a self-generated red flag nobody caught.

---

## 1. The verdict

**Primary: E — fundamentally the wrong way to frame the budget.**
**Secondary: C — reasonable only as a full-lifecycle, total-acquisition figure.**
**Live condition: B — insufficiently evidenced, and now demonstrably so.**

**Not A.** Per unit the number is not extreme: ₹8.44 lakh against a ₹1.35 crore ticket, which is **22.4% of gross profit at the assumed 28% margin.** That is a defensible ratio, not a reckless one.

**Not D.** Nothing here supports calling it conservative. No catchment data exists.

### 1.1 Why E, stated plainly

**₹31.75 crore bundles four economically different things and calls the total a marketing budget.**

| What it actually is | Amount | Share | Who should own it |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| **True marketing opex** | **₹23.80 Cr** | **75.0%** | The CMO |
| — *of which media* | *₹14.65 Cr* | *46.1%* | *The CMO* |
| **Cost of sale** — channel-partner commission ₹3.34 Cr + referral rewards ₹0.70 Cr | **₹4.04 Cr** | 12.7% | **Sales P&L.** Costs nothing until a unit sells |
| **Sales infrastructure capex** — experience centre, two show-home interiors, masterplan models, landscaping | **₹3.15 Cr** | 9.9% | **Project capex.** A building, not a campaign |
| **Technology capex** — website build, walkthrough platform, analytics stack, CRM implementation | **₹0.76 Cr** | 2.4% | **IT capex**, amortised |

> **This is not an accounting quibble. It is the entire reason the number sounds reckless.**
>
> **₹14.65 crore of media across 45 months is ₹32.6 lakh a month.** That is an ordinary regional media budget for a four-community development. It stops sounding like ₹31 crore the moment it is described correctly.

### 1.2 The finding that should have stopped the model

**Phase 5 §5.5 computed an allowable marketing cost of ₹5.0–6.3 lakh per unit.** Phase 7 respected it. **Phase 11 breached it, and no phase noticed.**

| Model | Cost per unit | Against the ₹6.3 L ceiling |
| --- | --: | --- |
| Phase 5 allowable | ₹5.0–6.3 L | *the ceiling* |
| Phase 7 — marketing only | ₹5.94 L | **Inside** |
| Phase 7 — all-in incl. commission | ₹6.83 L | 8% above |
| **Phase 11 — all-in** | **₹8.44 L** | **34% above** |
| **Phase 11 — marketing opex only, reclassified** | **₹6.33 L** | **At the ceiling** |

> **The two corrections converge on the same number, independently.**
>
> Strip cost of sale and capex out of the marketing line and you get **₹23.80 crore = ₹6.33 lakh per unit.** The ceiling this research set for itself was **₹6.30 lakh.** A 0.5% difference.
>
> **That is the strongest evidence in this audit that the reclassification is right and the ₹31.75 crore label is wrong.** The overshoot (₹8.06 Cr) is almost exactly the misclassified money (₹7.95 Cr).

### 1.3 A methodological problem in how the number was built

Phase 11 presented a **bottom-up** budget — quantity × unit cost, thirteen lines — and reported that it exceeded the top-down allocation by 28%.

**But the largest line is not bottom-up.** ₹9.84 crore of media was **imported directly from Phase 7's funnel projection**, which itself runs backwards from a booking target using assumed CPLs and assumed conversion rates.

- **₹11.02 crore — 33% of the bottom-up total — is a top-down model wearing a bottom-up label.**
- Because the media figure is *identical on both sides*, it cancels in the reconciliation. The "+28%" gap is real for the other two-thirds and **overstates the independence of the two methods.**
- **Nothing in the exercise priced media from a quote, a rate card, or an observed auction.**

## 3. What we know, assume, need to test, and recommend

### 3.1 What we know

**Very little, and it is important to say so.**

- The corridor's **asking** prices: ₹4,062/sq ft locality average, ₹5,100 at a delivered comparable.
- **Amaravati is AP's sole capital by statute** — works funded and active.
- **AIIMS Mangalagiri is operational**, ~3 km away.
- Land pooling is **real and sized** — 33,000+ acres phase 1, 16,666.57 acres authorised in phase 2.
- **Published Indian rate bands** for outdoor, CRM seats, PR retainers and channel-partner commission.

### 3.2 What we assume — every one of these is unverified

| Assumption | Value | Status |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cost per lead by community** | A ₹350 · B ₹1,600 · C ₹900 · D ₹1,900 | **ASSUMPTION.** Agency benchmarks from other markets. **Not catchment-specific** |
| **Lead → qualified** | 15.8% | **ASSUMPTION** |
| **Qualified → site visit** | 23.4% | **ASSUMPTION** |
| **Site visit → booking** | 6.9% | **ASSUMPTION**, anchored to a published 3–8% band for the ticket range |
| **Source mix** | 53% marketing / 28% channel / 19% referral | **ASSUMPTION** |
| **Development gross margin** | 25% / 28% / 31% | **ASSUMPTION.** No cost data exists |
| **Sell window** | 45 months + 6 pre-launch | **ASSUMPTION** |
| **Achievable price ladder** | ₹5,150–7,200/sq ft | **ASSUMPTION.** 50% above locality asking |
| **Agency fee** | 12% of media | **ASSUMPTION** |
| **Marketing team structure and cost** | **Not modelled at all** | **GAP — see §9 objection 9** |

**Not assumed, because it would be invention:** KMV's actual marketing budget, current media spend, internal headcount, agency roster, historical CPL, sales velocity or inventory position. **None of these is publicly available and none is estimated here.**

### 3.3 What we need to test

**In priority order, by what a wrong answer costs:**

1. **Catchment CPL and CPQL**, by community audience — underwrites ₹14.65 Cr of media.
2. **Cost per qualified site visit** — the only conversion metric a short test can measure honestly.
3. **Site-visit-to-booking rate** — the highest-leverage variable, and the one that **cannot** be tested quickly (§6.1).
4. **Whether the price ladder clears** — a 50% locality premium is unproven.
5. **What the developer's existing villa product actually sold for, and how fast** — free, and it would settle more than any test.

### 3.4 What we recommend

> **Do not ask for ₹31.75 crore. Do not ask for ₹23.80 crore.**
>
> **Ask for ₹25 lakh, and a decision rule.**

The full-lifecycle figure belongs in the appendix as a **ceiling under governance**, not in the ask. §10 and §11 set out the gates.

## 4. Scenario 1 — Validation / test

**Objective: discover the economics, not generate sales.** A test that is judged on bookings will be judged on noise.

### 4.1 Design

| Parameter | Value |
| --- | --- |
| **Media spend** | **₹18.0 lakh** |
| **Non-media** — landing pages, creative production, tracking build, analyst time | **₹7.0 lakh** |
| **TOTAL TEST INVESTMENT** | **₹25.0 lakh** |
| **Duration** | **8 weeks** — 2 setup, 6 live |
| **Audiences tested** | **4**: S1 trade families · S2 senior medical (AIIMS radius) · S3 US diaspora (Telugu-origin districts) · S6 local upgraders |
| **Channels** | Meta, Google Search, one portal, YouTube. **Nothing else** — a test with nine channels measures nothing |
| **Creative variants** | **12** — 3 per audience: one price-led, one proof-led, one density-led |
| **Landing pages** | **2 per audience** — gated enquiry vs **ungated price sheet.** This tests the strategy's boldest decision cheaply |
| **Offer tested** | Site-visit booking vs brochure download |

### 4.2 What it will produce, at assumed rates

**≈1,443 leads → 228 qualified → 53 site visits → 3.7 bookings**

### 4.3 What it can and cannot measure — the design decision that matters

| Metric | Events | 95% CI | Verdict |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| **CPL** | 1,443 | ±5.2% | **MEASURABLE** |
| **Lead → qualified rate** | 1,443 | ±12.3% | **MEASURABLE** |
| **CPQL** | 228 | ±13.0% | **MEASURABLE** |
| Qualified → visit rate | 228 | ±30.9% | Directional |
| Cost per site visit | 53 | ±26.8% | Directional |
| Site-visit show rate | 53 | ±26.8% | Directional |
| Visit → booking rate | 53 | ±26.8% | Directional |
| **Cost per booking** | **3.7** | **±102%** | **NOT MEASURABLE** |

> **A ₹25 lakh test cannot establish a booking rate, and any model that claims otherwise is wrong.**
>
> **To measure site-visit-to-booking to ±25% would need ~832 site visits — roughly 22 months of continuous spend at test run-rate.**
>
> **So the test is judged on cost per qualified site visit.** Booking rate is carried forward as a labelled assumption and measured over the first two quarters of live trading — which is the only honest way to get it.

### 4.4 Decision thresholds

| Result | Threshold | Action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Blended CPL** | ≤ ₹1,550 *(assumption +25%)* | **Proceed** to Scenario 2 |
| | ₹1,550–1,900 | Proceed at **70% of planned media**; re-test the two worst audiences |
| | > ₹1,900 *(+50%)* | **Stop.** Re-base the entire model before any launch commitment |
| **Cost per qualified site visit** | ≤ ₹42,000 *(assumption +25%)* | Proceed |
| | > ₹52,000 | **Stop.** The funnel, not the media, is broken |
| **Ungated price sheet** | ≥ gated on cost per qualified visit | **Publish ungated** — and the transparency position is validated commercially, not just strategically |
| **Any single audience** | CPQSV > 2× the best audience | **Cut it** from the launch plan |

### 4.5 How the test changes the full-year budget

**This is the mechanism, and it is the point of the whole exercise.**

| If observed CPL is | Media required to hold 201 bookings | Change | Recommended response |
| --- | --: | --: | --- |
| **As assumed** | ₹9.84 Cr | — | Proceed as modelled |
| **+25%** | ₹12.30 Cr | **+₹2.46 Cr** | **Do not fund the increase.** Hold media, accept 161 bookings, and re-cut inventory release |
| **+50%** | ₹14.76 Cr | **+₹4.92 Cr** | **Stop and re-plan.** The unit economics no longer clear the allowable |
| **−20%** | ₹7.87 Cr | **−₹1.97 Cr** | **Do not bank the saving.** Redeploy into the site-visit stage, where a point is worth ₹39.4 Cr |

**The asymmetry is deliberate:** a worse-than-expected CPL triggers a *volume* decision, not a *budget increase*. That is the discipline a CFO is looking for.

### 6.1 Returns, on gross profit — never on revenue

| Gross margin | Gross profit | GP less marketing | **ROMI** | **CAC as % of GP** | Break-even |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| **25%** *(ASSUMPTION)* | ₹126.7 Cr | ₹94.9 Cr | **2.99×** | **25.1%** | 94 units |
| **28%** *(ASSUMPTION)* | ₹141.9 Cr | ₹110.1 Cr | **3.47×** | **22.4%** | 84 units |
| **31%** *(ASSUMPTION)* | ₹157.1 Cr | ₹125.3 Cr | **3.95×** | **20.2%** | 76 units |

> **ROMI = (gross profit − marketing investment) ÷ marketing investment.**
>
> Revenue ÷ spend would read **16.0×**. It is a meaningless number — most of that revenue pays for construction, land and finance. **Any deck showing 16× ROAS on a property development should be disbelieved, including this one.**

**Break-even at 76–94 units of 376** is the number that makes the case: **the scheme is 4.0–4.9× past marketing break-even.** That is the honest margin of safety, and it holds across all three margin assumptions.

## 7. Sensitivity analysis

**Media held constant; bookings and revenue flex.**

| Case | Leads | Bookings | Δ bookings | **Δ revenue** |
| --- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| **BASE** | 78,880 | 201.0 | — | — |
| CPL 25% worse | 63,104 | 160.8 | **−40.2** | **−₹54.2 Cr** |
| CPL 50% worse | 52,587 | 134.0 | **−67.0** | **−₹90.3 Cr** |
| CPQL 25% worse | 78,880 | 150.8 | **−50.3** | **−₹67.7 Cr** |
| Site-visit rate 25% worse | 78,880 | 150.8 | **−50.3** | **−₹67.7 Cr** |
| Visit→booking 25% worse | 78,880 | 150.8 | **−50.2** | **−₹67.7 Cr** |
| **Visit→booking 25% better** | 78,880 | 251.2 | **+50.2** | **+₹67.7 Cr** |
| **Visit→booking 50% better** | 78,880 | 301.5 | **+100.5** | **+₹135.4 Cr** |

### 7.1 Which variable has the largest economic impact

**The honest answer is two answers, and giving one is a mistake.**

**The three conversion rates are multiplicative, so a 25% move in *any* of them changes bookings by exactly 25% — 50 bookings, ₹67.7 crore.** They are arithmetically identical. What separates them is **cost to fix and who controls them.**

| Variable | Who controls it | Cost to move it 25% | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **CPL** | The auction and competitors | **₹2.46–4.92 Cr of media** | **Largest risk** — least controllable, entirely unverified |
| Lead → qualified | Our CRM and qualification criteria | **Process. Near zero** | Cheapest fix |
| Qualified → site visit | Our follow-up and offer | **Process. Near zero** | Cheapest fix |
| **Site visit → booking** | Our sales team and the site | **Training and site experience. Low** | **Largest opportunity** |

> **CPL is the biggest risk. Site-visit-to-booking is the biggest opportunity. They are different variables and they call for opposite responses.**
>
> **One point of visit-to-booking — 6.9% to 7.9% — is worth ₹39.4 crore at zero additional media.** No amount of media buying produces that return.
>
> **This corrects Phase 11's headline.** Its "every ₹1 cut from media risks ₹24.60" framing made media the hero. The arithmetic says media is the *most expensive* place to buy a booking and the *least* controllable. **Phase 7 had this right and later phases lost it.**

## 9. CEO view — what releases the next ₹1 crore

**Gates, not persuasion. Each is a number agreed in advance and a named decision-maker.**

| Gate | Measure | Release condition | Stop condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Lead economics** | Blended CPL | ≤ ₹1,550 | > ₹1,900 |
| **Lead quality** | Lead → qualified | ≥ 12% | < 9% |
| **Intent** | Cost per **qualified site visit** | ≤ ₹42,000 | > ₹52,000 |
| **Show discipline** | Site-visit show rate | ≥ 60% | < 45% |
| **Pipeline** | Negotiations per 100 visits | ≥ 15 | < 10 |
| **Conversion** | Site visit → booking *(2 quarters)* | ≥ 5.5% | < 4.0% |
| **Unit economics** | Cost per booking, all-in | ≤ ₹8.44 L | > ₹11.00 L |

> **The rule that makes this credible:** *no gate, no cheque.* And the corollary the CEO should insist on — **a gate missed twice consecutively releases nothing, regardless of explanation.**

## 10. Board-level budget governance

**The budget is never released as one cheque. Five phases, each with a gate, an owner, a scale condition and a stop condition.**

| Phase | Release | KPI gate to pass | Decision maker | Scale condition | Stop condition | Reallocation rule |
| --- | --: | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **0 · Validation** | **₹0.25 Cr** | CPL ≤ ₹1,550 · CPQSV ≤ ₹42,000 | **CMO** | Both gates met | CPL > ₹1,900 | Any audience at >2× best CPQSV is cut before Phase 1 |
| **1 · Launch** | **₹8.89 Cr** *(12 months)* | 65 bookings · CPB ≤ ₹13.7 L · show rate ≥ 60% | **CEO + CFO** | ≥ 55 bookings **and** CPL within +25% | < 40 bookings, or CPL > +50% | Underspend moves to **site-visit stage**, never banked |
| **2 · Scale** | **₹12.00 Cr** *(months 13–30)* | V2B ≥ 5.5% over 2 quarters · CAC ≤ ₹9.5 L | **CEO + CFO** | V2B ≥ 6.5% | V2B < 4.0%, or B fails its 25% Q→V gate | Community failing its gate has **inventory cut, not budget raised** |
| **3 · Optimise** | **₹7.50 Cr** *(months 31–42)* | Referral ≥ 0.15/owner-yr · CAC falling QoQ | **CMO**, CFO notified | Referral share rising | CAC rising 2 quarters running | Shift from paid to referral and channel as they prove cheaper |
| **4 · Mature / referral** | **₹3.11 Cr** *(months 43–51)* | Inventory < 15% · referral ≥ 25% of bookings | **CMO** | — | — | Residual media redeployed to resale and owner community |
| | **₹31.75 Cr** | | | | | |

**Three rules that apply across every phase:**

1. **A saving is never banked.** Underspend moves to the highest-leverage stage — which is the site visit, at ₹39.4 crore per conversion point.
2. **A missed gate cuts inventory before it cuts price**, and cuts inventory before it raises budget.
3. **The reporting template contains the reading rules before the first review** — compare within a layer, judge demand generation on cost per qualified lead, judge brand and site on holdouts. Without this, the naive cost-per-booking-by-channel reading defunds ₹10.25 crore of the highest-value spend.

## 11. The interview answer

**"How should a CMO explain a ₹30+ crore marketing model without making it sound like reckless spending?"**

> *"I'd start by saying that number isn't a marketing budget, and I wouldn't ask for it.*
>
> *If you take a ₹500 crore development across a four-year sales cycle, the total cost of acquiring 376 buyers comes to roughly ₹31 crore. But that figure bundles four different kinds of money. About ₹4 crore is channel-partner commission — I only pay that when a unit actually sells, so it's a cost of sale, not a budget. Around ₹4 crore is the experience centre, the show home interiors and the technology platform — that's capital expenditure, it's a building and a system, and it should sit in the project budget. What's genuinely left as marketing operating expense is about ₹24 crore, and of that, media is around ₹15 crore.*
>
> *Fifteen crore of media across forty-five months is roughly thirty-two lakh a month. That's an ordinary regional media budget. It only sounds alarming when you describe it wrong.*
>
> *But here's the part I'd actually want to be judged on. I don't believe those numbers yet, because the cost-per-lead assumptions come from other markets — nobody has measured what a qualified lead costs in this catchment. So I wouldn't ask for ₹24 crore. I'd ask for twenty-five lakh and eight weeks.*
>
> *That test tells me cost per lead and cost per qualified site visit to a usable confidence level. It won't tell me the booking rate — the volumes are too small, and I'd rather say that upfront than pretend otherwise. Booking rate takes two quarters of live trading to establish, and I'd carry it as a stated assumption until then.*
>
> *And the thing the test really buys is a decision rule. If cost per lead comes back twenty-five percent worse than assumed, I don't come back asking for more money — I hold the media and we sell a hundred and sixty units instead of two hundred, and we adjust the inventory release. If it's fifty percent worse, we stop and re-plan, because at that point the unit economics don't clear the allowable.*
>
> *The other thing I'd say to a CFO is: don't judge this on return on ad spend. Revenue divided by marketing spend gives you sixteen times, and it's a meaningless number, because most of that revenue pays for land and construction. On gross profit it's about three and a half times, and break-even is somewhere between seventy-six and ninety-four units out of three hundred and seventy-six. That's the number I'd defend — the scheme is four to five times past marketing break-even, and that holds whichever margin assumption you use.*
>
> *So: the ceiling is thirty-one crore across four years, released in five tranches against gates. The decision on the table today is twenty-five lakh. That's how I'd frame it, and honestly, that's how I'd want it framed if it were my money."*

**Why this works:** it concedes the weak point before it is attacked, converts a number that sounds reckless into a monthly figure that sounds ordinary, demonstrates statistical honesty by refusing to over-claim the test, and ends with a decision small enough that saying yes is easy.

# Phase 11 — Marketing Budget

## Built from thirteen line items upward, not from a percentage downward — and the two answers disagree by 28%

**Prepared:** August 2026
**Purpose:** Marketing investment model for the Phase 6 architecture, the Phase 8 journey and the Phase 9 channel plan
**Method note:** The brief is explicit — *do not simply say "spend 10% of revenue."* So this phase does the opposite: it prices each of the brief's twelve named line items from quantity × unit cost — plus one the brief's list has no name for — sums them, and only then compares the answer to Phase 9's allocation. **They do not match, and the gap is the most useful thing in this document.**

---

## 0. Method

### 0.1 Bottom-up, and why it matters here

Phases 7 and 9 produced a budget **top-down**: Phase 7 assumed a brand line and a sales-infrastructure line (both explicitly tagged ASSUMPTION), derived a lead-generation figure from CPL × leads, and Phase 9 distributed the total across channels.

**This phase builds the same budget from the other end** — every line is *quantity × unit cost*, sourced where a published rate exists and labelled where it does not.

> **The result: ₹32.99 crore bottom-up against ₹25.68 crore top-down. A 28% gap.**
>
> That gap is not a rounding error. **It sits almost entirely in the things that do not look like media** — launch, production, technology, NRI infrastructure, events, and agency fees. §2.2 itemises it line by line, and the itemisation reconciles to Phase 9's ₹25.68 crore exactly.

### 0.2 The four classifications, carried from Phase 7

| Tag | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **MARKET DATA** | Observed in this catchment during Phases 1–6 |
| **BENCHMARK** | A published Indian rate from an identifiable source. **Most come from agencies and vendors — interested parties** — and are treated as directional |
| **ASSUMPTION** | A value set here, with the reasoning shown. Not observed anywhere |
| **CALCULATED** | Derived arithmetically from the above |

**§11 is the full assumption register.** Every figure in this document appears there with its tag and its basis.

### 0.3 A taxonomy note the brief's list requires

The twelve items are a **cost taxonomy**. Phase 9's four layers are an **accountability taxonomy**. Both are valid; they are different cuts of the same money, and they overlap:

| Overlap | How it is resolved here |
| --- | --- |
| **Outdoor** sits inside **brand building** | Outdoor is broken out as its own line because it is 6.9% of the budget and has its own published rate card. Brand building excludes it |
| **Events** overlap **sales enablement** | On-site owner events sit in Events. The experience centre and show homes sit in Sales enablement |
| **NRI acquisition** is an audience, not a channel — Phase 9 said so | **It survives as a budget line, and should.** The *media* for S3 and S8 sits inside Performance marketing; this line holds only the **incremental infrastructure** that audience needs and no other does. §1.11 states the exclusion explicitly, because double-counting here is the obvious trap |
| **CRM** and **Technology** are adjacent | CRM holds licences, integration and telephony. Technology holds the website, martech, analytics and the walkthrough platform |
| **The referral programme has no slot in the brief's twelve** | Phase 9 funded referral rewards and administration at **₹0.70 Cr** and modelled 70 bookings from them. Referral is neither a channel line nor a cost type the twelve names cover, so it is carried here as an explicit **thirteenth line** rather than hidden inside Events. §2.2 shows what happens when it is dropped: the reconciliation stops closing |

> **The pattern is consistent and worth stating plainly.**
>
> **A top-down percentage systematically under-funds everything that is not media** — the production that fills the media, the technology that captures its output, the people who run it, and the infrastructure a specific audience needs. It over-funds the one thing that looks expensive and is partly recoverable.
>
> **That is the case against "spend 10% of revenue" in a single table.**

**And one finding that came out of building the table rather than reading it.**

A first version of this reconciliation **summed to ₹4.61 crore against a ₹6.61 crore gap** — ₹2.00 crore adrift, because two lines had been eyeballed against Phase 9 rather than mapped to it, and **the referral programme had been dropped from the bottom-up build entirely** while §10.1 continued to charge its ₹0.70 crore against 70 referral bookings.

> **Forcing the reconciliation to close is what found it.** A decomposition that does not sum to the difference it claims to explain is not a decomposition. **This is the argument for building the table both ways round** — and it is a reason to distrust any budget variance analysis whose rows do not add to the variance.

## 4. What a cut actually costs

The question is never "what percentage?" It is **"which cut, and what does it cost?"**

**CALCULATED** — holding the Phase 7 CPLs and conversion rates constant and scaling marketing-sourced bookings with performance media:

| Performance media | Marketing-sourced bookings | Bookings lost | **Revenue at risk** |
| --: | --: | --: | --: |
| ₹11.02 Cr — full | 201 | — | — |
| ₹9.84 Cr | 179 | 21.5 | ₹29.0 Cr |
| **₹8.00 Cr** | **146** | **55.1** | **₹74.2 Cr** |
| ₹6.50 Cr | 119 | 82.4 | ₹111.1 Cr |

> **Every ₹1 cut from performance media puts roughly ₹24.60 of revenue at risk.** The ratio is identical at all three cut depths, because bookings scale linearly with media in this model — which is also its main limitation.
>
> That ratio is the single most important number in this phase. It holds only while CPLs and conversion rates hold — but it is the right frame for the conversation, and it is not available from a percentage-of-revenue rule.

### 5.1 The recommendation: ₹31.75 crore

**RECOMMENDED trims ₹1.24 crore from things that do not touch performance media** — Telugu OTT from 24 to 18 months, cinema from 12 to 6, the hoarding sequence from 24 to 20 months, standing boards from 30 to 24, one diaspora event deferred, four owner films fewer, and the PR retainer from 30 to 24 months.

**It delivers the same 376 bookings for ₹1.24 crore less, because none of those cuts touches the funnel.** CONSTRAINED does touch it, and the arithmetic above is what that costs.

### 5.2 On being above the 4–5% industry norm

Phase 7 anchored against an industry-normal marketing spend of **4–5% of GDV.** The recommendation here is **6.27%.** That difference deserves an answer rather than a footnote.

**Four reasons this project sits above the norm, all traceable:**

1. **There is no existing brand to spend against.** Phase 4 found thin search visibility and sparse reviews. The 4–5% norm largely describes developers with established recall and a working referral base.
2. **There is no referral base in year one.** Phase 9 established that 18.5% of bookings are modelled as referral-sourced, and **referral requires prior buyers.** In year one there are none, so paid channels carry more than the steady-state average implies.
3. **The strategy deliberately over-invests in sales enablement.** ₹3.78 crore on the experience centre, show homes and collateral — because Phase 8 valued that stage at **₹39.4 crore per conversion point.** That is a considered allocation, not overspend.
4. **Two of eight segments are overseas.** Phase 7 found Community B alone consumes 65% of the lead-generation budget. Overseas acquisition is structurally more expensive than domestic, and this project has more of it than a typical corridor scheme.

> **Reasoned.** 6.27% is defensible *for a first project, by a developer without established consumer recall, selling 31% of its revenue in an unproven price band.* **It should fall toward the norm from year three onward, as referral compounds and brand recall accumulates** — and the model should be re-based annually rather than fixed at launch.

## 8. Community allocation

**Method, stated because it is a choice.** Costs are split into **directly attributable** — performance media by community from Phase 7, the agency fee pro-rata, and the NRI infrastructure which serves Community B alone — and **shared**, which is everything else and is allocated **by revenue share.**

**Reasoned.** Revenue share is the defensible basis for shared costs here because the brand, the site, the technology and the launch serve all four communities and cannot be traced to any one. **Allocating by unit count instead would load Community A with 45% of the shared cost for 25% of the revenue**, which would misrepresent it.

## 13. Carry-forward

**The recommendation: ₹31.75 crore — 6.27% of the ₹506.7 crore target, ₹8.44 lakh per booking.**

**How it splits.**

| Cut | Headline |
| --- | --- |
| **By line item** | Performance ₹11.02 Cr · Sales enablement ₹3.62 Cr · Channel partners ₹3.50 Cr · Brand ₹2.55 Cr · Launch ₹2.20 Cr · Outdoor ₹2.13 Cr · Content ₹1.99 Cr · NRI ₹1.05 Cr · Technology ₹0.91 Cr · Events ₹0.82 Cr · PR ₹0.75 Cr · Referral ₹0.70 Cr · CRM ₹0.51 Cr |
| **By structure** | Committed capital ₹7.24 Cr · pay-on-performance ₹3.34 Cr · variable media ₹15.70 Cr · semi-fixed ₹5.47 Cr |
| **By phase** | Pre-launch ₹6.89 Cr (22%) · launch ₹6.40 Cr (20%) · post-launch ₹18.46 Cr (58%) |
| **By community** | A ₹5.70 Cr · B ₹12.63 Cr · C ₹5.04 Cr · D ₹8.38 Cr |
| **Media vs not** | **Paid media 46% · everything else 54%** |

**What it delivers.** 78,880 leads → 12,474 qualified → 2,922 site visits → **376 bookings → ₹506.7 crore.** CAC ₹8.44 L. **ROI 3.5× on a 28% gross margin, break-even at 84 of 376 bookings, margin of safety 4.5×.**

**Five findings a percentage-of-revenue rule would have concealed.**

1. **Bottom-up exceeds top-down by 28%** — and the gap sits entirely in things that are not media: the launch spread invisibly, the agency fee nobody budgeted, the NRI infrastructure with no home, the technology build with no line, the production that fills the media.
2. **54% of the budget is not media.** A budget conversation held in media terms is discussing less than half the money.
3. **Only ₹21.17 crore is genuinely flexible.** ₹7.24 crore is committed before revenue exists; ₹3.34 crore costs nothing until a unit sells.
4. **Every ₹1 cut from performance media puts ₹24.60 of revenue at risk.** Cutting to Phase 9's ₹25.68 crore saves ₹6.50 crore and risks ₹74.2 crore. **That is the answer to "why not less?"**
5. **Peak burn is pre-launch, at 2.05× the steady state, when revenue is zero.** A flat monthly average conceals it entirely — and a percentage of zero revenue is zero.

**Open.** The seven risks in §12 — of which **risk 1 is the cheapest and most valuable correction available**: three vendor quotes and one agency proposal would move this budget more than any further analysis. And risk 7 remains what it has been since Phase 6: **not a budget question, and larger than all of them.**

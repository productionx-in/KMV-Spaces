E

What this does to the conversation

₹14.65 crore of media across 45 months is ₹32.6 lakh a month. That is an ordinary regional media budget for a four-community development.

It stops sounding like ₹31 crore the moment it is described correctly. Channel-partner commission costs nothing until a unit sells and belongs in the sales P&L. The experience centre is a building. The website is a system. Neither is a campaign.

The convergence — two corrections, arrived at independently, landing on the same number

Strip cost of sale and capex out of the marketing line and you get ₹23.80 crore = ₹6.33 lakh per unit. The ceiling the research set for itself was ₹6.30 lakh. A 0.5% difference.

Put the other way: the overshoot against the allowable is ₹8.06 crore. The misclassified money is ₹7.95 crore. They are the same ₹8 crore.

That is the strongest evidence in this audit that the reclassification is right and the label is wrong.

§1.3 · A methodological problem in how the number was built

What was claimed
  • A bottom-up budget — quantity × unit cost, thirteen lines.
  • Reported as exceeding the top-down allocation by 28%.
What is actually true
  • ₹9.84 crore of media was imported directly from the Phase 7 funnel projection, which runs backwards from a booking target using assumed CPLs and conversion rates.
  • ₹11.02 crore — 33% of the total — is a top-down model wearing a bottom-up label.
  • Because the media figure is identical on both sides, it cancels in the reconciliation. The +28% gap is real for the other two-thirds and overstates the independence of the two methods.

§3

What we know, assume, need to test, and recommend

Kept rigidly apart, because conflating them is how a model becomes a claim.

1 · What we know
  • Corridor asking prices: ₹4,062/sq ft locality, ₹5,100 at a delivered comparable.
  • Amaravati is AP's sole capital by statute — works funded and active.
  • AIIMS Mangalagiri is operational, ~3 km away.
  • Land pooling is real and sized — 33,000+ acres phase 1.
  • Published Indian rate bands for outdoor, CRM seats, PR retainers, CP commission.
2 · What we assume — all unverified
  • CPL by community — A ₹350 · B ₹1,600 · C ₹900 · D ₹1,900. Not catchment-specific.
  • Lead→qualified 15.8% · qualified→visit 23.4% · visit→booking 6.9%.
  • Source mix 53 / 28 / 19.
  • Gross margin 25 / 28 / 31%.
  • Price ladder ₹5,150–7,200/sq ft — 50% above locality asking.
  • Marketing team structure and cost — not modelled at all.
3 · What we need to test
  • Catchment CPL and CPQL — underwrites ₹14.65 Cr of media.
  • Cost per qualified site visit — the only conversion metric a short test can measure honestly.
  • Site-visit-to-booking — highest leverage, and untestable quickly.
  • Whether the price ladder clears.
  • What the developer's existing villa product sold for — free, and it settles more than any test.
What is deliberately not assumed, because it would be invention

The developer's actual marketing budget, current media spend, internal headcount, agency roster, historical CPL, sales velocity and inventory position.

None is publicly available. None is estimated here, and a model that guessed at them would be less useful than one that names the gap.

4 · What we recommend

Do not ask for ₹31.75 crore. Do not ask for ₹23.80 crore. Ask for ₹25 lakh, and a decision rule.

The full-lifecycle figure belongs in the appendix as a ceiling under governance, not in the ask.

The metric that must not be used

ROMI = (gross profit − marketing investment) ÷ marketing investment.

Revenue ÷ spend would read 16.0×. It is a meaningless number — most of that revenue pays for construction, land and finance. Any deck showing 16× ROAS on a property development should be disbelieved, including this one.

Break-even at 76–94 units of 376 — the scheme is 4.0–4.9× past marketing break-even, and that holds across every margin assumption.

§8

CFO view

"If I were the CFO of KMV, what would make me reject this marketing budget?" Ten objections, answered.

1

“You've labelled ₹31.75 crore a marketing budget. ₹4.04 crore is commission I pay only when a unit sells, and ₹3.91 crore is a building. Why is that in your opex ask?”

CMO

Accepted, and corrected before you asked. The marketing ask is ₹23.80 crore of opex, of which ₹14.65 crore is media. Commission moves to the sales P&L; the experience centre and platforms move to project capex.

2

“A third of your 'bottom-up' budget is a top-down projection. The media line came from a funnel model, not a quote.”

CMO

Correct, and it is the model's weakest point. ₹9.84 crore was derived, not quoted. That is precisely why the first ask is ₹25 lakh to measure it rather than ₹9.84 crore to spend it.

3

“Your CPLs are from other markets. What if Guntur is twice Bangalore?”

CMO

Then we do not fund the difference. At +50% CPL the model fails the allowable and we re-plan. At +25% we hold media and take 161 bookings instead of 201. The response to a worse CPL is a volume decision, not a budget increase.

4

“6.27% of GDV, but GDV arrives over four years and your spend is front-loaded. Show me cash, not percentages.”

CMO

Agreed — the percentage is a reference, not a target. Peak burn is pre-launch at ₹1.15 crore a month against zero revenue. That is a funding-plan item, not a marketing-report item.

5

“₹8.44 lakh per booking. Against gross profit, not revenue — what is that?”

CMO

22.4% of gross profit at 28% margin; 25.1% at 25%. On marketing opex alone it is 16.8%. Those are the numbers I would be held to.

6

“You've assumed 28%. We haven't tendered. At 20% your CAC is a third of gross profit.”

CMO

The margin is a placeholder and I will not defend it. I would rather present break-even: 76–94 units of 376. That statement holds at every margin in the range, which is why it is the one I lead with.

7

“Community B takes 40% of the budget for 22% of the units. Justify it or cut it.”

CMO

I would not defend it — I would gate it. B's constraint is that its buyer is 12,000 km away and cannot visit. If qualified-to-visit does not reach 25% within two quarters, we reduce B's inventory release rather than raise its budget. The threshold is agreed before the money moves.

8

“₹6.89 crore before the first booking. Who funds that, and what if the price band doesn't clear?”

CMO

₹3.55 crore of it is the experience centre, technology and CRM — capex that must exist for any funnel to work. On the price band: 31% of revenue sits in a range we have not proven is occupied. I would spend ₹3.5 lakh proving it before committing the ₹6.89 crore.

9

“Where is your team cost? I see agency fees and no people.”

CMO

A genuine omission. The model carries a 12% agency fee and a brand retainer but no internal marketing headcount — because inventing a team structure would be worse than admitting the gap. It must be added before any budget is signed, and it will raise the total.

10

“What's the smallest cheque that tells us whether this works?”

CMO

₹25 lakh. Eight weeks. It measures CPL, CPQL and cost per qualified site visit to usable precision, and it tests the ungated price sheet. It cannot measure booking rate, and I will not claim it can.

Rule 1 · A saving is never banked
  • Underspend moves to the highest-leverage stage — the site visit, at ₹39.4 crore per conversion point.

  • A missed gate cuts inventory before it cuts price, and cuts inventory before it raises budget.

  • Compare within a layer; judge demand generation on cost per qualified lead; judge brand and site on holdouts.
  • Without this, the naive cost-per-booking-by-channel reading defunds ₹10.25 crore of the highest-value spend.
§11

The interview answer

"How should a CMO explain a ₹30+ crore marketing model without making it sound like reckless spending?"

Say this

I'd start by saying that number isn't a marketing budget, and I wouldn't ask for it.

If you take a ₹500 crore development across a four-year sales cycle, the total cost of acquiring 376 buyers comes to roughly ₹31 crore. But that figure bundles four different kinds of money. About ₹4 crore is channel-partner commission — I only pay that when a unit actually sells, so it's a cost of sale, not a budget. Around ₹4 crore is the experience centre, the show home interiors and the technology platform — that's capital expenditure, it's a building and a system, and it should sit in the project budget. What's genuinely left as marketing operating expense is about ₹24 crore, and of that, media is around ₹15 crore.

Fifteen crore of media across forty-five months is roughly thirty-two lakh a month. That's an ordinary regional media budget. It only sounds alarming when you describe it wrong.

But here's the part I'd actually want to be judged on. I don't believe those numbers yet, because the cost-per-lead assumptions come from other markets — nobody has measured what a qualified lead costs in this catchment. So I wouldn't ask for ₹24 crore. I'd ask for twenty-five lakh and eight weeks.

That test tells me cost per lead and cost per qualified site visit to a usable confidence level. It won't tell me the booking rate — the volumes are too small, and I'd rather say that upfront than pretend otherwise. Booking rate takes two quarters of live trading to establish, and I'd carry it as a stated assumption until then.

And the thing the test really buys is a decision rule. If cost per lead comes back twenty-five percent worse than assumed, I don't come back asking for more money — I hold the media and we sell a hundred and sixty units instead of two hundred, and we adjust the inventory release. If it's fifty percent worse, we stop and re-plan, because at that point the unit economics don't clear the allowable.

The other thing I'd say to a CFO is: don't judge this on return on ad spend. Revenue divided by marketing spend gives you sixteen times, and it's a meaningless number, because most of that revenue pays for land and construction. On gross profit it's about three and a half times, and break-even is somewhere between seventy-six and ninety-four units out of three hundred and seventy-six. That's the number I'd defend — the scheme is four to five times past marketing break-even, and that holds whichever margin assumption you use.

So: the ceiling is thirty-one crore across four years, released in five tranches against gates. The decision on the table today is twenty-five lakh. That's how I'd frame it, and honestly, that's how I'd want it framed if it were my money.

Why it works · 1
  • It concedes the weak point before it is attacked. The CPL assumption is named as unverified in the third paragraph, not defended in the tenth.
Why it works · 2
  • It converts a reckless-sounding number into an ordinary one — ₹32 lakh a month — without changing a single figure.
Why it works · 3
  • It demonstrates statistical honesty by refusing to over-claim the test, which is rarer and more persuasive than confidence.
Why it works · 4
  • It ends on a decision small enough that saying yes is easy — and it never claims to have spent ₹31 crore, only to have thought about how it should be released.
